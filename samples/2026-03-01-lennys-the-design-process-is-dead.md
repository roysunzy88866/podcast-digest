今天的嘉宾是 [[Jenny Wen|Jenny Wen]],她曾是 [[Claude|Claude]] 的设计负责人,现在领导 [[Claude Cowork|Claude Cowork]] 的设计。在加入 [[Anthropic|Anthropic]] 之前,她是 [[Figma|Figma]] 的设计总监,领导了 FigJam 和 Slides 背后的设计团队,也曾在 Dropbox、Square 和 Shopify 担任设计师。本集的核心问题是:当 AI 让工程师能独自构建大量功能时,设计师的角色和工作流程会发生怎样的剧变?接下来我们将围绕六个话题展开:旧流程为何失效、新型设计工作长什么样、在 AI 实验室做设计是什么体验、人的价值还剩什么、管理者该往哪走,以及未来要招什么样的设计师。

## 被迫改变的「设计流程」

一切的起点是一个共识:设计师过去被教导的那套[[设计流程|设计流程]]——先做研究、发散、收敛、再发散、再收敛——已经失效了 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="05:20" data-who="Jenny Wen" data-en="I think we've actually seen engineering change a lot more in the past little while than design actually has, but I think the result of engineering changing a bunch is that design is sort of forced to change." aria-label="回原文"></button>。Jenny 几个月前在柏林的一场会议上做了一场名为《不要相信设计流程》的演讲,她当时指出这套被设计师奉为福音的流程正在消亡。而仅仅过去三到四个月,随着 Opus 46 的发布以及工程师在假期大量使用 [[Claude Code|Claude Code]],她发现连自己的那场演讲都已经显得过时了 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="06:09" data-who="Jenny Wen" data-en="And I think that's the big thing that's changing. But I think even in the past three to four months since I did that talk, that talk actually starts to feel pretty ... It kind of feels outdated to me, which is a little embarrassing, but especially with the big shift of Opus 46 and a bunch of folks just really discovering and using Claude Code over the holiday break, I think we're seeing this force to change our process happen even more." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

这不是设计行业自己主动求变,而是被工程效率倒逼的 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="05:07" data-who="Jenny Wen" data-en="I think it's still also got a long way to go in terms of the way it's changing. I think we've actually seen engineering change a lot more in the past little while than design actually has, but I think the result of engineering changing a bunch is that design is sort of forced to change." aria-label="回原文"></button>。现在工程师可以同时启动多个 Claude 帮他们写代码,一天之内交付大量功能。在这种狂奔的速度下,设计师如果想用旧流程去卡进度,只会成为瓶颈;正确做法是放手让工程师去做。

但放手不等于设计师无事可做,工作只是发生了分化。Jenny 现在看到设计工作主要分成了两类。第一类是紧贴工程执行,也就是工程师用各种 AI 工具快速把想法做出来,设计师作为顾问加入,帮忙打磨和连接功能 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="06:48" data-who="Jenny Wen" data-en="So there's the first one, which is really just supporting the implementation and execution. So this is the one where engineers are using their seven Claudes to create all these features, and anybody can put an idea out there, and you can just talk about an idea and somebody, usually actually an engineer because they're still better at implementing this stuff than we are, they will just make a scrappy version of it and you can try it out." aria-label="回原文"></button>。在非确定性的 AI 模型面前,理论上画再多的高保真设计图也没用,因为模型实际表现出来的状态是无法穷举和预演的。只有真正用底层模型跑起来、用真实用户的用例去测,才能发现问题 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="12:00" data-who="Jenny Wen" data-en="I think you sort of have to choose and use your discretion as to when to actually ship something, but I think the ability to execute, try something out and try it with real data, and a real user's kind of mindset in the product, I think that does lead to a better product, especially as we're all working with these new developing AI models that are non-deterministic, you can't mock up all the states, and you can't theorize and you can't even make a clickable prototype with it." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

第二类则是制定短期愿景与方向 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="07:16" data-who="Jenny Wen" data-en="And you as a designer actually do not have the time to make these beautiful mocks anymore, or to lead in this way. And then I think there's the second kind of work that feels also really important, which is creating the sort of vision or direction for things." aria-label="回原文"></button>。这与过去也截然不同:过去设计师动辄制定两年、五年甚至十年的长远愿景;但在技术按月迭代、没人知道两年后世界会怎样的当下,愿景的保质期被压缩到了三到六个月 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="07:49" data-who="Jenny Wen" data-en="But the way that the technology is changing now, we don't know what's going to happen in two years. There's too much changing, and it usually becomes a vision that's three to six months out, and isn't necessarily something that is creating this beautiful deck that's beautifully story told." aria-label="回原文"></button>。这种愿景也不再是那些精美排版的幻灯片,而往往就是一个能指出方向的原型。

## 时间去哪了:画图少了,写码多了

既然旧流程死了,新流程长什么样?这直接体现在设计师的时间分配上。Jenny 回顾几年前自己的状态:当时大概 60% 到 70% 的时间都花在制作高保真设计图和原型上,20% 的时间和工程师沟通协作,剩下 10% 处理各类协调会议 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="17:58" data-who="Jenny Wen" data-en="Yeah. I think as a designer a few years ago, I would say maybe 60 to 70% of it was mocking and prototyping stuff up, and then spending some of the last 20 or so doing the sort of jamming with engineers, consulting with them, and the last 10% maybe doing coordination meetings, et cetera." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

而现在,她做高保真设计图的时间骤降到了 30% 到 40%。取而代之的是,有 30% 到 40% 的时间她都在和工程师直接结对编程、做探讨 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="18:29" data-who="Jenny Wen" data-en="But now I feel like the mocking up part of it is 30 to 40%. And then there's that other 30 to 40% there that is now jamming and pairing directly with engineers. And then there's a slice, I don't know how much I have left, but there's a slice of it that is now implementation as well." aria-label="回原文"></button>。更有意思的是,还多出了一块「写代码」的时间——她现在会直接上手做最后一英里的视觉打磨和功能实现。具体来说,工程师和 AI 跑出第一版后,她会自己打开 IDE 改前端细节 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="19:27" data-who="Jenny Wen" data-en="And then there's Claude Code, of course. I use it mostly with VS Code and the IDE because I'm usually tweaking front-end stuff, and it helps to just be able to see the code and then talk to Claude as well." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

在 AI 技术栈上,她基本上是「全 Claude」配置:日常对话用 Claude Chat,但越来越多转移到处理长线任务的 Cowork;前端微调则用 Claude Code 搭配 VS Code <button class="pd-ts" data-t="19:04" data-who="Jenny Wen" data-en="Well, we work at Anthropic, so we're going deep on the Claude stack. I'm using, obviously, chats, Claude Chat, but increasingly more and more Claude Cowork. I've basically shifted all of my chat use cases over to Cowork, because I've been finding that it sort of is better at these longer running tasks." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

> 【背景】Opus 46 指 Anthropic 发布的 Claude Opus 4.5 模型(转写稿误作 46);Claude Cowork(转写稿前文曾误作 Cowriter)是 Anthropic 推出的智能体产品;VS Code 是一款代码编辑器。

虽然代码写得越来越多,但 Jenny 认为现在还远远不是「代码取代设计工具」的时候。她仍在使用 Figma,因为用它来平行探索 8 到 10 种不同的排版和视觉方案依然是最快的,而直接用写代码工具去探索视觉方案过于线性,容易死磕在一个方向上 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="21:02" data-who="Jenny Wen" data-en="I think the best design happens when you're able to just throw a bunch of ideas at the wall, and curate and push yourself to come up with a bunch of these different directions." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

## 在 AI 风暴中心做设计

了解到工作方式的变化后,我们自然会好奇:在 Anthropic 这样处于 AI 风暴中心的实验室做设计,日常到底是怎样的?

Jenny 描述了一个非常特别的日常状态:她每天会花相当一部分时间仅仅是去跟进公司内部正在发生的事情 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="13:11" data-who="Jenny Wen" data-en="Just give us a day in the life of working at Anthropic, at the center of the storm. A good amount of time at Anthropic is actually just catching up on what's happening at the company." aria-label="回原文"></button>。一方面,研究和原型开发的节奏极快,内部有无数带有代号的项目在同时推进;她必须主动去搜寻情报,看看前方有哪些新东西即将落地。另一方面,内部 Slack 里有大量关于行业走向的洞见和哲学辩论,她形容为一个「金矿」,极其吸引人去跟进 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="15:15" data-who="Jenny Wen" data-en="It feels like I have this front row seat to so much that's happening in the industry. And so a lot of it is like, yeah, our Slack is a goldmine. I'm just excited to read through the things that people are working on, they're saying." aria-label="回原文"></button>。而在剩下的时间里,除了设定三个月左右的短期愿景,她做得最多的是和工程师结对工作,以及自己写代码做打磨 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="16:21" data-who="Jenny Wen" data-en="Because I think it still really helps to visualize that and show that to the team, and point everyone in the same direction. And then I also spend a bunch of my day just jamming on stuff with engineers." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

这种高频的发布和迭代,会带来一个很多人担心的问题:质量怎么保证?设计师会不会失去对产品的把控?Jenny 的解法是区分产品所处的阶段。如果是早期预览版,就要明确告诉用户它的局限性 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="25:08" data-who="Jenny Wen" data-en="But I think with this, I think about where the features or products are, where they are in the cycle of adoption versus early preview. So for example, we sometimes will launch things and we will say, &quot;Hey, this is a research preview." aria-label="回原文"></button>。把不完美的东西推向市场的核心,在于建立一种信任机制——你必须承诺会听取反馈并快速修复。如果发了早期版本就不管了,会严重损害品牌;但如果做到快速响应、持续交付改进,反而能在用户中建立极高的信任 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="26:16" data-who="Jenny Wen" data-en="You have to show that to the world, you have to respond to people's feedback, and you have to show that you are continuously shipping and improving it. Because I think the way that you really lose trust around quality and releasing something early, is if you release it early and then nothing ever happens." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

## 人的价值:从「执行者」到「拍板人」

工具变了、速度变了,人脑的价值还剩什么?这正是下一个话题。主持人 [[Lenny|Lenny]] 提到,如今连世界上最优秀的工程师都已经信任到不再看 AI 写的代码,而 AI 甚至开始帮人决定要构建什么产品。在这样的趋势下,Jenny 也承认:我们可能过于死守「设计师永远拥有最好的品味」这种幻觉了 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="28:33" data-who="Jenny Wen" data-en="I think it will get better at taste and judgment and design. Yeah, I think we might be holding onto that a little bit too much and saying, &quot;Oh yeah, a designer or somebody will always know the best thing to ship or the best version of this.&quot;" aria-label="回原文"></button>。

但她认为,人依然有一个不可替代的核心价值:承担责任 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="30:51" data-who="Jenny Wen" data-en="I think someone will still have to decide, oh, we want to build this kind of product. Or given what the AI is presenting us, someone still needs to be accountable for the decision." aria-label="回原文"></button>。就像即使 Claude 能写所有的代码,最终依然是工程师要为这堆代码能不能跑、是否符合产品逻辑负责。在品味和判断之外,构建软件最难的部分往往不是构建本身,而是在多人的分歧中拍板决定到底要做什么、不做什么 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="29:10" data-who="Jenny Wen" data-en="And when I think about people saying, &quot;Oh, AI is just going to build this software for us,&quot; a lot of the hard parts of building software are actually not building it." aria-label="回原文"></button>。这种承担决策压力的判断层,在可预见的未来依然落在人类身上。另外谈及与 AI 的交互界面时,Jenny 认为聊天窗口和终端并不是临时形态。因为交谈本身就是一种能跨越智力高低、完美适配模型能力增长的交互方式 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="32:26" data-who="Jenny Wen" data-en="There will likely be a combination of both, both UIs and interfaces that you are interacting with, clicking with, and that feel more tactile., We are already seeing this and playing with this within Claude, the chatbot." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

> 【背景】转写稿中提及的 OpenClaw、Moltbot 疑为 OpenAI 的 ChatGPT、Anthropic 的 Claude 等聊天机器人的转写误读,此处按原意理解。

## 管理者退回 IC(独立贡献者)

既然设计行业的工作方式发生了巨变,管理者该怎么办?Jenny 用自己的经历给出了答案:退回一线。她在 Anthropic 最初是以 [[IC|IC]] 身份加入的,后来因为组织需要短暂带过一段时间团队(在 Figma 时她曾管理过 12 到 15 名设计师),但现在又回到了全职 IC <button class="pd-ts" data-t="35:51" data-who="Jenny Wen" data-en="Yeah. So this past year at Anthropic, I joined as an IC at first, and then I managed a team for a few months in an org structure that sort of needed it, and now I'm actually back to doing full-time IC work." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

促使她做这个决定的原因之一,是对中层管理未来的焦虑——她想弄清楚现在的这种工作方式到底能不能持久 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="36:26" data-who="Jenny Wen" data-en="And I joined Anthropic as an IC because I was just really excited about the kind of work that there was to be done as an IC here, but also because I was feeling like I sort of want to be closer to the work, and I think this feels like a really important time to do it, before I ascend the corporate ranks." aria-label="回原文"></button>。但更重要的是,这一年的 IC 经历让她掌握了大量如果只做管理就根本没时间学的硬技能 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="37:06" data-who="Jenny Wen" data-en="So I love both sides of the coin pretty equally. But I think actually what being an IC across this past year has taught me, is that it actually just gave me a lot of skills that I don't think I would've gained if I was just managing throughout this year." aria-label="回原文"></button>。她认为,当下的管理者如果不亲自下场用新工具、不理解设计流程的剧变,就无法对团队产生真正的同理心 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="37:39" data-who="Jenny Wen" data-en="And I think at any point if I'm managing a team again, I think it will give me the empathy and understanding of how the design process has changed. And I think that's actually a really important thing right now because the teams are working so differently." aria-label="回原文"></button>。在她看来,纯粹的人员管理正在消退,未来的管理者必须是能给团队指明方向并能真刀真枪干活的人 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="39:03" data-who="Jenny Wen" data-en="But the way I think about what a helpful manager is these days, is somebody who is not just, I think pure people management like, oh, just somebody to set you up, help you in your career, have one-on-ones, make sure you're feeling good at work." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

在具体的团队管理上,她有两个很特别的理念。第一是看似低杠杆的任务其实很高杠杆 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="55:31" data-who="Jenny Wen" data-en="These are things anybody else can do, and everything else, it's low leverage and you shouldn't do that anymore.&quot; And a lot of the low leverage things were just things that are really nitpicky in the weeds, or just literally, yes, probably somebody else could do those tasks." aria-label="回原文"></button>。比如高级领导者亲自去测产品、复现 Bug 甚至提交代码补丁(她举例看到 Mike Krieger 亲自提交代码 PR),这种卷起袖子干脏活的举动会极大地带动团队士气。

> 【背景】PR(Pull Request)是工程师提交代码修改供团队审查的机制;Mike Krieger 是 Anthropic 的首席产品官,Instagram 联合创始人。

第二是鼓励团队互相吐槽(roast)。Jenny 认为团队内部能互相开玩笑、甚至模仿、取笑领导的口头禅,是心理安全感极高的标志 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="58:58" data-who="Jenny Wen" data-en="But I think it can be a really good sign when the people on your team feel comfortable just poking fun at each other a little bit. And I think that also can be a good sign when folks also feel the same way about you as a leader, where it's like there's just an element of they don't fear you as much, and they feel like there's a sense of safety where if they say something, they're not going to get fired." aria-label="回原文"></button>。这种舒适感与高标准并不冲突——就像做一个严厉的父母,团队知道你不会随意解雇他们,但也清楚你要求做到最好 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="60:44" data-who="Jenny Wen" data-en="And I think these two things can feel like they're at tension, but I think they work really well together, because it's like once you have that psychological safety, you have people trusting each other and you, applying the high standards actually I think becomes potentially easier because you can do it without fear, I think." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

## 招募未来的设计师:三种原型

工作模式和团队理念更新了,招聘标准自然也要跟着变。除了适应力和韧性,Jenny 现在特别看重三种人才原型 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="46:52" data-who="Jenny Wen" data-en="Well, I do think working specifically in the kind of environment that I do, there's just a sense of resilience and roll with it, kind of thing, that I think is really important because so much is changing around us and you have to be really willing to adapt, to try out new methods, to try out new tools and learn stuff, as opposed to just be stuck in the old processes and the old ways." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

第一种是「强通才」。不是什么都会一点的人,而是技能呈块状——在几个不同的核心领域都能达到前 80% 水平的多面手 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="47:09" data-who="Jenny Wen" data-en="So the first one I would call is strong generalists. And I think this person is really interesting right now because I think most companies are just hiring senior talent, folks that have done things before, are super experienced, but given how much the roles are changing and what we're expected to do is changing, I think having somebody who almost has a blank slate, and is just a really quick learner and is really eager to learn new tactics and stuff like that, and doesn't have all these baked in processes and rituals in their mind, that's super valuable." aria-label="回原文"></button>。对于想入行的年轻人,她的建议很直接:别只学理论,去真正造东西、玩技术。

在方法论上,Jenny 分享了一个她极为推崇的「[[不可读性框架|不可读性框架]]」(legibility framework)<button class="pd-ts" data-t="62:00" data-who="Jenny Wen" data-en="And I hear you're a big fan of something called the legibility framework. And I was like, &quot;Cool, I think there's something here, but I just don't understand it yet.&quot;" aria-label="回原文"></button>。

> 【背景】Legible/Illigible 在此框架中意指一个想法或人是否容易被大众主流理解和归类;SPC 是一家知名创业孵化与投资机构。

## 本集带走

1. **设计旧流程已死,执行力倒逼新分工**:传统的发散-收敛流程在极速的工程交付面前失效了。现在设计师的核心要么是贴身支持执行(写代码打磨、做顾问),要么是制定极短期的产品方向。
2. **AI 没有剥夺人的判断责任**:即便 AI 能写出所有代码、甚至提出方案,但面对多方分歧时,最终拍板决定「到底做不做、怎么做」并为此担责的,依然是人。
3. **管理者必须重新下场**:纯粹的人员管理正在失去价值。只有退回一线、亲自体验工具剧变的管理者,才能真正理解并引领当下极度变化的团队。
