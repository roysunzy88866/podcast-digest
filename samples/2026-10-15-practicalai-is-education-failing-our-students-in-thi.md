# AI时代，学校还在教孩子“不用AI”

Practical AI 的两位主播——Prediction Guard 的 CEO [[Daniel Whitenack|Daniel Whitenack]] 和 AI 研究工程师 [[Chris Benson|Chris Benson]]——这期没有请嘉宾，而是对谈了一个他们都切身体会的话题：教育系统跟不上 AI 的变化。

Daniel 一个月后即将迎来第一个孩子；Chris 有一个 14 岁上高中的女儿，还在几所大学做顾问，正写一本关于智能体时代的书。

两人一个是即将入场的家长，一个是正在场内的家长。

## AI 和以往的技术冲击有什么不同？

Daniel 拿社交媒体做对比：社交软件改变了生活，但除了做数字营销的人，大多数机械工程师的日常工作并没有因为它改变。

AI 不同——它来得更快，而且直接改变每个人干活和学习的方式 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="08:09" data-who="Daniel Whitenack" data-en="But AI has come on both more rapidly than social media, and it actually is affecting the way we all do our jobs, the way we all do our work, and the way that we learn, right, in our educational system. So in a sense, it's both more impactful and happening faster than other" aria-label="回原文"></button>。

学校的最初反应是禁令：不许用 AI，自己完成作业。Chris 认为，这很大程度上是因为学校根本不知道怎么把 AI 融进教学。

现在风向开始松动，因为所有人都明白：**这东西不会消失，孩子们长大后整个职业生涯都要和这些工具打交道** <button class="pd-ts" data-t="08:57" data-who="Chris Benson" data-en="things It is. We've And I think we hear a lot about educational institutions at all levels struggling with that. And the initial guidance was you're not allowed to use AI, and you gotta do the work yourself. That was largely, I think, because there was no concept of how to integrate AI, the reality of that, into that work stream that students are producing for their education. I think that's starting to change a little bit, at least in some places, because at this point it's very clear this is not going away, so I think some forward leaning people are starting to try to identify how do we integrate for the rest of these kids' lives as they grow up and they're in the workplace all the way?" aria-label="回原文"></button>。

## 该教“用AI”，还是该教“AI之外的东西”？

Daniel 提出一个区分：一边是教人怎么把 AI 用进工作；另一边是那些人之所以为人需要学会的基本功——比如数学直觉。

一个孩子完全可以在学校正式教数学之前，就从[[聊天机器人|聊天机器人]]那里得到答案。

如果只拿答案、不练思维，很多人会觉得这是问题。

但反过来也有热观点：拼写还有必要学吗？文字处理软件早就有拼写检查，AI 甚至能直接替你写。也许有些“基本功”就是会过时。

Chris 的答案是：不管具体技能怎么变，[[批判性思考|批判性思维]]和[[创造力|创造力]]永远是底座。

你得有足够的知识背景，才能和 AI 协作解决难问题——比如你得懂数学，才能跟上 AI 帮你做的更难的数学 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="13:09" data-who="Chris Benson" data-en="I think it does. I agree with everything you said. I think to draw that out a little bit more, there are some skills such as the ability to do critical thinking across a number of domains, the ability to be creative across a number of domains, and then to have the content... Whatever discrete discrete skills that you might be taught in school, as you pointed out, and those may change, and some things that we think of as foundational may be changing. But having enough context in your learning around your ability to do critical thinking and to be creative together so that you can collaborate with the technologies going forward and arrive." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

## Daniel 的育儿观：没有挣扎，就没有成长

Daniel 说得更狠一点：「**我认为人离开不适和[[挣扎|挣扎]]，就无法在品格和成长上进步**。」

无论是婚姻、工作还是价值观，人生的挣扎正是这些东西被锻造的方式 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="14:19" data-who="Daniel Whitenack" data-en="Yeah. Maybe another way to put it is, I I... Well, again, people have different opinions probably, but at least from my point of view or worldview or however you would frame it, I don't think you really advance in your character and development as a human without discomfort and struggle. Right? I agree with And so and so a part of whether that's, you know, advancing in your marriage, advancing in your ability as a worker, in your, you know, character as a as a human being and the values that that you wanna hold to." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

所以哪怕数学、拼写不再是一辈子天天用的技能，如果学习它们的过程锻炼了正确类型的思考、制造了必要的困难，那它就仍然有价值。

Chris 用自己举例：他天天用 AI 写代码、做研究，产出比一年前高得多。

但挣扎没有消失，只是换了位置——写 Rust 代码写得多好变得没那么重要了，他把精力挪到了驱动这一切的创造性过程上 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="18:07" data-who="Chris Benson" data-en="Are we already offloading some of those skills to AI, and how are we collaborating to solve really tough problems? To your point about the struggle, I know that in my own life, as I'm working with AI every day, I know both you and I are collaborating with AI models all the time, and that has changed the workflows on how I engage a lot of problems, and in a lot of ways it supercharged what I can do, the productivity. Know I've mentioned that before in terms of what I can generate, so my output is way above what was possible a year ago. And so I'm starting to really look at how am I doing that, but I am noticing that that struggle still exists. It's just being placed in different places." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

## 老师的手是被绑住的

Chris 曾在节目里建议老师该怎么教，结果好几位老师私下联系他：你塞给我们一件我们没有权限做的事。

课程是上面定的，老师只能照办 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="20:36" data-who="Chris Benson" data-en="Control of, yeah. We don't have control of. That comes down from on high, and we're within those boundaries And they implement. Yeah. That's correct. So for all of" aria-label="回原文"></button>。

所以他提醒家长（尤其是即将开家长会的 Daniel）：

你从自己很现代的工作世界走进孩子的教室，会觉得很陈旧、很憋闷，但别忘了**老师戴着学术手铐**。

Chris 的激进建议是：开学第一天就把全年的课程（不含考题）公开。

反正孩子会用 AI 去查，与其叫作弊，不如让他们真正学进去，再想办法考察他们理解得深不深。

老师则转型为“定制化学习的引导者”——这样人类教师的价值反而会更高，而不是被 AI 取代 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="23:06" data-who="Chris Benson" data-en="I've kind of given their profiles about me, and they're able to present information that's really optimal for me. So even at 56, my current age, I am learning rapidly, certainly as fast as I have done over the last few decades or faster, because I have these tools to do that. What I'm suggesting is we need to push that down into more of a systematic approach in the school system and let these tools help kids at an individual level learn. I'll give you one example before I shut up. I'm gonna be radical here, but I did suggest this to a set of teachers recently, and I think their face just dropped, and that is I think school systems, most of the curriculum is well set long before, and the teachers know what they're teaching far before they actually get to a given day when they're teaching it." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

Daniel 补充了听到的一些做法：

弱化背事实，比如让学生让 AI 生成一篇文章，然后要求他们批判性地分析哪里不准确、哪里可以改进——把 AI 的输出当作需要检验的东西，而不是标准答案 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="26:08" data-who="Daniel Whitenack" data-en="I know some of the things that I've heard is a bit of reprioritization and tie in of AI. So less of focus on memorization and repeating facts. Right? And maybe a way for you to look at the output of AI and find what is maybe not not accurate. Right?" aria-label="回原文"></button>。

## 数据说话：AI 伴学是把双刃剑

Daniel 引用了两项研究：

一项 2025 年在土耳其做的、近千名高中数学生的现场实验发现，用通用聊天机器人辅助练习，虽然当时有帮助，但之后不借助 AI 的考试成绩反而比对照组低 17%；

另一项哈佛物理课、约 200 名学生的研究发现，精心设计的 [[AI 导师|AI 导师]]在两节课里带来的即时学习收获，超过了活跃学习课堂 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="30:10" data-who="Daniel Whitenack" data-en="So Chris, just to highlight a couple things, which you you had sent me prior to this conversation, which I think are interesting, just a a couple of data points here. There was a 2025, study field experiment with nearly a thousand high school mathematics students in Turkey, that found that a general chatbot improved assisted practice but reduced subsequent unassisted exam performance by 17% relative to a control. The... Study, that was from Harvard undergraduate physics program, this was with around 200 students, found stronger immediate learning gains with a carefully structured AI tutor across two lessons than with its active learning classroom comparison. I know actually even when I was a teaching assistant at Purdue, this sort of active learning, stuff was very much the thing that everyone was experimenting with." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

结论不是“AI 好或坏”，而是**怎么用差别巨大**。

他所在的大学城里的 [[Purdue|Purdue]] 已经在所有本科专业里加了 AI 能力必修要求，目标是培养批判性思维、负责任的 AI 使用和就业技能。

Daniel 说，在他的公司，新员工如果不愿意在日常工作中主动用 AI，会是个问题——但很多毕业生因为在大学被教授围追堵截地防作弊，反而对工作中的 AI 有负面联想 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="31:29" data-who="Daniel Whitenack" data-en="To your point, Chris, the system is doing certain things. So Purdue... And I know this... They're not the only ones doing this, but they have now an actual requirement in the undergraduate, like across all undergraduate degrees that there's an AI competency requirement, and they say, it's AI focused coursework designed to build critical thinking, responsible AI use, and workforce ready skills. So, yeah, I think to your point, Chris, you were mentioning mentoring people coming in out of university." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

## 博士还值得读吗？

Chris 问：==世界变得这么快，读博还有意义吗==？

Daniel 回顾自己的经历：他在博士项目里的成长——自信、批判性思维、解决问题、创造力——比高中和本科加起来高出许多个数量级。

博士的独特之处在于：不发表新颖的研究就拿不到学位。AI 再强，它目前只知道现有的知识。

更重要的价值是那个过程：你在 50 位资深专家的质疑下，为自己的新想法辩护。

这练出的是在模糊中工作、走向一个能站得住的立场的能力——创业也是同一种磨练 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="37:07" data-who="Daniel Whitenack" data-en="I have a mental model of the fundamentals. Now where can I draw outside of the lines? Knowing also that others who deeply know the fundamentals are going to be skeptical of me and question me around this... Where I'm drawing outside of the lines. Right?" aria-label="回原文"></button>。

Chris 从中受到的启发是：

能不能把这种“模糊、创造、挣扎”提前到基础教育阶段，把死记硬背交给工具，让孩子更早开始练真正的思考。

## 本集带走

- AI 比以往任何技术都更快地改变所有人的工作和学习方式，教育系统的“禁用”策略撑不下去了。
- 具体技能（拼写、某些计算）可能过时，但批判性思维、创造力和“挣扎中成长”的能力是永远的底座。
- 老师往往没有权限改变课程；真正卡脖子的是学区和州一级的课程审批。
- 研究显示：随意的聊天机器人辅助会降低独立考试成绩，精心设计的 AI 导师则优于传统课堂——怎么用是关键。
- Chris 的激进提案：开学就公开全年课程，让老师转型为个性化学习的引导者，人类教师的价值反而更高。
