这一集聊的是一次罕见决绝的创业转型：一家有 2000 万用户、融了 4300 万美元 B 轮的公司，在还没想清楚要做什么之前，就决定关掉旧产品、从 70 人裁到 7 人，从零再来。说这话的人是 [[Keith Peiris|Keith Peiris]],他创办的 [[Tome|Tome]] 原本是一款 AI 演示文稿产品，如今改名为 [[Lightfield|Lightfield]],做 AI 原生的 [[CRM|CRM]](客户关系管理系统)，正面挑战 [[Salesforce|Salesforce]] 和 HubSpot。

## 旧产品的问题：用户多，但没人在乎

Keith 判断一个产品有没有真正扎根的标准很朴素：问一句「如果我们把这个拿走，你会怎么样？」。

Tome 有 2000 万用户，ARR 只有几百万美元，获客成本超低(全靠自然流量，不用花钱买增长)，但重复使用率低、净收入留存([[NDR|NDR]],即老客户第二年还愿意付多少钱)只有 20% 左右。他形容：「如果我们把产品拿走，用户会有点难过，然后回到 Google Slides。」<button class="pd-ts" data-t="02:24" data-who="Keith Peiris" data-en="So it was actually, I would say the moment before scale, which is to say with our previous company, we got lots of users. I mean, they had like 20 million users. But the thing that was missing was that, you know, it wasn't like an indispensable part of anyone's life. If I asked our customers, what would happen if we took this away? I think they would be like, yeah, be a little sad. I'd go back to Google Slides. With Lightfield, the thing that got us excited was, you know, a CRM is a lot of work. We're nowhere near done. We're like a year, two and a half. And we're still missing a lot of stuff. We're catching up. But we built the earliest version of Lightfield in about three months. And we started asking customers to use it. At the time, it was friends. It was extended network. And even though it was missing like 99% of the features that Salesforce and HubSpot had, we had customers in it every day. And on the flip side, they had a lot of feedback. Just to compare with Tome, with Tome, we had lots and lots of users and none of them cared enough about the product to give us feedback. Whereas with Lightfield, our slacks were lit up, you know, 24-7, 365, about stuff that could be better, stuff we need to fix, stuff that could be different. And we were like, oh, this means that someone cares about this problem. Like our customers are using this all the time. They're hopping mad at us about the things that we got wrong. There's enough care here that maybe there's something here." aria-label="回原文"></button>

## 决定转型的两个领悟

第一，Tome 永远做不出惊艳的演示文稿。它缺上下文、缺数据结构，「这个东西永远不会长大、永远不会被那些做 deck 特别厉害的人使用」。顺着「谁做 deck 最多」这条线，他们找到了销售和市场团队，进了 10 家公司做试点——结果几周之内，所有人对「帮我们做 deck」失去了兴趣，转而拉着他们做客户调研、写提案、回复 RFP、写邮件。<button class="pd-ts" data-t="04:01" data-who="Keith Peiris" data-en="Yeah, it's so funny. I could talk to you about like the 20 metrics that I looked at that made me believe that, you know, Tome wasn't working. But honestly, the thing that like we really wrestled with is, hey, this is a presentations product and it's never going to be capable of making amazing presentations. It was just missing the context. It was missing the data structure. It was missing so much that we were like, this thing will never grow up and be used by the people that are incredible at making decks." aria-label="回原文"></button>

第二，做这些事需要的数据根本不在传统 CRM 里。销售信息散落在通话录音、邮件、Slack、数据仓库里，彼此对不上。

「我们就想，等等，在公司内部为客户建一个单一事实来源，这其实是个更重要的问题——因为以后到处都会有智能体，它们稍有错位就会产出糟糕的工作。」<button class="pd-ts" data-t="04:01" data-who="Keith Peiris" data-en="Yeah, it's so funny. I could talk to you about like the 20 metrics that I looked at that made me believe that, you know, Tome wasn't working. But honestly, the thing that like we really wrestled with is, hey, this is a presentations product and it's never going to be capable of making amazing presentations. It was just missing the context. It was missing the data structure. It was missing so much that we were like, this thing will never grow up and be used by the people that are incredible at making decks." aria-label="回原文"></button>

## 烧掉船只：在不知道做什么之前先归零

中间还有一段更黑暗的弯路：他们一度只做成叠在 Salesforce 和 Snowflake 之上的薄薄一层，数据不专有、模型要付钱给 Anthropic 或 OpenAI,「这个局面可能比 Tome 还糟」。<button class="pd-ts" data-t="15:56" data-who="Keith Peiris" data-en="Yeah. And I think, you know, hindsight's 20-20. To be honest, we had way more consumers than businesses and I think it actually would have been a more direct path to build a second consumer product. You know, we could have built like a, you know, what are these code generating tools or something and I think that would have been a shorter path. I think deep down, you know, Henry and I wanted to build a B2B product. We wanted to build something that like people used every day and cared about and that's probably what steered us. I'd argue it actually steered us initially in a much darker place because it steered us towards sales and marketing. As you know, like GTM tech is so crowded. It's brutal, right? It's super crowded because I would say most sales teams are willing to try something new, see if it helps them be more successful. But the sort of pilot conversion rate is super low. You know, most of these companies don't have proprietary data, right? So it's just a race to the bottom, right? If you have got like 30 tools in every company and they're all trying to offer roughly the same thing because they don't have the data, they don't have the model, right? And it just turns into a commodity game. And that was kind of the, I think the dark thing that we were trying to navigate in the, call it like mid pivot, where we're like, all right, we're here. You know, we've got these customers where we're building these tools. This also isn't going to lead to a very good business, you know, because we're just this layer that sits on top of Salesforce and Snowflake and we're not an LLM company, you know, so we're also just paying Anthropic or OpenAI. We've found some problems to solve, but like this, you know, at the time we were like, this is probably a worse setup than Tome. I think that that took some distance from the problem. You know, we started to sit back and be like, did any of us really wake up to build like this point solution, GTM piece of tech that's like hard to sell, hard to convert? I think we actually just needed some space from the problem to think about like, well, how's the world going to change? What's going to be different? And what do we actually want to do?" aria-label="回原文"></button>

真正推动决断的是董事会成员 Dan 的一句话：技术超级周期十年、二十年才一次，你现在太早了，资金和团队都在；如果选软着陆，你会被困在大公司两到四年，出来时这波就结束了。「为什么不干脆烧掉船只，去构建你有信念的东西？」<button class="pd-ts" data-t="11:24" data-who="Keith Peiris" data-en="Yeah, exactly. Exactly. It could be that you've got the right platform, you know, with the data, you know, with the infrastructure and you can build something else. Or maybe you just got a great team, right? And that's your asset and you can figure out what to build next. But I think it really depends on like what you want out of it. Because at that time, you know, when we were adding a million users a month or two million users a month, there probably was a way to get a soft landing for the team, you know, where investors make a little bit of money, the team does okay. We all go work at some big corporation for a couple years and then come out and do the next thing. And that was like top of mind for me. But the thing that actually really pushed me, it was one of my board members, Dan, who's on the board of Lightfield now, because of this advice, he was like, these technology super cycles, they don't happen every day, right? You know, this is the sort of thing that happens like once a decade or once every two decades. And he's like, you're so early. You know, at the time, we were like two years in and he was like, you have a lot of capital, you've got an incredible team. You know, if you go and take the like, the soft landing, you're going to be stuck there for two to four years. You're going to come out and this thing will be over." aria-label="回原文"></button>

于是他们在还不知道要做什么的情况下，关掉旧产品、大幅削减消耗、裁到 7 个人。Keith 的理由：一个半死不活的公司里噪音太多——客户工单、市场活动、infra 账单都还在，「我必须杀死噪音，把所有不想构建新东西的人请出房间」。<button class="pd-ts" data-t="19:59" data-who="Keith Peiris" data-en="Yeah. Yeah. To be honest, it was the right move because you just have so much noise in a company that's half working. Your team, it's not their fault. It's your fault. Red is the CEO. It's my fault. But the team's like, we got to find something to work on. Otherwise, what are we doing? Right? There's sort of like a, like a toxic quality in the air of like, you know, do these guys even know what they're doing? And the truth is we, you know, we, we didn't. And, you know, you're still have customer tickets and you're still have, you know, marketing campaigns and, you know, you still have your like infrabill. So at that point, my view is I have to kill the noise. Right? So we got to cut burn. We got to get everyone out of the room that doesn't want to build the new thing. And unfortunately, we couldn't get rid of this office. We had this giant office and it was kind of awkward for seven people to show up." aria-label="回原文"></button>

还有一个关键认知支撑了这个决定：**指标是业务天性的副产品，很难靠优化翻盘**。Keith 的强烈看法是「你不可能拿着同一个产品把 NDR 从 20 提到 40,留存几乎是从核心产品继承来的」。

他好多创始人朋友说自己的获客成本八年没变过，「第一年你有什么，第五年就大致有什么。你必须决定，这是不是你想玩的游戏」。<button class="pd-ts" data-t="12:53" data-who="Keith Peiris" data-en="I think there was that path. My strong view on this is that you can't take the same product and take NDR from 20 to 40. I think you almost like inherit retention based off of the core product and then you can make it a little better but like, it's not going from 20 to 80 ever, you know, with this kind of product. So I was almost taking for granted that like, retention would be about the same but I could play with acquisition. I could play with selling the next thing." aria-label="回原文"></button>

## 为什么选「重造系统记录」这条最难的路

团队最终锁定方向，有四个理由：一要拥有并索引专有数据，不做没有数据的同质化工具；二是技术挑战够硬——做一个智能体化的[[记录系统|系统记录]](system of record,即公司存放客户事实真相的核心系统)，比「vibe coding 一个 CRM」难得多；三是这个赛道打法怪异到没几个人会试——头几年只能做还没有 CRM 的新公司(Greenfield),服务一个小且高流失的客户群，还得跑得够快，免得客户起量后转投 Salesforce。「我们宁愿攻克一个困难的问题，也不愿解决一个简单问题然后面对一千个竞争者。」<button class="pd-ts" data-t="22:24" data-who="Keith Peiris" data-en="I just mean being in go-to-market in general. Okay. We're like, we need to own and index proprietary data. No question. Second, we're like, look, we're productivity tool people. You know, and I think we all got excited about the technical challenge of building, you know, an agentic system of record. We're like, this sounds super hard. You know, even though there's people talking about vibe coding a CRM, you know, to build an automatic agentic CRM that works for like a thousand people, you know, in terms of API data model, we're like, we would enjoy doing this. And then I would say that it feels like one of those strange go-to markets where it's sort of hard to pull off. Let me explain it. You know, ripping out systems of record, it's incredibly hard historically, a little easier now. We're starting to do it now. But the first couple of years of the company, you have to go Greenfield, right? You have to go be new companies for CRM. And then, many have to build fast enough so that when their company works, they don't go to Salesforce, right? We're like, that seems like almost crazy enough of a company that there aren't going to be that many people that try, you know, which is to say it's a huge build out. You're working on a small ICP, super churny, and then for you to hold on the best ones, you need to run as fast as you can. And actually, that difficulty made us excited because we were like, we'd rather tackle a difficult problem than tackle an easy problem and have like a thousand competitors. So that sort of got us excited. And then I think maybe the last part is that it felt like a company-wide problem. And I think that that got the founding team really excited. They're like, look, if you imagine how a system of record works in the future, people that work with customers fill it, everyone benefits from it. You know, engineers should use it, finance should use it, you know, forward-to-play engineers should use that. And we're like, we're actually excited about that to have a small hand in shaping how future companies get built. And we're like, we could all work 14 hours a day on that. So it actually felt like a very personal decision." aria-label="回原文"></button> 四是这是个全公司的问题——未来销售填数据，工程、财务、售前都受益，创始团队愿意每天为它干 14 小时。

切入点也是被客户逼出来的：中型市场客户说「不可能换系统记录」，于是他们下沉到 YC 和种子期公司，对方回答：「如果你从第一天就解决好这个问题，我付你的钱会超过 HubSpot,而且只要跟得上我们的需求，我们就一直用。」<button class="pd-ts" data-t="25:13" data-who="Keith Peiris" data-en="Yeah, exactly. That was the thinking. And then we just kept pulling the thread, you know, because obviously when we were speaking to, you know, we had some mid-market customers early on in the pivot. They were like, that sounds cool. We can't possibly change our system of record. So then we needed to go, honestly, a little earlier. You know, we started speaking to YC companies. You know, we started speaking to seed stage companies, speed run companies. And they were like, if you like solve this problem correctly on day one, that's awesome. One, I would pay you way more than I pay for like a HubSpot or an Adio. And two, you know, we would be inclined to stay on this thing as long as you can keep up with our demands. I think that was the first time we were like, oh, interesting. There's actually like a new kind of wedge to solve the problem we want to solve." aria-label="回原文"></button> 六个月后他们才找准痛点：卖给 YC 公司，第一件事不是卖系统记录，而是帮它做 pipeline 生成——基于它已有的客户建名单、写触达；卖给大公司，则是解决销售与实施交付之间的完美交接，因为「Salesforce 从来不是为智能体设计的」。

## 怎么卖给 YC 创始人

Keith 的打法有三招：

- **换渠道、换开场**：不用冷邮件，用 LinkedIn(对方一点击就能看到你的资料和可信度)；从不以「买我的产品」开场，而是请人来办公室办晚宴、请 HubSpot 的 CRO 和《Predictable Revenue》的作者来演讲——「我们不会向你推销任何东西」。请动作者的代价是买他 100 本书送给到场观众。<button class="pd-ts" data-t="30:25" data-who="Keith Peiris" data-en="We did a couple of things and the first thing I would say is as a founder, you should have good taste on how you want to be bothered, right? ## 本集带走

- **用「拿走测试」判断产品是否扎根**：问客户「如果把这个拿走会怎样」——回答「有点难过，回到旧工具」说明没戏；每天在用、气得跳脚提反馈，才是真正有戏的信号。
- **指标翻不了盘，业务天性才能**：NDR 从 20 到 40 不靠优化同一个产品实现；获客成本这类指标第一年是什么，第五年大致还是什么。核心不对，销售和营销上的努力只有正负 5-10%。
- **敢在没想清楚前先归零**：关掉半死不活的旧产品、把消耗砍到底、把不想做新东西的人请出房间——半运转公司的噪音，是思考新方向的头号敌人。
- **有分发就别从零开始**：旧产品的客户关系是资产，带着「我的客户还有什么更痛的问题」的开放心态进去，问题自己会把方向拽出来。
- **卖复杂软件：免费送 + 活动做人情**：前 10 个客户免费换满意，靠圈子的转介绍起量；用请名人演讲、办晚宴代替冷邮件，LinkedIn 开场先给背景不推产品。
- **发布视频：窄定受众、讲故事、同步引爆**：不优化播放量，优化「目标人群看了有感觉」；两个月、低六位数、反复迭代；发布日组织几百人同一时刻点赞评论转发。
