Let's get into it. Welcome to The Product Podcast, Faisal." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

他最反直觉的一个观点,是关于 Shopify 联合创始人 Tobi 那份鼓励全员用 AI 的 CEO 宣言的。Faisal 指出其中的讽刺:一方面鼓励所有人 all-in、没搞清楚 AI 就别来开会,另一方面又抱怨组织里垃圾内容(AI 生成的低质量内容)泛滥——「你不能鼓励一个组织毫无护栏地 all-in,然后又对大量垃圾内容有意见。

你必须选定你的车道」<button class="pd-ts" data-t="04:51" data-who="Faisal Masud" data-en="My take, and I wrote about this, is that the FOMO is off the charts. Two years ago it was completely off the charts, and now it's settling down a bit with all the tokens spent. But I always find it's good to be an early adopter, and it's also good to have some data around the adoption, versus just going all-in. The response you're referring to might have been one around the slop grenades he's talking about. The irony I found was: on one hand, we're encouraging everybody that AI will replace every job, don't come to a meeting if you haven't figured it out with AI, and encouraging that as much as possible. And then, on the other hand, talking about how it's just slop central and that's what's happening. With the introduction of AI at work, for those adopting it, it's becoming hard to understand what's actually written by a human versus pure slop. I find it ironic that you can't encourage an organization to go all-in, which is what they did, with no guardrails around it, and then, on the other hand, have a problem with the massive amounts of slop in your organization. You have to pick your lane. Getting Through the Slop to Quality" aria-label="回原文"></button>。他的立场是:做早期采用者是好事,但要有采用数据,而不是直接 all-in。

AI 真正擅长的是「告知和咨询」——帮你获取信息、当顾问;它不能做的是让你完全复制粘贴,那里需要[[人类判断|人类判断]]。他引用 Nassim Taleb 的说法表示认同:高智商但没有原创想法的人,在 AI 世界里会非常艰难 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="06:31" data-who="Faisal Masud" data-en="AI has a very dominant position in what we do at our enterprise, and even outside a startup environment, AI is really good at the inform and consult part of the equation: getting information so you can consult with it. But what it can't be is completely copy-paste. That's where things get difficult, because human judgment is going to become really important here. If you lack ideas and you're relying heavily on AI for everything, you're not really going to be that important. Nassim Taleb recently tweeted this. I don't know if you follow him, but he said something like, those with high IQ and no original ideas are going to find it very hard in the world of AI. And I tend to agree. You have to be creative at the same time as utilizing AI along the way. So I think you did the right thing, and you probably expected the slop to go up and at some point come back down. I'm still waiting for the come-back-down part." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

## AI 不是你的战略

现在涌入市场的工具太多了——知识图谱、集成进协作工具、机器人警报——「在某些情况下,它们在给你的工作流里增加更多工作,而不是减少」<button class="pd-ts" data-t="08:01" data-who="Faisal Masud" data-en="I've seen enough tools now flooding the market: knowledge graph this, integrated into all your collaboration tools, getting alerts and alarms through bots powered by the litany of providers. In some cases they're adding more work. They're actually introducing more work into your daily workflow, not deducting it. So where I have a challenge is: AI is not your strategy. It's part of your strategy. It's going to help with your strategy. It cannot become the strategy, because then you've got a problem. It's a tool in the toolkit. It's not going to be the be-all and end-all. Leveraging it in the best way possible while utilizing human judgment, that's where the real impact is. The AI Layer Being Built on Top of SaaS" aria-label="回原文"></button>。所以他的核心主张是:AI 是工具箱里的一件工具,它帮你执行战略,但不能成为战略本身;真正的影响力在于「在运用人类判断的同时以最好的方式利用它」。

至于企业怎么用?HP 自己的路子是内部工作流先行。

对产品经理的 push 很直接:能通过 AI 完成的就必须通过 AI 完成,需要人类在环的才自己上。从构思到 PRD,大量环节被大幅压缩,连早期演示视频都变得非常容易——「直到你需要走向生产环境,那完全是另一回事」<button class="pd-ts" data-t="11:04" data-who="Faisal Masud" data-en="Our strategy is for internal workflows. I'm not running IT for the organizations I sell into; I sell into IT leaders. But for our internal organization, the product managers are pushed very hard: what can be done through AI should be done through AI, and what needs a human in the loop, you do that. So the PMs use all the tools available, whether it's the early days of ChatGPT down to Claude now, and wherever they can use tools, they do all that work, from ideation to PRD and beyond. A lot of that is getting heavily compressed. Even early demo videos are so easy now. Everything is just so much easier now, until you need to go to production. That's a whole different ballgame. What we do internally is try to make sure we can leverage as much of the out-of-the-box capabilities Microsoft provides in every facet possible, and then internally accelerate our overall cycle time for getting from ideation all the way to deploying features to the market. Our developers use Codex, Claude Code, and the PMs are all pretty heavy users of AI as well. Pushing Through Enterprise Bureaucracy" aria-label="回原文"></button>。

开发者用 Codex 和 Claude Code。他承认大企业有官僚摩擦,但他的解法是经营内部关系、把人带到你这边——比如他刚加入时力推 Figma 全员设计,现在摩擦比想象中小,真正的痛点反而是 token 烧得太快、预算得算得清 [[ROI|ROI]] <button class="pd-ts" data-t="12:48" data-who="Faisal Masud" data-en="Large organizations are about relationships. As much as there's friction to get approvals for certain tools or capabilities, which could be exposure for the organization, you have to build the internal relationships to bring people with you. Early on, back when I joined, Figma was pretty popular, but it wasn't really being utilized much internally. I pushed very hard to make sure that whatever out-of-the-box capabilities Figma provided, we were using for all our design. That's now obviously moved forward to pretty much being able to do everything with the toolset you have internally. I've not seen a ton of challenges outside of the fact that, when you're using a lot of these products, the tokens run out fairly quickly and the budgets are challenging. So that's where it comes down to: where's the ROI for all of this? Because if you can't see the ROI on the amount of money you're spending on those tokens, you have to be able to do the math and be okay with it. When it comes to friction internally, I'd say we haven't faced the level of friction I would assume from a large enterprise like ourselves in utilizing the tools available. Change Has to Come From the Top" aria-label="回原文"></button>。

## 劝客户少买硬件的软件生意

Faisal 负责的主力产品叫 [[Workforce Experience|Workforce Experience]]:一个提供客户全部 IT 覆盖面(硬件、软件、操作系统、网络)洞察的平台。讽刺在于,它同时鼓励客户「少买」——这对硬件公司是反直觉的。理由是:偏向性会毁掉世界级的员工体验,立足原则必须是「如何改善我销售对象的体验」<button class="pd-ts" data-t="14:39" data-who="Faisal Masud" data-en="I can tell you there was a lot of blood, sweat, and tears going into building the original thesis for Workforce Experience. We're in a business where our platform provides insights on our customers' total surface area: their hardware, software, OS, network, and so on. But at the same time, we also encourage buying less, which is counterproductive to a hardware business, right? So why do we do that? Because ultimately we exist because we want to make that employee experience absolutely world-class. And you can't make a world-class employee experience if you've got a bias in the process. From the top, you have to make sure that bias is removed, and your central focus, your grounding principle, is: how do I improve the experience for the people I'm selling this into? For that, you have to make some trade-offs. To your point, it wasn't easy to convince everybody, but it's less about convincing everyone. It's really important to convince yourself first: Will this work? How relevant is it? Will it have ROI for the people you're selling it into? Is there product-market fit? Is this a plug product, or are you selling it into these people? You have to go through all these questions. Once we found we had PMF, it was all roads leading to &quot;let's go build this faster.&quot; Go-to-Market: Selling the Solution, Not the Hardware" aria-label="回原文"></button>。

走向市场上有个关键决策:不做单点解决方案,做完全设备无关、操作系统无关的平台——「我们不在乎你运行什么硬件,我们想帮你把那个环境运行得更好」<button class="pd-ts" data-t="16:13" data-who="Faisal Masud" data-en="We do both. One big friction point early on, although it didn't really become an issue, was when I started pitching Workforce Experience as not a point solution, which we used to have back in the day. Point solutions are interesting, but they're not that relevant to the economic buyer long-term. When you're pitching a platform, the platform has to be completely agnostic of your own stuff that's involved in it. So we went device-agnostic and OS-agnostic. We didn't care if the fleets we were selling into were competitors' brands or a completely different OS. Our principled approach was: we don't care what hardware you're running, we want to make sure we can help you run that environment better. Once you have those very specific principles outlined on why you're doing that, and people are on board, it becomes a lot easier to move forward. On how we do the go-to-market: we rely heavily on our existing go-to-market rails. As a very large hardware seller, an OEM, we are constantly in conversations where our account reps are dealing with customers who are buying competitor product. In the past, HP used to sell third-party software. We don't do that anymore. All the IP we've built, we're selling as our own software. So the transition was basically: we're not going to do that anymore, we're going to sell our own home-built, owned-IP product. In those conversations, what's evolved now is that instead of selling hardware, we're selling the solution: what problem are you trying to solve? Our go-to-market relies heavily on CIOs and RFPs coming in with explanations around &quot;we're trying to solve this problem,&quot; and Workforce Experience fits into that, versus selling the software with the hardware, which just doesn't scale. How Close Are You to the Outcome?" aria-label="回原文"></button>。HP 不再代售第三方软件,改卖自研、自有 IP 的产品,客户经理从卖硬件转向卖解决方案。而且这个产品是先在自己身上验证的:HP 内部有 80,000 台设备,说服自家 IT 团队用了很高的门槛,走了很长的 alpha、beta 和延长发布,每个阶段带 15 到 100 个合作伙伴一起审视 ROI,然后才走向市场 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="18:35" data-who="Faisal Masud" data-en="We're very clear on that, because when we started dogfooding the product inside HP, it wasn't a low bar. It was a high bar to convince our own IT teams to use it. We had 80,000 devices and hundreds, if not thousands, of pieces of software running in these environments. We had to convince our internal teams that this is the path forward and it will help them manage HP's IT environment better. Not until we crossed that threshold and accomplished some success there did we even go to market. So we had a very long alpha, a pretty long beta, then an extended release. We didn't go GA for a long time, and at every stage we had 15 to 20 to 100 partners alongside us looking at the ROI of what we deliver. An example: we're not walking into our customers' offices saying, &quot;buy this and we'll promise you the world and nothing will happen on the other side.&quot; We're actually telling them their ticket volume will go down by 30 to 40%, because Workforce Experience will tackle all those tickets behind the scenes. Second, we have a feature called Smart Refresh, which evaluates all your fleet and says what you should not be buying. You should be buying 55,000 machines, upgrade these, and Workforce Experience can manage all the others in a perfect configuration, so you can extend the life of your machine. That comes at a very low cost to them, because we can manage all of it. But think about that for a second: nobody else is doing that." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

## 席位变少之后,定价跟着结果走

人类席位变少对传统按席位收费的 SaaS 是威胁,但 Faisal 说对他完全没问题——因为他们承诺的本来就是 ROI。「我们可以想象这样一个世界:你每年有 X 张工单,我们承诺整体减少这么多,基于此收取一笔基于结果的费用」。

## 本集带走

- **AI 是工具不是战略**:它擅长「告知和咨询」,不能接受无脑复制粘贴;没有原创想法、事事依赖 AI 的人会变得不重要。
- **all-in 之前先设护栏**:鼓励全员用 AI 又抱怨垃圾内容,是自相矛盾;垃圾内容先升后落,而「回落的部分我还在等」。
- **先内部验证再上市**:80,000 台设备上先说服自家 IT 团队,超长的 alpha/beta + 合作伙伴陪跑审视 ROI,才走向市场。
- **平台必须中性**:设备无关、操作系统无关,连自家硬件都「不可知」,才能拿到经济买家的长期信任。
- **卖结果,不卖仪表板**:承诺工单降 30-40%、解决时间降 30%、设备采购省 25-30%;定价要么按席位附结果承诺,要么纯按结果收费。
- **AI 先削的是中层**:监督、汇总、上报这些层被智能体替代;原来的管理者变成「管理智能体的 IC」,但高保真决定仍由人拍板。
