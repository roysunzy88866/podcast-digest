## 画中画导览：在真实项目里动手，而不是看箭头

Basecamp 之前的引导基本就是指箭头——"这是这儿，这是那儿，这样用"。到做 HEY（他们的邮件产品）的时候，他们试了一种新方式：用户必须把邮件一封封筛选进来，这个过程本身就是在互动中学会产品 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="00:30" data-who="Jason" data-en="Yeah, onboarding is something that’s always been really fun to work on, although Basecamp never really had a great onboarding. It was decent and there’s some things we did to make it better, but it was a little bit like pointing arrows at things and this is where this is and this is where this is and this is how this works. And when we did HEY a number of years ago, we went through a really nice process of sending you basically a bunch of emails and you have to screen them in because that’s the first step in HEY. And so I’m not going to go into all the detail, but it became this interactive thing that you work through the product to get to understand and learn the product. And we never really had that in Basecamp and now we do. So with Basecamp 5, when you sign up, we have a whole new approach and actually it’s very different. We have a picture-in-picture..." aria-label="回原文"></button>。这套体验成了他们的新标杆。

Basecamp 5 把这个思路往前推了一步。注册之后，用户看到的是一个内容完整的真实项目，不是空壳。

角落里有一个画中画小视频，[[Kimberly|Kimberly]] 出镜说"试试这个，试试那个"。用户就在这个真实项目里点来点去、留评论、随便玩。

玩完之后，下一步才是创建自己的第一个项目。整个过程没有风险——你不会搞砸任何真正重要的东西 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="02:25" data-who="Unknown" data-en="But what we do is we say, here’s a project, a fully fleshed out project. Play around with it. Click this, do this, leave a comment. It’s a real project. And before you get to make your own, you just walk through a real one and you really mess around with it. And there’s a little video of you in the corner saying, “Hey, try this and try that, whatever.” And you’re like, “Okay, I got that.” And you’re like, “Next.” And then the next step is now make your own first project. And you kind of make that and you get to learn that procedure and you’re using the product to learn how to use the product. And there’s no stakes here. That’s the other thing. You’re not making projects that matter and you’re not going to screw anything up if you play around. And then at the end it’s like, okay, now I’m ready to go." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

整个导览大概三分钟。走完之后，界面完整呈现，侧边栏会再推几条通知，教你更多功能，同时你也学会了怎么用通知系统本身 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="03:01" data-who="Unknown" data-en="And then we bring the whole interface in and drop you basically to a sidebar. We have a few more notifications for a few more things you can learn. And at that point, we’ve sort of explained the whole thing with the product and also with your notifications so you learn how to use those too. It’s a really kind of a cool process and I’m proud of it as well. It feels really good. The product has not been around long enough to look back on it and see how it’s been doing compared to the previous versions of that, but we will at one point. And I don’t know, it’s always fun I think to explore onboarding because the ideal version of onboarding, the thing I’ve always tried to do or try to think about is what would it be like to sit next to somebody and walk them through the product?" aria-label="回原文"></button>。

Jason 说他心里一直有个黄金标准：如果你能坐在每个客户旁边，亲手带他们过一遍产品，你会怎么做？他觉得 Basecamp 5 是他们最接近这个愿景的一次 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="03:36" data-who="Unknown" data-en="If you could literally sit next to every single customer and go, here’s what I want to show you, what would you do? And that’s the gold standard for me. And I think the current version of Basecamp is maybe the closest we’ve gotten to that. Like, hey, here’s a project, play around with it. Here’s how you make your own, play around with it. Now here’s this stuff, and then you’re off on your own. Anyway, that’s kind of what I’m always aiming for. And I think Basecamp 5 has a pretty good shot at being as accurate as possible to that vision." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

## 引导为什么总是烂：你自己不注册，当然不知道坏了

David 指出了一个结构性问题：做产品的人自己不注册自己的产品。他们天天泡在满是数据的账户里，根本看不到"稀疏状态"——只有一个项目、两个待办列表时产品长什么样 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="04:01" data-who="David" data-en="I think one of the reasons why this is hard to do really well is that you don’t see it that often. We are not signing up for Basecamp every day because we already have Basecamp and we’re very engrossed in it and we have a lot of data in it. So one thing is the signup flow, you’re putting in your credentials and you’re getting onboarded. But it’s also what does the product look like when it’s sparse? What does it look like when it only has one project, when it only has two to-do lists? Those states, blank slates we used to call that when there was literally nothing and tried to do some stuff around that, but I think structurally why it’s hard for software companies to really get good at it is because there’s not a lot of iterations. Even for us, this required some very dedicated focus." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

更致命的是漂移。你发布那天做好了引导流程，然后产品不断迭代，东西挪了位置、加了新功能，但注册流程还停在发布日的样子。因为团队不会每天都走一遍注册流，不像某个拖拽交互坏了，你天天被烦就会去修 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="05:31" data-who="Unknown" data-en="But before that, it had been a long time. It’d probably been months, maybe in some cases it could even have been a year. There’s always opportunities for these things to drift so that your nice onboarding flow, yeah, it was nice when you designed it and then seven things changed in the product and things moved around and maybe you introduced new features or whatever and you just don’t see it or it just didn’t keep up. You kept evolving the product, you kept making it better and improving and so on. But the signup process is as it was on launch day because that was the last time you looked at it. So I think those are some of the structural factors of why it often seems weird because as a new customer, this is the thing you see. The first thing you see is the onboarding flow." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

David 说他以前注册别人的产品，看到明显过时的引导会想"他们怎么不知道？"，现在回想起来，他自己上次注册 Basecamp 可能都是一年前的事了。所以现在他多了点同理心 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="06:10" data-who="Unknown" data-en="So you of course assume that it’s going to be the best that that team has to offer. And that’s just not the case. In almost all cases, onboarding flow is going to be substantially worse than the rest of the product if someone had kept working on it because all the attention goes somewhere else. So having gone through that and then having seen it on the inside gives me a little more empathy when I go through one of those kind of mediocre or even shitty onboarding flows where I go like, “Wow, why can’t they just? How could they not see this?” And I go like, “Yeah, maybe I wouldn’t have seen it either.”" aria-label="回原文"></button>。

## CEO 亲自演示：不是销售，是展示哲学

Jason 不只是设计了画中画引导，他还亲自上阵。他在 Twitter 上说，接下来 90 天内注册某个方案的前五名客户，他每隔几周带他们过一遍账户，随时问答。立刻就满了五个人 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="06:53" data-who="Jason" data-en="I just enjoy it. I mean, I’m really proud of the product. Basecamp 5 is really, really good and I just want to show people. And so I basically said, hey, I’ll take five customers under my wing, wherever the heck their language was. If you sign up for this plan over the next 90 days, we’ll do a call every couple of weeks. I’ll walk you through our account and we can do Q&amp;A along the way. And immediately got five people who want to do that. And so I’m working with them now. We haven’t started yet, but we have some scheduled calls coming up. And it’s a joy to be proud of what you’ve made and to show it off. What better thing is there to do? And what’s really cool about Basecamp 5 specifically is that it’s gotten so good and it’s so flexible that I feel like there’s nothing it can’t do for somebody who’s like, well, how do I manage this and how do I manage that? And what about this? What about that?" aria-label="回原文"></button>。

他说这不是销售。他不推销，不承诺定制功能，不在乎你买不买。

他就是想展示产品，因为他为它自豪 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="08:48" data-who="Jason" data-en="Yeah, I don’t know. I mean, I just think of myself as a guy who likes the product and knows it inside and out and I’m happy to show it off. So wherever my role is my role. But the key is that someone’s available and I’m not a salesperson. I’m not trying to sell you anything. I don’t care if you buy it or not. I think it’ll leave an impression, make an impression on you and it’s up to you at that point. And I’m not making promises about adding things or it’s not like a sales thing where I’ll take that back to the team and we’ll make these three customizations. I’m proud to show the product off and it should be the job that I do. I should show the product off. We should show the product off. We’re proud of it. And we do a lot of things in it that people don’t know are possible." aria-label="回原文"></button>。而且演示的时候，他传递的不只是功能，还有他们的工作哲学——比如很多人问"怎么接 GitHub issues"，他的回答是"你其实不需要接那些"。

人们现在被教育成觉得需要一堆工具、设计师和程序员分开在不同地方工作。而 Basecamp 的方式是一个程序员和一个设计师在同一个地方协作，这常常让人震惊 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="07:39" data-who="Unknown" data-en="And people are like, “How do you hook in GitHub issues and all these things?” I’m like, “You don’t actually have to do those.” People come at software these days thinking you need a bunch of tools and a bunch of separate things and a bunch of separate places. People are confused and they think designers work in one place and programmers work in another. And so not only do we get to show them the product, but we get to show them the point of view and the philosophy of working that a team is two people. It’s a programmer and a designer. Those are the functional roles, but they work together in the same place on the same stuff. And that blows people’s minds and it’s always fun to blow people’s minds. That’s a fun thing to do. And to do it in such a simple way and you can show them how we do it, it really makes an impression and it’s just great fun. I actually really enjoy it. Aside from making the thing, I think demoing the thing is my second most favorite thing to do. It’s great to just hop on a Zoom and show people our account and walk them through it. And that’s open for anybody. Anyone who wants to do that, you can get in touch with me. I’m happy to set something up with you." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

Kimberly 提到，让人惊讶的不仅是这件事本身，而是做这件事的人是 CEO <button class="pd-ts" data-t="08:33" data-who="Kimberly" data-en="I think also what probably is blowing people’s minds, I mean myself included, that you were doing it. It’s not just like someone random. The CEO, the founder is doing it. I think that’s kind of unheard of for most companies." aria-label="回原文"></button>。David 的说法更直白：大多数"职业 CEO"根本不碰自己的产品，就像麦当劳 CEO 在广告里咬一小口汉堡，看起来像完全不吃自己东西的人，语言里都透着不对劲 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="13:01" data-who="Unknown" data-en="You say, Kimberly, like, oh, you’re surprised that Jason would do that. I can understand it’s surprised because most company CEOs, certainly the professional ones never do. And that’s why their shit sucks because they’re not actually exposed to it. It reminds me of the McDonald’s CEO. I’m going to take a bite of this tasty product here. And he takes this little nibble and he looks like a total fucking moron, right? Because he looks like someone who despises his own product, who would never in a million years eat a McDonald’s burger. He just takes a tiny little bite of a product. Not a burger, a product. Already there, just in that language, you know it’s all wrong. You know, here’s someone who doesn’t give two fucks about the actual burger, whether it’s tasty or not. And I think this is the magic in large part about founder-led companies, that someone who went from there was nothing here, I literally had to make every sale manually, is way more likely to stay connected to that exchange, reciprocal exchange." aria-label="回原文"></button>。他认为[[创始人主导的公司|创始人主导的公司]]有一个天然优势——创始人从零开始手动做过每一单销售，更可能保持和客户之间的双向连接 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="14:02" data-who="Unknown" data-en="I actually care about the thing I’m selling. I’m not selling product. I’m selling burgers. I’m selling project management tools. I’m selling something specific. I’m not selling a category. I’m not selling content. And it’s surprisingly hard, I think, to create that connection, especially after the fact, and especially if you are a company who’s being led by professionals who could just as well sell burgers, that they could sell toilet papers, they could sell sugar water to invoke Steve Jobs again." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

## 引导也是介绍"我们是谁"

Basecamp 5 的引导还有一层目的：不只是教人用产品，是介绍公司本身。注册后第一件事是看到 Jason 的一封信（格式信函，但写的是他本人的邮箱地址，有问题可以直接写给他，他每天亲自回） <button class="pd-ts" data-t="09:24" data-who="Unknown" data-en="And how would they ever know? You can make a marketing site about all this stuff, but people aren’t going to even look at that. They want to watch something. They want to walk through something and it’s fun to show these things off. I think the other thing we try to do with Basecamp 5 onboarding is not only introduce people to the product, but introduce people to us. Like what are we like as a company? Who are we? Which is why you’re heavily featured. Look, we have friendly faces here. We talk in normal words. We don’t use ridiculous technical jargon. Also, the first thing you see when you sign up is a letter from me. Obviously it’s a form letter. I wrote it once. I’m not writing each person a letter, but it’s like, hey, here I am and here’s my email address. Write me if you have any questions." aria-label="回原文"></button>。应用内的公告也改成了个人头像发出，来自 Jason、Kimberly 或 Brian，而不是"Basecamp 公司"这个笼统品牌 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="11:07" data-who="Kimberly" data-en="And actually another example that you made me think of is our announcements that we send to customers within the app are now personalized. So it used to be just generically coming from the company, Basecamp. Now it is an avatar, it’s a message from Jason, it’s a message from me, it’s a message from Brian. So that is just that additional, we’re real people. There’s real people behind the scenes. It’s not just this generic company brand." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

Jason 说，现在这个行业里产品越来越像——大家都在往中间塞一个大文本框，打字就行。产品之间区分度越来越低。

那你怎么让人记住你？除了产品本身，还有你作为一家公司是什么样的人、多平易近人、多愿意沟通 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="10:40" data-who="Unknown" data-en="I don’t think they really know what they want necessarily. And also the products become more and more alike, not more and more different from one another. And then it’s like, why do you exist? Why are there five things that kind of do the same thing? So I think differentiating yourselves not only in terms of the product offering, but also in terms of who you are, how approachable you are, presentable you are, open you are to communication is a fundamentally important thing that we try to do as often as we can. And I think the onboarding process is the first example of that." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

## 双向收获：演示也是用户测试

David 补充说，这种一对一不是单向的。他们以为发布后会有争议的改动，有的确实成了问题，但很多要么根本没人注意到，要么被注意到的是他们完全没预料到的地方 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="11:28" data-who="David" data-en="I also think it’s a very reciprocal process actually. Yes, someone is getting a tour of Basecamp, but Jason or I are getting a tour of how someone receives Bacecamp. What they notice, what they don’t notice, this was one of the things, especially with Basecamp 5, where we changed quite a lot of things and some of the changes we thought before launching it, these were going to be the controversial bits. <script>
(function(){
  function fmt(s){
    if(!isFinite(s)||s<0) s=0;
    var m=Math.floor(s/60), x=Math.floor(s%60);
    return (m<10?'0':'')+m+':'+(x<10?'0':'')+x;
  }
  function wire(box){
    if(box.dataset.wired) return; box.dataset.wired='1';
    var a=box.querySelector('audio'), pb=box.querySelector('.pb'),
        bar=box.querySelector('.bar'), fill=box.querySelector('.bar > i'),
        tm=box.querySelector('.tm'), t2=box.querySelector('.t2');
    if(!a||!pb||!bar||!fill||!tm) return;
    var total=0;
    function paint(){
      var cur=a.currentTime||0;
      fill.style.width=(total?(cur/total*100):0)+'%';
      tm.textContent=fmt(cur)+(total?' / '+fmt(total):'');
    }
    a.addEventListener('loadedmetadata',function(){
      total=a.duration||0;
      if(total&&t2) t2.textContent=Math.round(total/60)+' 分钟 · AI 合成朗读';
      paint();
    });
    a.addEventListener('timeupdate',paint);
    a.addEventListener('play',function(){ pb.textContent='❚❚'; pb.setAttribute('aria-label','暂停'); });
    a.addEventListener('pause',function(){ pb.textContent='▶'; pb.setAttribute('aria-label','播放'); });
    a.addEventListener('ended',function(){ pb.textContent='▶'; });
    pb.addEventListener('click',function(){ if(a.paused) a.play(); else a.pause(); });
    function seek(ev){
      if(!total) return;
      if(ev.clientX==null) return;
      var r=bar.getBoundingClientRect();
      var x=Math.min(Math.max(ev.clientX-r.left,0),r.width);
      a.currentTime=(x/r.width)*total;
      paint();
    }
    bar.addEventListener('pointerdown',function(ev){
      seek(ev);
      function mv(e){ seek(e); }
      function up(){ document.removeEventListener('pointermove',mv); document.removeEventListener('pointerup',up); }
      document.addEventListener('pointermove',mv); document.addEventListener('pointerup',up);
    });
    a.addEventListener('error',function(){
      box.classList.add('pd-play-dead');
      box.textContent='本集中文精华音频还没生成好,稍后再来听。';
    });
  }
  function all(){ document.querySelectorAll('.pd-play').forEach(wire); }
  document.addEventListener('nav', all);
  if(document.readyState==='loading') document.addEventListener('DOMContentLoaded', all); else all();
})();
</script>
