# 视觉协作在 AI 时代为什么消失了

<div class="pd-byl"><b>Elaina O'Mahoney</b> · Mural CPO · 2026-08-27</div>

这一集是 Product School 的 CEO Carlos 和 [[Mural|Mural]] 的首席产品官 Elaina 聊[[视觉协作|视觉协作]]在 AI 时代的处境。Mural 是一个在线白板协作工具，疫情期间靠替代线下头脑风暴快速崛起。Elaina 的核心判断很反直觉：AI 时代里，视觉协作反而缺席了。<button class="pd-ts" data-t="03:01" data-who="Elaina O'Mahoney" data-en="Yeah, what's interesting is visual collaboration is actually absent in the AI era. We are all in our tools, all by ourselves, with words or voice. I don't know if you dictate to yourself too often or speak to your LLM of choice often. I probably do that more than type these days, but it's very lonely and it's not visual. And when I go to collaborate with my coworkers, I'm now sharing words in a doc, rather than understanding through visual collaboration and designing something more easily digestible to get to decision-making faster." aria-label="回原文"></button>

## 想法廉价了，瓶颈变成了共享上下文

现在用 AI，你可以在几小时内从想法变成可用的[[原型|原型]]，几分钟做出一套幻灯片——想法极度廉价。但问题出在下一步：当你把做出来的东西分享给团队，大家又回到各自的孤岛里看文档。每个人在跟自己的 LLM 对话，本质上是在玩单机游戏，彼此不知道对方从哪里开始、到了哪一步。<button class="pd-ts" data-t="04:23" data-who="Elaina O'Mahoney" data-en="It's really interesting, because now ideas are really cheap. You can go from an idea to an actual working prototype in hours, or write a doc, or create a slide deck in minutes. That's actually really compelling. But when you go to share any sort of artifact, it goes back into these silo moments. So for us, and when we're thinking about our product process, we almost have to forget the process part. We have to create a system that really enables faster decision-making now that ideas are cheap. How do you take all of these different artifacts spatially and connect them together so people have the same context? Because that's what the game is about now: am I playing the same game as you are, and do I actually know where you started? Can I catch up to you as fast as you are, so we can just make decisions faster, because you have so many more decisions to make? Shared Context Is the New Bottleneck" aria-label="回原文"></button>

Elaina 认为现在的核心问题不是"怎么产生想法"，而是"怎么让所有人看到同一块画布上的信息，拥有相同的上下文，然后快速做决策"。工具栈里每个产品都在宣称自己是"工作发生的地方"——OKR 工具管计划，Slack 管即时沟通，Jira 或 Linear 管执行记录——但"决策是怎么做出的、为什么这样做"，这个空间仍然是空白的。会议纪要只能记录结论，记录不了决策的上下文。<button class="pd-ts" data-t="07:11" data-who="Elaina O'Mahoney" data-en="Yeah, it's an interesting concept, where every tool right now in the stack is almost converging in a circular area, and everybody is also converging on a chat-widget UI where work happens. How many different companies have the same tagline of &quot;where work happens&quot;? OKRs can be in a certain area, and it'll say what work is planned, and then you can measure against what's been planned. Slack is very good for communication in the moment. Then you'll have Notion, Confluence, Docs, Jira, or Linear, where you're still planning work or capturing decisions that have been made. The space that really is still ambiguous is how decisions are getting made and the context of why. That's a blank canvas area we think we're converging on. Yes, in meetings we talk about stuff, but the meeting notes come after; they capture a summary. You still don't have the context of why the decision was made. So how do you bring some of this disparate information together and really get to the why behind decision-making in the moment? When AI Is a Layer, Not a Button" aria-label="回原文"></button>

## 不写五页战略文档，直接做原型

Elaina 在 Mural 内部推了一个做法：产品领导者别再写那种四五页的战略文档了，写成文档传一圈、大家评论几句，然后就慢慢死掉了。更快的方式是你自己把设想做成一个视觉原型——不指望它直接上线，但它能传达你希望团队探索的原则和北极星方向。一个视觉原型比五页文字让团队更快进入构思和决策节奏。<button class="pd-ts" data-t="13:05" data-who="Elaina O'Mahoney" data-en="Yeah, it's also something we went through here at Mural, which hopefully is relevant to other product leaders and to engineering, design, and product teams. Usually a product leader would write this very, very big product strategy doc. Sometimes it's five pages, sometimes it's four pages. It makes the rounds, people comment, and maybe it starts to slowly die. What we can actually do faster as leaders is create a prototype of something we're envisioning. I'm not saying it will ship like this prototype, but it conveys some of the principles that you want the teams to explore, and a North Star direction. You as the product leader can do that so much faster. Or you as the PM, or even the engineer, could say, &quot;Hey, what about this concept and idea?&quot; And it actually moves the entire team faster in their ideation cycle, or their decision-making cycle, just with a visual." aria-label="回原文"></button>

她还主张"每个人都应该发布"。不一定是上线生产环境，可以在安全环境里发布工作流、发布[[智能体|智能体]]、发布研究工具。CPO 可以自己动手解决一些本来只有少数人能做的稀缺工作——比如数据分析综合——用这种方式让团队整体变快。<button class="pd-ts" data-t="11:33" data-who="Elaina O'Mahoney" data-en="Yeah, absolutely. I do think everybody should be playing and shipping now. Should everybody be shipping in production all the time? Maybe. The lens is where you can ship in safe environments. You can actually solve some of your core bottlenecks as the CPO, where you inherently would make decisions and choices. You can democratize some of that by just shipping workflows, shipping agents, shipping research agents. There are still some things that are quite scarce that you can work on internally. Data science is still scarce. Really good taste is still scarce. Synthesizing analytics is still scarce. So you can actually decide as a CPO where you play in that space to make your team's techniques faster." aria-label="回原文"></button>

## 大企业怎么落地：找"前置部署员工"

大企业不可能让所有人同时转型。Elaina 的经验是必须打破旧流程，但不能一开始就建新流程——流程会随着学习变化，所以要先建系统。

具体操作上，要找到那些已经自发用起 AI 工具、自动化了自己工作的人，把他们嵌入还没起步的团队。这个人不一定是工程师。<button class="pd-ts" data-t="17:19" data-who="Elaina O'Mahoney" data-en="I think forward deployed engineers are really, really helpful, because they're usually the ones who can use the tools that are best for the job today. It also gives people insight, as long as they're sharing, to say, &quot;Oh, this is how that person did it, this is how they automated that workflow, this is how they did the discovery, this is how they used Claude or Codex or your tool of choice. I now can do a bit of what they did.&quot; So whether it's a forward deployed engineer, or somebody in legal that's automated their process, it doesn't necessarily have to be an engineer. It can be somebody in any department who has already adapted to the tools, already automated some of the way they work, and you embed them into a different team or environment that hasn't yet." aria-label="回原文"></button>

她举了个例子：Mural 法务部有一个人，已经自动化了该部门 50% 的流程，产品和工程这边一直想把他挖过来，法务负责人说"别碰他"。这个人把他在 Mural 上做的工作分享给全公司，每个部门看了都有灵光乍现的时刻。这就是"[[前置部署员工|前置部署员工]]"的价值——不限于技术岗位。<button class="pd-ts" data-t="18:26" data-who="Elaina O'Mahoney" data-en="Yeah, I think it's about being able to create a forum for people to showcase their work. And I hate to keep saying Mural, but it's a collaborative tool; you can see what people are doing. I use the legal example because we have a person who sits in our legal department that we've been trying to steal to the product and engineering side for a while, and our head of legal is like, &quot;Don't touch him. He is so good here. He's automated 50% of our processes already.&quot; And when I think back, it's actually probably better that he's there automating a different part of our business than me stealing him for us. But he's able to showcase his work in Mural. He shares it with the entire organization, and it gives people these lightbulb moments in every department. Tapping the Community Without Chasing ROI" aria-label="回原文"></button>

## Token 黑市与定价困境

谈到定价，Elaina 说这是让她睡不好觉的事。Mural 不是 AI 原生公司，定价还带着传统 SaaS 按座位收费的模式。但按使用量收费也有问题：她发现大企业内部已经出现了"token 黑市"——员工自己的 token 额度用完了，去找同事"借"token 继续做原型，这激励了错误的行为。<button class="pd-ts" data-t="22:34" data-who="Elaina O'Mahoney" data-en="This is the thing that actually keeps me up at night. I wonder how many organizations that are, to use the very vibe name, &quot;AI-native.&quot; We weren't born when tokens and LLMs were there. So our pricing model reflects a lot of the seat-based pricing that everybody now attaches to legacy SaaS. But there's almost a double-edged sword with usage. When we speak to some of our larger customers, there's almost a black market for tokens. If they use their own tokens, they'll have to go to three other employees to say, &quot;I've got 80% of the way there with my prototype, and my tokens got cut off. Susie, can I siphon some of your tokens?&quot; And now it disincentivizes the wrong behavior. So it's a decision we're taking with the utmost care. It goes back to the fact that we serve totally different types of businesses, from a person who just bought us for themselves, for their three-person company or even their solo consulting gig, all the way up to a Fortune 500 company with 10,000 people. We have to think of pricing models throughout that same range, and maybe the pricing models are completely different. We know the value of our product in this very near future has to be filled with AI at its core, but we also don't want to disincentivize folks from using it and have to keep an accounting ledger of tokens. Making Sure Usage Drives the Right Outcomes" aria-label="回原文"></button>

更荒唐的是，有些公司下达"必须消耗 X 数量 token"的指令，结果员工像当年在 CRM 里虚报通话一样，为了凑 token 用量而用 AI。Elaina 的态度很直接：能用 AI 做不等于应该用 AI 做，用 AI 做拼写检查就是浪费钱。定价的核心要求是可预测性——成本不能月月大幅波动，否则企业没法投资、也没法激励正确的行为。<button class="pd-ts" data-t="24:40" data-who="Elaina O'Mahoney" data-en="I think the first thing comes with measurement, which was actually the first inclination of most organizations: &quot;Here's a tool, just use it.&quot; And usage showed adoption, and then everybody's like, &quot;Holy crap, this is very expensive. Pull back, pull back, pull back.&quot; So it's really about starting with a measurement layer, and then measuring how they're using it. Just because you can with AI, and I'm probably going to say the controversial thing, doesn't mean you need to do it with AI. Maybe spell check with AI is quite expensive. There are other things that can do spell check that don't have AI. So can we apply it to the right things? Can we at least measure our costs? Everything is going to come back to learning loops at the moment, that give us the flexibility of decision-making and don't go too far down the future casting of decision-making, because it's really hard to pull back pricing models. Once pricing models are in the air, people are really going to fight tooth and nail to not change them. And the last thing we're trying to make sure of is predictability. Any business has to understand its cost when things are so uncertain. So the cost of our tool can't wildly swing from month to month at all. There has to be predictability to it, so they can continue investing, so it can incentivize the right usage behavior, and people can get value out of it freely. How Mural Structures Its Product Teams" aria-label="回原文"></button>

## 未来赌注的管理：每月问"学到了什么"

Mural 把团队分成两类。核心产品团队用传统的"铁三角"（设计师、工程师、产品经理），因为服务银行金融这类客户不能随便试错。

新领域团队则用混合角色，可以非常快地发布到生产环境。Elaina 用城市来打比方：核心团队像城市规划师，改动一条高速公路都要深思熟虑；前沿团队像当年去西部的拓荒者，自己背包、自己找食物。<button class="pd-ts" data-t="27:42" data-who="Elaina O'Mahoney" data-en="Absolutely. I take it from the model of a city planner. You have a working city, so the way you change it has to be more thought through. If you're going to break down a freeway and put in a new one, or make a toll bridge, that's different from being a frontier team. The frontier team is back in the day before America was America, heading out West. You have to backpack, you have to kill your own food, you have to forage. The way those two teams work is completely, oppositely different, but they're serving the same business. Balancing Core Business Against Future Bets" aria-label="回原文"></button>

对于未来赌注的资源配置，她每月和 CTO 一起跟这些团队开会，但问的问题不是"你发布了什么"，而是"你学到了什么"。如果一个月后决策方式没变，说明没收集到新信息。她把这种模式形容为在一家十年以上的公司里同时跑三家迷你创业公司。<button class="pd-ts" data-t="30:11" data-who="Elaina O'Mahoney" data-en="Oh, that's probably once a month. On the future-bet casting, that feedback loop is once a month. Our CTO and myself are meeting with the teams to say, &quot;What did we learn?&quot; We're not just saying &quot;What did you ship?&quot; It's &quot;What did you learn?&quot; Because you need to change your decision-making faster. If we're not changing our decision-making, or we're still thinking the same way we did a month ago, we didn't gather the right information. It has to be something net new. So meeting with our future-bet teams once a month helps reinforce that feedback loop, and helps us decide, &quot;Oh, maybe we need more resources in this future bet, because they're onto something.&quot; It's almost like running three mini-startups inside a business that's been around for over ten years." aria-label="回原文"></button>

> 【背景】Mural 创始人名叫 Mariano，阿根廷人，最初是从游戏开发的协作体验中获得了创建 Mural 的灵感。

## 本集带走

- **瓶颈从创意变成了[[共享上下文|共享上下文]]**：AI 让产出想法的成本趋近于零，但团队决策仍然需要所有人看到同一块画布、理解同样的来龙去脉。
- **用视觉原型替代战略文档**：别写四五页文档传阅，直接把你的设想做成可看的原型，传达原则和方向，团队进入决策节奏更快。
- **找"前置部署员工"而非只找工程师**：任何部门里已经自发用 AI 自动化了工作流的人，都是最好的内部推广者，把他们嵌入未起步的团队。
- **别让 token 指标变成新的虚荣指标**：下达"必须消耗 X token"的指令，结果就是员工为凑数而用 AI，跟当年 CRM 虚报通话一样。
- **未来赌注每月复盘，问"学到了什么"**：不问"发布了什么"，问决策方式有没有因为新信息而改变，没有就说明没学到东西。
