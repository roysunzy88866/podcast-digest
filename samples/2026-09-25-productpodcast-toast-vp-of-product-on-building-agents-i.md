餐厅老板可能是最不该讨好 AI 的用户:利润薄如刀片、没空研究新工具、对「为了 AI 而 AI」零容忍。这一集的嘉宾 [[Maggie Crowley|Maggie Crowley]] 是 [[Toast|Toast]] 的产品副总裁——Toast 就是你去餐厅、咖啡店刷卡结账时碰到的那个销售点(POS)系统,背后还管着菜单、库存、排班、备餐。

她聊的是怎么把 AI 真正装进这套有 14 年历史、体量庞大的老平台,而不是从零起家。开场她甩出的最反直觉的发现是:最先爱上 AI 助手的,不是那些懂技术的早期采用者,而是**最不懂数据技术的运营者**——因为他们不用学软件,直接用自己的话问问题就行了 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="24:34" data-who="Maggie Crowley" data-en="Totally. Some of the really interesting magic when we first started is that it wasn't necessarily that you couldn't do this, or couldn't answer these questions if you wanted to in Toast, but maybe it just wasn't the easiest to do, or it wasn't intuitive to find. Toast has been around for 14 years. We have so much product, and a customer might not want to spend the time learning all of it. So this is just such a fast path to getting the answer. What was really interesting is that I also learned in the very early days that we had this assumption that only really tech-savvy early adopters would want to use this. But what happened was we found that some of the less tech-savvy operators loved it, because they didn't have to learn software. They just used their human language, and they loved it, because they could just ask questions without needing to feel like they had to learn a tool. I always thought that was an interesting learning from the early days. Okay, so what do we have here? It looks like the drivers I have are this prepaid dinner and my pepperoni flatbread. So I'm trying to remember: can you list out all the flatbreads on the menu? Because I'm thinking maybe I could juice my sales if I add to this menu category. A lot of what our customers are doing is trying to establish, day in and day out, how their menus might want to move, especially if there are changes in the cost of ingredients. One of the things we hear all the time is that the cost of ingredients and food is going up, and it's really difficult to keep raising your menu prices to match. So they're thinking about other ways they can construct a menu that's going to work, that's going to help their business be successful, and take into account that food costs are rising. So it looks like I've got eight flatbreads." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

## 把 AI 装进老平台,和 AI 原生完全是两种游戏

AI 原生产品没有「之前」可比,Toast 不是。Maggie 说这带来三层挑战 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="04:40" data-who="Maggie Crowley" data-en="There are a couple of challenges. One is that your customers, if you're lucky, already know what they use you for. They have really specific ways they've set up their workflows, whether that's just the muscle memory of how they use your tool, or specific workflows in the tool. They're used to doing that. So anything you want to do has to convince them to do it in a different way. There's a layer of behavior change you have to figure out. There's also the question of how much change your customers can absorb. Do they want to try something new? Is the thing your AI tool does actually germane to the outcome they're trying to get, and how do you make it useful for them? How do you make them want to use it? And then there's a scale question, which is that you can't necessarily launch something that doesn't work, isn't fast, isn't accurate, or isn't performant to your install base. Because then they're going to not want to use your product, and you're going to burn trust. If you're early, there's a bit of a sense that you can just try things and test them out. There are lots of things we're seeing come through a channel like X that are early, a little bit broken, maybe the integrations aren't great or the security isn't perfect, but that's okay, because we're all early adopters. When you're building AI in a platform like Toast, you have to think about the bar for quality and excellence being really high from the start. So you have to get creative about how you build in that environment. It's not that you can't build quickly and iteratively; it's just a different way of thinking about it. The Design-Partner Playbook" aria-label="回原文"></button>:一是客户已经用肌肉记忆形成了固定工作流,任何新东西都得说服他们换一种方式做;二是要掂量客户能吸收多少改变;三是最要命的质量门槛——**你不能向整个安装用户群发布一个不好用、不快、不准的东西,那样会烧掉信任**。X 上那种「有点破损但没关系,大家都是早期采用者」的宽容,在 Toast 这种平台里不存在,质量和卓越的门槛从一开始就得很高。

所以只能换一种构建方式,她的答案是一套「[[设计合作伙伴|设计伙伴]]手册」:2023 年底立项时,团队甚至不清楚最终用例是什么,只知道这技术要来了、想先试起来。他们找了十来个设计伙伴客户,直接把自己拉进和这些客户的 WhatsApp 群,只选移动端一个平台、只选报表一个用例,发布给这十个人,然后封闭起来以最快速度迭代,直到产品好到可以扩展 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="06:13" data-who="Maggie Crowley" data-en="The way we thought about it, in the very early days when we started working on this at the end of 2023, we didn't even really know exactly what the use case would end up being. But we knew this technology was coming, and we wanted to start trying. So we found just a handful of design partners, as we called them, and we put ourselves in WhatsApp groups with these customers. We started turning an assistant on. We picked one platform, so we started on mobile just to make it easy for ourselves, and we picked one use case around reporting. We shipped it to these ten people, and we walled ourselves off to iterate with them as fast as we could, to get to a point where the product was good enough that we could start to scale it. We've done that with every major feature we've released since then. We did that for honestly about a year. Then the models finally got good enough in early 2025, and we made it generally available last fall, in October, and have built a lot into it since. But it was really that process: finding a couple of customers, building really quickly and iteratively with them, being in a channel where you could get fast feedback, and then scaling from there. That's how we figured out how to operate. Probably the other thing that's unique about building in this space is that you have to bring the AI to your customer. We couldn't assume they'd discover it on their own. Just adding a chat box somewhere isn't enough. You have to bring it to them. You have to get them over the blank-page problem. You have to help them understand when to use it. And an enduring challenge of these tools is that even if you get one of your users to use it, you're probably adding all sorts of capabilities in the background, and there's no way for them to see that, because it's all behind the scenes. So you have to constantly think about how you get them to use it, and then how you help them understand everything you're adding. You have to constantly re-onboard your users, because it's constantly changing. It's an interesting way to think about a product. Maybe before, you could launch something and assume that once your user figured it out and used it once, they understood how it worked. That's not the case with AI. You have to build a better muscle around bringing the product to them, helping them understand what it does, and then re-engaging them when you have new things. Re-Onboarding Users Who Have Other Jobs to Do" aria-label="回原文"></button>。

这套流程老实说跑了一年,到 2025 年初模型终于够好了,去年十月正式发布,之后每个大功能都照此办理。方法核心就三件事:找几个客户、和他们极快地迭代、待在一个能拿到快速反馈的渠道里,然后再扩展。

## 你必须把 AI 带到客户面前,还得不断让他们重新上手

在这个领域构建的另一条独特规矩:不能假设客户会自己发现 AI。只加一个聊天框是不够的——你得帮他们跨过「空白页问题」,帮他们理解什么时候该用。

更持久的难题是,这些工具的能力一直在后台悄悄增加,用户根本看不见。Maggie 的结论是:**AI 产品必须不断让用户「[[重新上手|重新上手]]」,因为产品在不断变化**。以前可以发布一次、用户用懂一次就完事,AI 时代不行,你得建一套「把产品带给用户、讲清楚它做什么、有新东西时再激活他们」的肌肉 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="06:13" data-who="Maggie Crowley" data-en="The way we thought about it, in the very early days when we started working on this at the end of 2023, we didn't even really know exactly what the use case would end up being. But we knew this technology was coming, and we wanted to start trying. So we found just a handful of design partners, as we called them, and we put ourselves in WhatsApp groups with these customers. We started turning an assistant on. We picked one platform, so we started on mobile just to make it easy for ourselves, and we picked one use case around reporting. We shipped it to these ten people, and we walled ourselves off to iterate with them as fast as we could, to get to a point where the product was good enough that we could start to scale it. We've done that with every major feature we've released since then. We did that for honestly about a year. Then the models finally got good enough in early 2025, and we made it generally available last fall, in October, and have built a lot into it since. But it was really that process: finding a couple of customers, building really quickly and iteratively with them, being in a channel where you could get fast feedback, and then scaling from there. That's how we figured out how to operate. Probably the other thing that's unique about building in this space is that you have to bring the AI to your customer. We couldn't assume they'd discover it on their own. Just adding a chat box somewhere isn't enough. You have to bring it to them. You have to get them over the blank-page problem. You have to help them understand when to use it. And an enduring challenge of these tools is that even if you get one of your users to use it, you're probably adding all sorts of capabilities in the background, and there's no way for them to see that, because it's all behind the scenes. So you have to constantly think about how you get them to use it, and then how you help them understand everything you're adding. You have to constantly re-onboard your users, because it's constantly changing. It's an interesting way to think about a product. Maybe before, you could launch something and assume that once your user figured it out and used it once, they understood how it worked. That's not the case with AI. You have to build a better muscle around bringing the product to them, helping them understand what it does, and then re-engaging them when you have new things. Re-Onboarding Users Who Have Other Jobs to Do" aria-label="回原文"></button>。

具体做法上,Toast 的助手叫 [[Toast IQ|Toast IQ]],它的入口遍布产品各处:预写好、且经过验证效果好的提示词(用户大概率会问的问题),再按移动端/桌面端、一天中的时段、用户在店里的位置和角色来定制起始提示词。 Maggie 说你得真正理解用户「物理上」在哪——是不是在班次中、在店里哪个位置、什么角色——才能在对的时刻把用例递到他们面前 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="08:55" data-who="Maggie Crowley" data-en="If I had totally cracked the onboarding problem for an AI product, maybe I'd have my own startup, I don't know. The way we think about it: the more you understand your customer, your user, the way they're working, and what they're up to, the better you're going to be at figuring out when they should use the thing you're building. And that's the same as any product you'd build. But you have to really understand, for us physically, where they are in the store or the restaurant. What time of day is it? Is it during a shift or not? What role do they have in the restaurant or retail shop, and where are they? And then, what might they need to do? And how do you close that gap and bring them whatever the use case is at that moment? So practically speaking, we of course have this assistant, Toast IQ, in our product. But it's entry points throughout the product. It's pre-written prompts that we know work really well, that we know are questions they're going to ask. And it's making sure it works really well in our mobile app as well as our desktop, and that we tailor the starter prompts based on what they're doing on those platforms at different times of day. So it's about getting really specific about all of those things and trying to make it really easy for them to get started. Once they get in and start asking their questions, then it's relatively easy; you just have to figure out how to get them in to start. From &quot;Help Me Do This&quot; to &quot;Do This for Me&quot;" aria-label="回原文"></button>。

## 从「帮我做」到「替我做」,中间有一个时钟

主持人 Carlos 提出一个观察:用户对 AI 的期望正从「帮我做这件事」变成「替我做这件事」。Maggie 完全认同,而且她说这里面几乎有一个时钟:消费市场一旦出现某种交互模式,距离它变成用户的默认期望就只剩一段时间——**在用户默认「你的工具也该这么干活」之前,就是你要做完这件事的窗口期** <button class="pd-ts" data-t="12:25" data-who="Maggie Crowley" data-en="Absolutely. And there's almost a clock, because the second you start to see interactions like that in the consumer market, there's a certain amount of time until those patterns become standard. And then, if you have an AI product, your users expect that. So I also see it as: okay, we're seeing these patterns happen now, how long do I have before my users assume that's how my tool will work? I have that amount of time to make sure I can do whatever those things do. We have to stay relevant enough in the space. It's fun, because to me it means you almost have no idea where your product is going to go, and you have to figure out how to be more flexible, because you don't really know what the patterns are going to be, in terms of the interaction patterns, but also in terms of what the models can do. Before AI, building was almost getting boring, relatively. Now it's much more dynamic and interesting, because we have no idea what's coming." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

这也让构建变得前所未有的不确定:你几乎不知道产品会走向哪里,交互模式和模型能力都在变,必须保持灵活。「AI 之前,构建相对来说几乎变得无聊了;现在完全不知道会来什么」。

## 竞争格局不重要,理解用户才重要

聊到 Square、DoorDash、OpenTable 这些公司, Maggie 的态度是:没人知道市场会怎么落地,与其被竞争动态牵着走,不如盯住一件事——**最终赢的公司,是为自己的市场用最好的方式解决正确问题、并深刻理解它的那一家**。去客户的地方、看他们干活、成为他们能打电话的那个人,你为用户做得越多,无论市场怎么变你都处于有利位置 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="13:55" data-who="Maggie Crowley" data-en="It's a good question, and everybody is trying to figure out how the market is going to play out. I don't know that anybody knows how we're all going to interact and how it's all going to land. The way I think about this problem is: how do we make sure we're keeping our customers at the heart of what we're doing? It's so easy to get distracted by the competitive dynamics, the market dynamics, who's going to be the winner, whether there's going to be a winner, how it's all going to play out. It's interesting to game that out from a strategic standpoint. But at the end of the day, the company that wins, if there is such a thing, is the one that solves the right problem in the best way for their market and deeply understands it. So now, even more than before, the most important thing is to really understand the user and what they're up to. Be with them physically, go to their places, watch them do their work, be the person they can call. The more you do that, the better positioned you'll be for whatever the future holds. In the same way that three weeks ago we'd never heard of some of these consumer AI tools and now they're all we can talk about, who knows what new entrants will happen or what other companies will do? If you try to anchor too much on gaming that out, you're going to miss the point of right now, which is: build for your user and do everything you can for them, because that's what's going to make you successful no matter what happens in the market. Growth Levers as a Public Company" aria-label="回原文"></button>。增长逻辑同样朴素:深刻理解客户需要什么、解决真实问题、打造他们愿意付费的东西——尤其餐厅利润这么薄,你必须看懂一家餐厅的损益表,才知道你要收的每一笔额外的钱从哪来、值不值得他们掏。

## 最佳甜蜜点:替客户干他们本来要花钱外包的活

Toast IQ 定位正在从「我们管理你的销售点」走向「我们帮你的餐厅增长」。突破性用例是营销[[智能体|智能体]] [[Toast IQ Grow|Toast IQ Grow]]——目标是「让顾客坐进座位」。

有个客户用它把营销代理支出砍掉了超过 70%,还能把具体金额的销售归因到营销活动上 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="17:05" data-who="Maggie Crowley" data-en="The marketing one is really interesting. That product has been so successful. What's interesting is that there's one customer who has reduced their marketing agency spend by over 70%, and can tie marketing to sales and attribute a specific amount of sales to marketing since they launched it via this product we built. We have examples like that showing you can use AI to dramatically reduce the cost of doing something, but also take off work you're not willing to do yourself, or don't want to do yourself. A lot of the sweet spot for us is that a marketer or a restaurateur isn't necessarily someone who loves doing email marketing or online marketing or building a website. So they pay someone to do it, and that's really expensive, and it's a low-margin business. ## 本集带走

- **老平台发 AI,门槛是信任,不是速度**:X 上「有点破损没关系」的宽容不存在;不好用、不快、不准的东西一旦推给全体安装用户,烧掉的是信任。先在封闭小圈子里磨到够好再扩展。
- **设计伙伴手册**:十来个客户 + 一个 WhatsApp 群 + 只选一个平台一个用例,极快迭代约一年,模型成熟后一次推全量——之后每个大功能照此办理。
- **AI 产品要不断让用户重新上手**:能力在后台持续增加、用户看不见,别指望「教会一次管一辈子」,要持续把新能力主动带到用户面前。
- **最不懂数据技术的用户可能最爱它**:不用学软件、用自己的话提问就行——别只按「技术早期采用者」画像做 AI 采用策略。
- **找对甜蜜点:替客户干他们本来要花钱外包的活**:有客户用 Toast IQ Grow 把营销代理支出砍掉超 70%——AI 的价值叙事要落在省下的真金白银上。
- **给管理者的:保住一线手感**:顶 PM 的空缺、直接带一线团队,否则两年内你会看不懂团队的构建方式。
- **消费级模式是一张倒计时表**:消费市场出现的交互模式,会在一段时间内变成你 B2B 用户的默认期望——那就是你的准备窗口。

  function fmt(s){
