这一集是 [[a16z|a16z]] 的 [[Martin Casado|Martin Casado]]、[[Sarah Wang|Sarah Wang]] 和 [[Matt Bornstein|Matt Bornstein]] 三位投资人复盘他们投资 [[Cursor|Cursor]] 的全过程，从最早的 A 轮一直到后续多轮。Cursor 是一个 AI 编程编辑器，创始人是 Michael 和 Aman。

2024 年初，AI 编程的赛道看起来几乎被 [[Microsoft|Microsoft]] 锁死了——[[Copilot|Copilot]] 遥遥领先，Microsoft 手里握着 GitHub、VS Code、Office，还能拿到 [[OpenAI|OpenAI]] 的[[模型|模型]]权重。当时市面上做 AI 编程的公司大致分两派：一派认为必须训练编码专用基础模型，另一派选择给现有 [[IDE|IDE]] 做[[插件|插件]]。

Cursor 两条路都没走，选了最激进的那个——直接 fork VS Code，做一个独立的编辑器产品。这个决定在当时看起来「几乎是不理性的」<button class="pd-ts" data-t="00:51" data-who="Unknown" data-en="You really couldn't do the flip of that unless you started as a frontier-led. Two years ago, betting on an independent AI coding company looked almost irrational." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

但他们有一套非常自洽的逻辑。为什么不训练编码专用模型？

因为「你不是用代码跟这些模型说话，你是用自然语言跟它们说话，所以模型必须理解人类经验和语言的广度——这意味着你要建一个编码模型，实际上就是在建一个前沿语言模型，那太难了」<button class="pd-ts" data-t="06:54" data-who="Unknown" data-en="So, for example, why wouldn't you build a coding-specific foundation model? So what they said at the time was, it turns out that you don't speak to these models in code." aria-label="回原文"></button>。为什么不做插件？

「如果你真的相信产品，你永远不会做插件，因为那样你就成了别人产品的一部分」<button class="pd-ts" data-t="07:37" data-who="Unknown" data-en="And that dictated a lot of their decisions. If you really believe in the product, you would never do a plugin because then you're part of somebody else's product." aria-label="回原文"></button>。为什么不做企业销售？「如果你真的相信产品，你不会很早做企业业务，产品本身就是一种推向市场的动作」<button class="pd-ts" data-t="07:41" data-who="Unknown" data-en="If you really believe in the product, you would never do a plugin because then you're part of somebody else's product. If you really believe in the product, you wouldn't do enterprise early, you'd do it later because the product itself is to kind of go to market motion." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

他们还从一个很早就形成的信念出发：「未来的代码看起来会像伪代码」<button class="pd-ts" data-t="04:33" data-who="Unknown" data-en="And this is something that I remember Michael and Aman saying a lot and saying, just saying very clearly from the earliest days of Cursor saying, Code in the future will look like pseudocode." aria-label="回原文"></button>——程序员只需要用类自然语言写出意图的最小规范，模型来填充实现。这意味着人机之间的界面才是关键，不是模型本身。「我们现在不需要在模型上与 [[Anthropic|Anthropic]] 和 OpenAI 竞争，人类与模型之间的界面才是关键所在」<button class="pd-ts" data-t="04:17" data-who="Unknown" data-en="Of course, that changed later for different reasons, which we're going to get into later in this podcast. But it's like, we don't need to compete with Anthropic and OpenAI on models right now." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

## 极度专注：90% 的会议在说「不」

a16z 团队让 Michael 来给他们的 GP 团队做推介，结果「90% 的会议时间他都在对各种事情说不」<button class="pd-ts" data-t="05:35" data-who="Unknown" data-en="They had an unusual degree of focus. I remember we got Michael to come pitch our GP group, and 90% of the meeting was him saying no to things. Which is just like, for founders out there working on products that you love, like..." aria-label="回原文"></button>。VC 们照例会问「你们会不会做这个、会不会做那个」，Michael 礼貌听完，回答就一句：「不，我们不会做那个。

」<button class="pd-ts" data-t="05:57" data-who="Unknown" data-en="And Michael literally just sat there and like very politely listened to all the questions and said, hmm, interesting. No, we're not going to do that. Wait, wait." aria-label="回原文"></button> 这种专注不是嘴上说说。Matt 问 Michael 工作之外做什么消遣，「他几乎没听懂这个问题」<button class="pd-ts" data-t="08:40" data-who="Unknown" data-en="I remember when we were trying to convince the Cursor team to let us invest in their company, I asked Michael once what he did for fun outside of work. And it was almost like he didn't understand the question." aria-label="回原文"></button>——编程本身既是工作也是乐趣。

## 竞争反应：偏执但不被吓倒

这个赛道竞争从未停过。先是 Copilot，然后 Windsurf 在 YC 圈子里火了一波，Cognition 用[[智能体|智能体]]（能自主执行多步任务的 AI 程序）掀起波澜，后来 [[Claude Code|Claude Code]] 在 2025 年 5 月又强势入场。

Cursor 团队的反应是「偏执但不被吓倒」<button class="pd-ts" data-t="16:38" data-who="Unknown" data-en="They were 100% unfazed by the competition. Yeah, paranoid but unfazed. I'll never forget, you know, and we've been lucky to do, I think, all of their rounds starting from the A." aria-label="回原文"></button>。Claude Code 发布三个月后，Martin 问 Michael 怎么看，Michael 的回答是：「我们在追求世界上最大的市场，总会有竞争对手，从一开始就有一轮轮的角色在轮换，那不吓倒我们」<button class="pd-ts" data-t="17:35" data-who="Unknown" data-en="And I remember there was around, I think, a couple months after, three months after that, and I asked Michael, I was like, what do you think about Claude Code? And he said something to the extent, actually, you know, exactly what you're saying here, which is..." aria-label="回原文"></button>。Martin 说那种态度是「谦逊与自信的混合，几乎是一种贝索斯式的东西」<button class="pd-ts" data-t="18:13" data-who="Unknown" data-en="Yeah, somewhat blown away by the clarity of that comment, the lack of fear, right? There's humility as well, like it's humility mixed with bravado. And I just think that is kind of the winning combo when you're going after these juggernauts like that." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

真正让他们紧张的不是竞品，而是模型本身的能力跃升。「绝对有那么几个该死的时刻，但总是围绕着模型——比如 Opus 4.5 出来的时候」<button class="pd-ts" data-t="18:36" data-who="Unknown" data-en="That I'll say. I will say they were definitely a couple of oh fuck moments, but they were always around models. Like, remember when Opus 4.5 came out?" aria-label="回原文"></button>。

## 两年内三次自我颠覆

Cursor 的产品经历了剧烈的演变：先做 IDE 编辑器，然后转向智能体平台，再转向模型平台。这种自我蚕食的节奏，被比喻为里德·哈斯廷斯（Netflix 创始人）式的做法——在两年内完成<button class="pd-ts" data-t="19:04" data-who="Unknown" data-en="These models were getting so good so fast. And I think another thing that the Cursor team probably doesn't get, you know, the level of credit for is to the extent they cannibalized themselves so dramatically in like a Reed Hastings type way in a matter of two years, right?" aria-label="回原文"></button>。最初让他们出圈的是 Tab 补全功能（Karpathy 有个著名帖子就是反复按 Tab），但现在「那种事情不再那么重要了」<button class="pd-ts" data-t="19:38" data-who="Unknown" data-en="Yeah, exactly. Like, that kind of doesn't matter that much anymore. Yeah." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

## 从拒绝销售到史上最快销售团队扩张

早期 Martin 多次跟 Michael 说「你最好投资销售」，Michael 的回答是「不」<button class="pd-ts" data-t="22:16" data-who="Unknown" data-en="Because, you know, early on, I think I told Michael a few times, too, it's like, oh, you better invest in sales. He's like, nope, I'm not going to do that. They literally set up enterprise at Cursor.com." aria-label="回原文"></button>。他们在 Cursor.com 上设了一个企业咨询入口，仅此而已。但当他们决定认真做企业业务时，逻辑很清楚：大模型公司靠企业端赚高利润率来补贴个人用户，Cursor 要长期存活也必须拿下企业这一块——「这是存在级别的问题」<button class="pd-ts" data-t="23:12" data-who="Unknown" data-en="The margin tends to be the value here. We've created a great, you know, top-of-funnel engine, and so, you know, this is existential and it's important. And then when they decided to do it, they really went in." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

然后他们以一种极端的方式切入。不是等简历上门，而是像做产品研究一样做招聘研究：先找出在此类销售上表现最好的 10 家公司，再找出这些公司里最顶尖的团队，再定位到排名第一第二的个人客户经理，然后通过大量私下渠道交叉验证「这个人是不是真正的超级明星」<button class="pd-ts" data-t="26:11" data-who="Unknown" data-en="Epochs of a person's career or of a company and who was the key dial in making that happen. I love this intense research period that goes on where it's like, hey, these are the top 10 companies who have seen great in this type of selling." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

技术创始人花 40% 的时间在招聘上<button class="pd-ts" data-t="24:15" data-who="Unknown" data-en="This is amazing. For technical product founders to spend 40% of their time recruiting. I've never actually seen it before." aria-label="回原文"></button>，有人坐红眼航班去欧洲只为关闭一个候选人。结果是「他们做到了财富 500 强企业的 50% 以上，而且比我们见过的任何人都快」<button class="pd-ts" data-t="25:33" data-who="Unknown" data-en="All of those things that have been written a bit on the end side, they did that on the go-to-market side and clearly to phenomenal results. I mean, they got to over 50% of the Fortune 500 and I think faster than anyone we've ever seen." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

## 品味与决断力：未被充分讨论的最大优势

讨论里反复出现一个词：品味（taste）。不只是产品品味，还延伸到办公空间——斯堪的纳维亚风格、进办公室换拖鞋、二手家具但追求特定的氛围<button class="pd-ts" data-t="31:26" data-who="Unknown" data-en="But it extended to how not only they did hiring on the end, go-to-market side, but even the space they created, the events they throw. Like, I remember walking into their office and thinking, wow, in my early 20s, I would not have paid attention to putting, I don't know, twinkle lights here." aria-label="回原文"></button>。营销上他们也极有主见，拒绝「垃圾信息式」的推广，很多活动建议被直接否掉<button class="pd-ts" data-t="32:28" data-who="Unknown" data-en="So marketing was a very obvious one. They were very, very particular. Like, we use X." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

但更硬核的优势是决断力。「一旦他们做了决定，不管多难——不管是不进某个市场、还是在做的事情上非常克制、还是谁留谁走——他们都会贯彻到底」<button class="pd-ts" data-t="32:50" data-who="Unknown" data-en="And they were very consistent about that. And so I would say it's almost this kind of untalked about probably greatest strength of the company is that once they made a decision, it doesn't matter how hard that was, whether it's deciding not to enter a market, deciding to like be very limited in what they're doing, like they kind of follow through or like, you know, who would stay in the company, who wouldn't stay in the company." aria-label="回原文"></button>。不合适的人，很快做艰难决定，这在早期创始团队里非常罕见<button class="pd-ts" data-t="32:42" data-who="Unknown" data-en="I will say that they were also very good about this when it came to personnel. Like, if there wasn't a fit, they'd make the hard decision pretty quickly. You very, very rarely see this with early founding teams." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

## 并购：先抢人，后布局战略

人才争夺战太激烈，Cursor 很早就通过小规模并购来获取优秀团队。「他们从不担心运营复杂性——无论多混乱都会去做，也不太担心文化融合问题，因为他们在这方面本身就很强」<button class="pd-ts" data-t="35:56" data-who="Unknown" data-en="The early motions that they did were of that nature, which is like really good team, you know, they'll just go ahead and do the acquisition. And what was really nice about Cursor, they were just never worried about like operational complexity." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

后期像 Graphite 收购案才开始从战略方向角度考虑<button class="pd-ts" data-t="36:14" data-who="Unknown" data-en="It wasn't until later... Like the Graphite acquisition where they started thinking about acquisitions in terms of strategic direction. And of course, that was a much larger acquisition." aria-label="回原文"></button>。他们把创始人级别的人招进来，不只是塞进独立贡献者角色，而是真正用这种方式构建公司——「在运营上极其复杂，但他们做到了」<button class="pd-ts" data-t="38:03" data-who="Unknown" data-en="Like, they're actually building the company this way, which I thought, it's very unconventional, but, like, really... It's actually operationally incredibly complex." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

## 本集带走

- **不跟巨头拼模型，拼界面**：编码模型的输入是自然语言不是代码，所以建编码模型等于建前沿语言模型，太难且不必要；人机交互界面才是差异化所在。
- **独立产品 > 插件**：做插件等于成为别人产品的一部分，丧失控制权；哪怕 fork 巨头的 IDE 也要做独立产品。
- **产品好就不需要早做销售**：让产品本身成为获客引擎，等到自助增长开始触顶再建销售团队——而且一旦决定做，就用做产品研究的方法论去找最顶尖的人。
- **对竞争偏执但不恐惧**：大市场永远有轮换的竞争对手，关键是不被吓倒、保持专注。
- **快速自我颠覆**：从 IDE 到智能体平台到模型平台，两年内主动蚕食自己的核心产品，这需要极强的纪律性。
- **决断力是隐藏优势**：对不合适的人快速切割，对不符合品味的营销方案坚决说不——一旦决定就贯彻到底。

  function fmt(s){
    if(!isFinite(s)||s<0) s=0;

        bar=box.querySelector('.bar'), fill=box.querySelector('.bar > i'),
