52岁进了家科技公司，平均年龄26岁，你指导的老板比你小21岁——[[Chip Connolly|Chip Connolly]] 做到了，但他第一件事不是摆资历，而是让自己当"房间里最笨的人" <button class="pd-ts" data-t="06:24" data-who="Chip Connolly" data-en="see whether the younger crowd there... I was 52, the average age was 26," aria-label="回原文"></button>。他去 [[Airbnb|Airbnb]] 时不懂任何技术术语，连"产品"这个词的意思都跟团队拧着：他觉得产品就是那些房子和公寓，首席产品官 Jobot 告诉他，科技行业里"产品"是另一回事 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="08:12" data-who="Chip Connolly" data-en="you know, I'm the chief product officer. And I'm like, well, isn't the product the homes and the apartments?" aria-label="回原文"></button>。他能待下去、甚至干出价值，靠的不是经验碾压，是一套具体的生存策略。

## 给创始人干活的三件难事，以及怎么扛

给 Brian Chesky 干活，Chip 总结了三件事 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="13:44" data-who="Chip Connolly" data-en="So it was sort of a growth mindset. What was hard with Brian is, I'd say three things. Number one is Brian assumed everybody else was going to work at the same pace and duration." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

第一，Brian 假定所有人都跟他一个节奏和时长。"他的观点是，嘿，我们今晚10点在办公室开会，到场就好。" <button class="pd-ts" data-t="14:16" data-who="Chip Connolly" data-en="his point of view was like, hey, we're having a meeting in the office tonight at 10 o'clock," aria-label="回原文"></button> Brian 后来在播客里自己承认过工作狂的问题，但在当时，这对一个52岁、兴趣广泛的人来说是真实的负担。

第二，Brian 太崇拜 Steve Jobs，作为罗德岛设计学院出来的产品人，他觉得自己比谁都懂。走进一个房间，指出问题——这个过程让人"感觉被需要"，但如果没有情商，会让人恼火、让人失去动力 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="15:14" data-who="Chip Connolly" data-en="my boutique hotel company, is it feels good when you feel needed. And to come into a room and sort of see something and then point out the things" aria-label="回原文"></button>。

Chip 相对幸运，因为他负责房东，Brian 对酒店业确实不熟。他去20个城市走访了房东的家，回来后有了可信度："我们的数据科学团队在获取一些东西，但我实际上进入了这些世界各地的房东家中。" <button class="pd-ts" data-t="16:08" data-who="Chip Connolly" data-en="Folks who are doing qualitative interviews, they're getting something out of this. But I actually went into the homes of these hosts all around the world. And I think I was lucky because Brian did less of that than he did with other people." aria-label="回原文"></button> 但产品团队就没这么幸运了——跟 Brian 开产品会，前一天晚上睡不着，不光因为要通宵准备，还因为紧张到根本睡不着我 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="16:21" data-who="Chip Connolly" data-en="I mean, a product meeting with Brian would keep people up the night before, not just because they were actually working all night long to get prepared," aria-label="回原文"></button>。

第三，Brian 喜欢给目标"加个零"——设定不合理的截止日期。逻辑是：如果不这么干，潜台词就是"人们不会自己发奋"。问题是，当你没达到目标，而设定目标的人手里有权，你就是在给人施加巨大压力 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="17:05" data-who="Chip Connolly" data-en="that adding a zero to something in terms of expectations or thinking you're going to set a deadline that is unreasonable is necessary because if you don't do that there's an almost an underlying message" aria-label="回原文"></button>。

Chip 的应对方法很具体——**会前对齐**。开会前先跟团队说好："Brian，我们聊聊要完成什么。

这个产品迭代的意图是什么，什么算成功，这次会要达成什么？"如果对不齐，"你不如不开这个会" <button class="pd-ts" data-t="19:05" data-who="Chip Connolly" data-en="I want you to start the meeting with the following as you present, and Brian's in the room. So Brian, let's talk about what we're trying to accomplish here. Let's get really clear," aria-label="回原文"></button>。

这样 Brian 中途挑刺时，你可以反复拉回这三个原则。另外，**别依赖 PPT**——遇到容易跑偏的创始人，你的幻灯片按原顺序可能完全讲不通，所以把 deck 压到最少，只在开头用来立原则、定目标 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="20:39" data-who="Chip Connolly" data-en="It just... Especially when you have a combustible founder who may take you off path such that your deck in its current order makes no sense at all." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

## 年长大脑 vs 年轻大脑：不是谁更强，是能互补

Chip 写了《工作中的智慧》这本书，做了大量脑科学研究访谈。结论很清晰：年轻大脑有**[[流体智力|流体智力]]**——快速、专注、擅长线性思考和解决问题；年长大脑有**[[晶体智力|晶体智力]]**——专注度降一点，但整体思维、系统思维、连点成线的能力更强 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="21:48" data-who="Chip Connolly" data-en="neuroscientists, and realized that a younger brain has fluid intelligence, tends to be fast and focused," aria-label="回原文"></button>。

一个团队里，年长者在外围广泛地想，年轻人快速聚焦地干——"这种组合成功时，简直精彩" <button class="pd-ts" data-t="22:20" data-who="Chip Connolly" data-en="So you can imagine that on a team, when you have older brains connecting the dots, thinking broadly," aria-label="回原文"></button>。他跟 Laura Hughes（当时是他的酒店总监）合作就这种感觉：她的大脑跟他的运作方式不同，这种差异本身是机会 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="22:48" data-who="Chip Connolly" data-en="but also we worked in so many different capacities with her in the company. I loved working with her because her brain worked different than my brain. And that's the opportunity," aria-label="回原文"></button>。

他还有个具体贡献：**发现盲点**。他很早就跟 Brian 说，我们现在做的所有事本质上是"不想被监管、不想交入住税"——酒店要交床位税，Airbnb 不交，这让平台显得更便宜。

但"如果我们三年后做到那个规模，我向你保证我们会被监管。让我们现在就主动建策略。

" <button class="pd-ts" data-t="24:09" data-who="Chip Connolly" data-en="But it made us more affordable by not having to have our guests pay taxes. Long story short is I said to Brian, if we're as big as we're gonna be three years from now," aria-label="回原文"></button> 这后来一直是 Airbnb 最大的挑战。如果早几年开始，在纽约等市场可能不会走到有毒的地步 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="24:26" data-who="Chip Connolly" data-en="So let's take some proactive steps toward building a strategy for how we're gonna be regulated. And that has consistently been Airbnb's biggest challenge, is regulation in municipal markets all around the world." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

他管这叫**[[隐形生产力|隐形生产力]]**——不是你自己解决问题，而是让周围所有人都变得更好 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="25:51" data-who="Chip Connolly" data-en="I call this invisible productivity. It's productivity in which you make everybody else around you better. And that's something I tried to do with my teams at Airbnb." aria-label="回原文"></button>。比如工程师 John Q. Smith，不是最好的码农，但成了伟大的管理者——知道怎么激发那些技术上比自己强的人 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="25:17" data-who="Chip Connolly" data-en="And he was a little bit nervous about telling people his age. But the thing that was great about John is over time, he was not necessarily going to be the best coder at Airbnb." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

## 年长者在科技界怎么不被淘汰

年龄歧视在科技行业是真实存在的，但比12年前好一点——至少现在有"Airbnb 智慧"这种40岁以上员工资源组了 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="27:31" data-who="Chip Connolly" data-en="Yeah, you know, it's clearly a problem. I'd say it's maybe a little bit less of a problem than it was a dozen years ago. Because I think a dozen years ago, it was almost a blind spot." aria-label="回原文"></button>。核心障碍没变：年长者既贵，又被认为慢 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="28:30" data-who="Chip Connolly" data-en="are coming in with a whole new set of skills that an older person doesn't have, the older person is both expensive and may be perceived as slow. So..." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

Chip 给年长者的建议不是"证明你值这个价"，而是**带着年轻人的精力出现**。精力有两部分：一是身体上的投入——他能每周工作60到70小时、满世界跑，Brian 在台上叫他"公司的国务卿"，没人会说"把这个老顽固踢走" <button class="pd-ts" data-t="33:09" data-who="Chip Connolly" data-en="yeah, I could work 60 and 70 hours a week and I could travel around the world as the secretary of state of the company," aria-label="回原文"></button>。

二是**积极正面的能量**——人们不一定会盯着你的皱纹，但会注意到你的精力 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="32:04" data-who="Chip Connolly" data-en="You have physical energy in how you do your job. And when people are like that, they are sort of timeless. They're age fluid, as I say." aria-label="回原文"></button>。他管这叫"年龄流动"——不被年龄定义，像人们说性别流动一样 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="32:06" data-who="Chip Connolly" data-en="And when people are like that, they are sort of timeless. They're age fluid, as I say. We talk about gender fluidity." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

还有一个务实建议：**愿意降薪换时间**。他在 Airbnb 第四年主动砍到40%或50%时间，薪水、期权同步下调 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="29:43" data-who="Chip Connolly" data-en="are you open as I ultimately was with Brian in my fourth year at Airbnb? I took a substantial pay cut. I think it went down to 40% or 50% time." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

很多公司里"怎么把事办成"是最大挑战——年长者懂组织架构、懂不同团队的动机，这种流程知识是随时间建起来的 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="30:10" data-who="Chip Connolly" data-en="And in tech companies, that's really important. Airbnb, one of the biggest challenges at Airbnb has always been," aria-label="回原文"></button>。如果你愿意接受20%到40%的降薪换80%或60%的工作时间，公司会觉得物超所值 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="30:39" data-who="Chip Connolly" data-en="Older people might look at their workload and say, I'm willing to take a 20% or a 40% pay cut to go to 80% or 60% time. And the company is going to get their money's worth in that." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

对招聘者的建议：AI 时代，**通才比专才更重要**。David Epstein 的《Range》说的就是这个——我们正从专家时代进入通才时代，AI 在加速这个过程 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="35:39" data-who="Chip Connolly" data-en="them find these people? I think we're moving into, there's a book that David Epstein wrote called Range. And the whole premise of Range is that we are moving out of the era of the" aria-label="回原文"></button>。

招人时除了看热情和好奇心，还要问：这个人解决问题时是不是通才型的思考方式？ <button class="pd-ts" data-t="36:30" data-who="Chip Connolly" data-en="But I would also say... Are they a generalist when they're a problem solver? Because I actually think that's going to be an increasingly important part of how" aria-label="回原文"></button> 另外，建立**互为指导**的关系——年长者教你怎么开会、怎么做员工回顾，年轻人教你用 iPhone、用 Google Doc，像学徒制一样在现场学，不用去参加培训课 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="36:54" data-who="Chip Connolly" data-en="intergenerational collaboration in the form of mutual mentorships. One of the things I loved at Airbnb, there were a few people I did this with where I had something to teach them and" aria-label="回原文"></button>。

简历怎么写？别列头衔和要点，**用一段话讲一个棘手问题**：问题是什么、你用了什么技能、结果怎样。

你越年长，越能写出这样的段落 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="42:09" data-who="Chip Connolly" data-en="Give me in a paragraph. a thorny problem you faced. What was the problem and what skills you used to actually accomplish it and what" aria-label="回原文"></button>。面试时问对方：定义你们文化的三到五个形容词是什么？

这个文化里最大的根深蒂固的问题是什么？问多个人，看答案一不一致 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="52:01" data-who="Chip Connolly" data-en="be, what are three to five adjectives that define this culture? What's the biggest problem in this culture in terms of something that's just" aria-label="回原文"></button>。

## 文化不是虚的：老板不在时发生的事

"文化就是老板不在时这里发生的事情。" <button class="pd-ts" data-t="48:15" data-who="Chip Connolly" data-en="but you think about it very tangibly. Culture is what happens around here when the boss is not around. And the more distributed a company," aria-label="回原文"></button> 公司越分散，文化越重要——他有52家酒店、25家餐厅、4个水疗中心，不可能到处都在 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="48:43" data-who="Chip Connolly" data-en="But in my company in Joie de Vivre, we had 52 hotels and 25 restaurants and four spas, and it was distributed." aria-label="回原文"></button>。远程工作时代更是如此，线索全是数字化的，所以**线下聚会要更频繁** <button class="pd-ts" data-t="49:04" data-who="Chip Connolly" data-en="in the remote work world we live in, the more culture is important and more difficult because when you're remote, you know," aria-label="回原文"></button>。

文化的两个功能：**指导决策**和**吸引对的人**。Oracle、Apple、Facebook 文化不同，你可以据此选公司 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="49:38" data-who="Chip Connolly" data-en="But it's also a magnet for the right kind of people. So Oracle has a different culture than Apple, which has a different culture than Facebook. And so you can choose the place you're going to work based upon the culture." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

Amazon 的人到了 Airbnb 适应得不如 Apple 的人好——两种文化不同 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="50:08" data-who="Chip Connolly" data-en="In fact, Airbnb saw it, I think, with Amazon people. Apple people have resonated pretty well at Airbnb. Amazon people, less so." aria-label="回原文"></button>。他不喜欢"文化契合"这个词，更喜欢"[[文化增项|文化增项]]"——不是让你硬融入，而是你的差异能丰富文化 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="50:37" data-who="Chip Connolly" data-en="There's language about culture fit. I like to say culture add, because culture fit to me means, can actually be quite negative. toward somebody who is" aria-label="回原文"></button>。

他的员工需求模型源自马斯洛层次：底层是钱/报酬，中间是认可，顶层是意义 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="54:33" data-who="Chip Connolly" data-en="The employee model is really simple. It's money or compensation at the base. recognition in the middle and meaning at the top." aria-label="回原文"></button>。有些行业钱占金字塔90%，差异化的往往在认可和意义。

## 本集带走
- **会前强行对齐**：跟强势创始人开会，开头就定"这次要完成什么、什么算成功"，反复拉回这三点，比准备完美 PPT 管用

- **年长者的入场姿态**：别摆资历，当"最笨的人"＋带年轻人的精力（身体投入＋正面能量），别人注意你的精力多于你的皱纹
- **愿意降薪换时间**：主动提降薪到60%-80%时间，公司获得你的流程知识（怎么在组织里把事办成），你觉得物有所值
- **面试时反问文化**：让多个人用三到五个形容词描述公司文化，看答案一不一致——不一致说明没对齐，或者部门跟整体文化不同
- **焦虑资产负债表**：四列（知道/不知道/能控制/不能控制），把模糊的焦虑填进去，5分钟内降低焦虑感

  function fmt(s){

    }
