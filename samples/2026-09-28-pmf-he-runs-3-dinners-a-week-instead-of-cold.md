这一集聊的是一家法律 AI 公司怎么从零做到几十上百家企业客户——而最反直觉的一点是：还没找到 PMF（[[产品市场契合|产品市场契合]]），创始人就雇了行业里最顶尖的销售，投人不看融资材料的 [[Sequoia|Sequoia]] 也同一天给了支票。说这话的人是 [[Nick Fleisher|Nick Fleisher]]，[[Sandstone|Sandstone]] 的创始人，刚拿了 3000 万美元 A 轮。Sandstone 做的是给企业法务部门用的 AI 工作管理平台——大概理解成「法务版的 Jira 加 CRM」。

## 第一个 PMF 时刻：客户主动帮你拉引荐

Sandstone 还是 pre-seed 时就拿下了一家财富 500 强——对那个阶段的初创公司来说很罕见。部署几周后，团队去现场跟 GC（总法律顾问）和法务团队待在一起，发现对方不只用了最初搭建的那一个团队空间，而是自己建了一打，每个上面挂着几百个工单 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="02:14" data-who="Nick Fleisher" data-en="The first moment for us was really one of our early big clients. And it's rare for startups, at that stage we were pre-seed, to be able to work with Fortune 500s. And we had deployed with them for a couple weeks. This is kind of before we had like product telemetry and like understood usage at a reasonable amount. And we actually went on site and spent time with the GC and the legal team. And we're watching them open Sandstone. And you may be familiar with like a JIRA or Linear. Sandstone has a very similar interface for managing all your legal work. And we saw they had not just the one team we had initially set up for them, but a dozen. And a couple hundred tickets or items on each one. And so it was clear that they were using it a lot. And I think that was the first spark. But where it got a little bit more, you know, what I would say like, hey, this is real PMF is when we left that meeting in Boston, the GC or the person running the team had fired off six, maybe seven or eight intros to other GCs in Boston, suggesting that we get time with them and saying, this is a product that you need to be using. And to me, that's like, you know, when you're not even going to them asking for the intros, it's just happening. Right. That's like real PMF to some extent. And it's how you drive much higher growth. ## 产品是怎么长出来的：法务从没有过自己的 Jira

Nick 原是工程师，后去 [[McKinsey|McKinsey]]，工作恰好全落在法律科技上。转折来自他在 McKinsey 内部的亲身经历：申请一份工作说明书（statement of work），要在 Slack 上找到对的律师、等几天回复、再被转发、10 天后收到一堆问题、回答完再等几天才拿到草稿——好几周的流程，而大部分工作量根本不在最终产出物本身，全在前面的找人、收集上下文 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="07:30" data-who="Nick Fleisher" data-en="This was five years ago. Okay. This is pre-Gen AI. But legal started to have this moment where there was a lot more automation. And people were starting to say, we need to get our data in order. So we took this work with legal tech companies. And we started going to law firms and in-house legal teams and saying, your data is a mess. It's all over the place. You don't have any tech people within the org. Let's just go figure that out. And it was very nicely timed because then Gen AI became a thing. And all these law firms and big, massive legal departments were like, hey, we want our AI to work better. But our data is a mess. And so we pivoted into becoming sort of like a Gen AI consultancy for big law firms and legal departments. That was most of my work. But another chunk of it was actually helping McKinsey's legal team. And so McKinsey's legal team is a couple hundred lawyers. We would work with them and think about what they were doing and how it could relate to what our clients are doing. At McKinsey, if I needed a statement of work for a new client, I would message my favorite lawyer that I knew very well on Slack. She would take a couple of days to respond because she's a busy person. She would then forward it to the right person, usually through Slack or through email. And then 10 days later, that person would come back to me with a bunch of questions. After I answered those questions, a couple of days later, they would draft the statement of work and send it over to me. And so it was like this multi-week process to create something that was like, to one, connect to the right person, to two, gather the right context, and three, like create a pretty simple work product. But what I realized in that was like, most of the work was not in creating the work product, right? Which is where you think of like all the J&amp;I applications. It was just like all the stuff before it. And so that's where the first idea was born, which was my co-founder, Jared, who's also at McKinsey. He's a lawyer. And we sort of said, why can't we just build a tool on top of Slack and Teams and email that can do that first back and forth, connect you to the right person, gather the context, and then just put it on their plate with ready to go work on the work product. And so that was, you know, V1. It was centralizing intake across all these different sources, finding the right lawyer in the legal department to work on it, and asking questions using AI with the business user to gather all the context before it hits the lawyer's plate. And like, we realized that was quite an unmet need. Like there were some intake point solutions, but none that were using Gen AI like that. 业余测试时发生了两件出乎意料的事：第一，人们开始拿它单纯地跟踪和管理工作——法务团队从来没有过属于自己的 Jira 或 Linear；第二，他们也没有 CRM，没有统一管理上下文的地方。产品因此从「intake 前门」扩展成「法务上下文引擎」：集成 Slack、Teams、Drive、SharePoint 的数据，当一份销售协议落到你桌上，它知道对手方的一切、上一轮谈判是谁做的、结果如何 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="10:39" data-who="Nick Fleisher" data-en="No. So the first part was, okay, this is becoming from intake to like managing work. What we then realized is, to the point you just raised, they don't have a CRM and they don't have a single place to just manage all the context behind what's been going on. And so it expanded from just doing intake to actually the data that we have in Slack and Teams, Drive, SharePoint, all these places were integrated, is super rich for the legal team to be able to get the context on anything they're working on. So we went from just saying, let's do intake and work management to actually we're just a context engine or a CRM for the legal department. So when that request hits my desk and it's a sales agreement I need to review, we know everything about the counterparty, we can tell you who worked on the last negotiation, how it went, and we can then use that to help you answer the questions or review the new contract. And that's where the tool started to evolve from like intake front door to a real legal context engine, which is where clients are starting to get, you know, are getting more value today." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

为什么法务一直没有这套东西？Nick 的解释：律所侧是合伙人各自经营生意，很难统一到一套系统；企业法务侧则历来是后台职能、只是其他部门软件的被动接受者。

2000 年代长出来的[[合同生命周期管理|合同生命周期管理]]（CLM）算「法务 CRM 的 V1」，但它只看已签署的合同、只是数据的一个时点快照——法务做的远不止合同，而 AI 需要的是完整的上下文。Sandstone 自我定位是 CLM 的第二波浪潮 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="11:55" data-who="Nick Fleisher" data-en="Some law firms use like Salesforce and CRMs, not very successfully. And I think part of that on the law firm side is because you also have, and it was similar to McKinsey, you have partners who kind of run their own businesses. And so to get them all bought in on like this one shared unified instance is like really hard. On the corporate legal side, like where we work, which is like we only work with in-house legal departments, right? The Fortune 500s all the way down to legal teams that are two or three people. The problem there is they're just takers of the software of the rest of the business. Like historically, like a legal department is like this back office function. It's already a big cost. Why would we give them their own hub for managing work? In the early 2000s into like 2010s, like a market did get created or grew pretty significantly, which is contract lifecycle management. And I would think of this as like V1 of the legal CRM, which was, here's a place that the legal department has that is not part of the rest of the company. And it's where they store and manage their contracts. The problem with that is twofold. One is it's only looking at contracts, right? Legal teams do a lot more than contracts. Like they answer complicated questions. They work on HR matters. They work on many different things. And two, it's very point in time. So it's just looking at contracts that are like executed and signed versus like what are all the communications and processes that we're doing that lead up to that, right? So it's not actually the most helpful resource for AI because we're only looking at a snapshot of the data of the legal department. And so we kind of think of like what we're building now, this legal relationship management tool as like the second wave of contract lifecycle management." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

只做[[内部法务|内部法务]]（in-house）、不做律所，也是刻意选择：律所通过创新负责人容易进门，但要规模化使用得对着每个合伙人挨个攻，太难扩展；而 in-house 市场更大，且公司里所有重要的事——销售、采购、雇人裁人——都会经过法律，这个上下文层是切入更广企业业务的楔子 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="13:33" data-who="Nick Fleisher" data-en="Twofold. One, when I was at McKinsey, a lot of my work was with law firms. And I just think they're a really tough market to not to sell into, but to expand into. And what I mean by that is it's easy to get into a law firm through the head of innovation. But to actually get usage at scale in a law firm is really hard because you're going to every partner in every practice area as if it's its own business. And I was just like, I don't want to do that. I think second, like on the in-house side, it's a massive market, right? Like it's just objectively a bigger market in terms of count of lawyers and amount of work to be done. ## 本集带走

- **PMF 的最硬信号是「没开口的引荐」**：客户愿意押上自己的声誉给同行发邮件说「你必须用这个」，比任何使用数据都可靠。
- **设计伙伴每条反馈都问「还有谁有这个问题」**：找不出五六个有同样问题的客户就不做；问题与市场不对齐的客户直接「开除」。
- **融资可以不带 deck**：带着客户视频、演示和「我们太忙了」进会议室，比精心准备的花哨页面有说服力得多。
- **极早雇 AE 是一场人驱动赌注**：标准只有一个——此人是不是市场上最顶尖的；他的工作不是冲配额，而是每周 100 场演示验证 PMF。
- **当面是一切的前提**：赢单率显著更高；私信 intake、Coupa 集成这些关键产品决策全部来自坐在客户旁边看工作流真实发生。
- **晚宴公式：10 人里只放 2 个自己人**：混入客户和潜在客户，聊行业不聊产品；几乎 100% 转化为演示，场地可以找律师老板免费赞助。
- **不要为了招聘而招聘**：融完资别急着雇一堆工程师，宁肯自己多扛、压力再大，也等真正最顶尖的人出现。

(function(){
  function fmt(s){
    if(!isFinite(s)||s<0) s=0;
    var m=Math.floor(s/60), x=Math.floor(s%60);

        bar=box.querySelector('.bar'), fill=box.querySelector('.bar > i'),

    var total=0;
    function paint(){
      var cur=a.currentTime||0;
      fill.style.width=(total?(cur/total*100):0)+'%';
      tm.textContent=fmt(cur)+(total?' / '+fmt(total):'');
    }
    a.addEventListener('loadedmetadata',function(){
      total=a.duration||0;
