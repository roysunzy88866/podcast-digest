如果你的直觉 95% 是对的,那你的想法 75% 都是错的——这是 [[Mark Pincus|Mark Pincus]] 给产品人的当头一棒。说这话的人是 [[Zynga|Zynga]] 的创始人,做过扑克、做过《开心农场》,做过十几个用户上亿的消费产品。

这一集 [[Lenny|Lenny]] 的播客里,他带着即将出版的新书《Life at the Speed of Play》来聊产品哲学。整场对话你可以听到四块内容:他最核心的「[[已验证、更好、全新|已验证、更好、全新]]」产品框架,以及为什么把雄心降到 1000 英尺高度反而能做出大生意;怎么判断你的想法只是个 B+ 并及时止损,把 AI 当成测试机器来用;为什么今天的社交产品全是空热量,而下一个金矿藏在[[智能体|智能体]]时代;以及他作为五个孩子的父亲,在 AI 时代怎么教孩子批判性思维。

## 先搞懂什么是「已验证、更好、全新」

这个框架脱胎于 Zynga 早期的产品管理实践。Mark 说,他在 Zynga 创立之初就确立了一个基本哲学:我们每个人直觉里那些人类层面的本能几乎总是对的,但我们基于直觉之上堆砌的具体想法,通常是错的 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="04:43" data-who="Mark Pincus" data-en="And then we put these ideas on top of the instincts that are usually wrong. And my rule of thumb is your instincts are right 95% of the time, your ideas are wrong 75% or at best right 25% of the time." aria-label="回原文"></button>。他的经验法则是:你的直觉 95% 的时候是对的,但你的想法 75% 的时候是错的 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="04:43" data-who="Mark Pincus" data-en="And then we put these ideas on top of the instincts that are usually wrong. And my rule of thumb is your instincts are right 95% of the time, your ideas are wrong 75% or at best right 25% of the time." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

既然如此,框架的意义就是把你的直觉和想法隔离开来,围绕直觉狠狠地试错。他举了个反例:大名鼎鼎的游戏设计教父席德·梅尔(Sid Meiers)曾做过一款社交版《文明》,但游戏上线十分钟后,Zynga 的产品经理们就断定它出生即死亡——因为他的新用户引导(FTUE)做得太糟糕了,点击太多,根本没人能熬到看到他优秀的游戏设计 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="05:40" data-who="Mark Pincus" data-en="Sid Meiers, the godfather of game design, who's the most revered game designer, he came out with, I think, a social Civilization on Facebook and we thought, &quot;Oh, God, here comes the ultimate game designer.&quot;" aria-label="回原文"></button>。连最顶级的设计师都会在「已验证」这件事上栽跟头,就因为他没有完美复制当时 Facebook 平台上已被验证的最佳用户引导做法,他的创新根本没机会被任何人看见 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="06:12" data-who="Mark Pincus" data-en="Even Sid Meiers tripped over what were understood by the most junior product managers at Zynga was the best of breed approach to onboarding a new user to the first time user experience on the Facebook platform." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

> 【背景】FTUE 指的是 First Time User Experience,即用户第一次打开产品时的引导体验流程。如果这个流程太繁琐,用户在体验到核心功能前就会流失。

所以框架的第一步「已验证」,就是要求你先把那些别人已经做对的、不打算创新的部分,完美地、合法地复制过来。想做一个 AI 相机?先把图标、操作方式做到 Apple 或 Snapchat 那样世界顶级的水平,别一上来就想创新相机本身 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="07:30" data-who="Mark Pincus" data-en="Go to workos.com to make your app enterprise ready today. So the point of Proven, Better, New is to say, &quot;Let's take all the proven off the table.&quot; If you want to build the AI Snapchat or the AI camera, there's so many of those I see now, AI cameras, fine." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

第二步「更好」,要求更苛刻。Mark 强调,「更好」不是你觉得更好,而是该产品十个现有的重度用户里有十个都会说「太棒了,我就要这个」 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="08:36" data-who="Mark Pincus" data-en="And then better is usually we can't find better. Better is usually very small increments and innovations and better is something that 10 out of 10 of the existing users of that product would say, &quot;Fuck yeah.&quot;" aria-label="回原文"></button>。比如他们的游戏《Words With Friends》,核心就是 Scrabble(一种拼字游戏),但在移动端的精致打磨就是那个让十个人都说好的「更好」 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="09:46" data-who="Mark Pincus" data-en="And the new, what's the novel new idea, the back of the box idea that's going to get people to download and try it? In the case of Words with Friends, it was social." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

第三步「全新」,才是你真正想创新的、那个能吸引人来尝试的「盒子背面」的点子。比如《Words With Friends》的全新点子就是社交——连接你的 Facebook 图谱 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="09:53" data-who="Mark Pincus" data-en="And the new, what's the novel new idea, the back of the box idea that's going to get people to download and try it? In the case of Words with Friends, it was social." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

但关键是,你要一开始就预设这个「全新」的点子大概率会失败,如果失败了就赶紧换下一个去试 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="10:17" data-who="Mark Pincus" data-en="Even that which you'd think, &quot;Of course I want ...&quot; You might not get 10 out of 10 people saying yes, and we have to accept that that new idea is probably going to fail." aria-label="回原文"></button>。Lenny 听完总结道,这就像一台时光机,如果你能把别人验证过的东西做对,你就有机会比大公司领先一年甚至更多 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="12:46" data-who="Lenny" data-en="There's also like, if you think about the iPhone and the iPod, you don't think about them that way, but basically the iPod, there was a music player, they made it better and they added some new stuff and it-" aria-label="回原文"></button>。但创始人常犯的错,是拿一个 90 年代流行过的游戏来充当「已验证」,完全忽略了平台和受众已经变了 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="13:34" data-who="Mark Pincus" data-en="And I want to say proven gets so misused. And that what I've found over the years with founders is that they start to use Proven, Better, New to justify a wrong idea." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

## 为什么必须克服「复制羞耻症」

这个框架里透着一股浓浓的「复制」味,很多产品人会觉得这很没面子。Mark 坦言这就像一种「道德套利」:你当创始人是因为你想做创新者,学校也教你复制就是作弊 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="15:27" data-who="Mark Pincus" data-en="Talk about what you think people are missing and how you get over that hump of like this is actually the right approach in most cases. In the Peter Thiel sense, it's almost a moral arbitrage because there's something in our gut as a product maker." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

但他给出的解法很直接:如果你真的有雄心壮志,就烧掉你的简历 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="16:13" data-who="Mark Pincus" data-en="But that also makes that opportunity in some ways more available for people who have less ego involved. And I like to say, and I said this to my product makers at Zynga, if you're truly ambitious, burn your resume." aria-label="回原文"></button>。别去追求同行的掌声,你的雄心应该定义在消费者的眼中 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="16:21" data-who="Mark Pincus" data-en="And I like to say, and I said this to my product makers at Zynga, if you're truly ambitious, burn your resume. And if you define your ambition in the eyes of your consumer, not your peers, you're not trying to win awards and respect from your peers, you're trying to win the hearts and minds of nurses in Indiana, like for Farmville, you're going to define innovation differently and you're not going to worry about whether ... You're going to take the best ideas wherever you can find them if they are in service of giving her an experience that she loves more." aria-label="回原文"></button>。如果你只是把印第安纳州护士(他的《开心农场》受众)喜欢的东西拿过来,做得再好那么一英寸,她就会爱你,谁在乎这是不是复制呢?

他甚至拿 Craig Newmark 做 Craigslist(分类广告网站)举例。Craigslist 花了两年时间才给列表加上照片,因为 Craig 深知用户对每天依赖的产品有强烈的拥有感,哪怕是为了更好而改动,用户也会因为改变了原有的模式识别而愤怒 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="18:57" data-who="Mark Pincus" data-en="We feel this sense of ownership of products that we rely on every day and we're angry when they change. Even if they change for the better, we're angry because we go through life faster because of pattern recognition and we don't want to think about these things." aria-label="回原文"></button>。这才是世界级的产品制造者。

Mark 还给出了两条截然不同的创业路径:一条是 Rovio 的「野猫式钻探」,他们在做出《愤怒的小鸟》前盲试了 45 款完全不同的游戏,纯靠碰运气 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="22:10" data-who="Mark Pincus" data-en="And I talked about this a little bit in the book and in my course, but the question is at the outset for all of us, which path do you want to be on? And I argue that if you go down this path of just innovate, start with a blank whiteboard, don't look at any other products, that is kind of Rovio getting to Angry Birds." aria-label="回原文"></button>;另一条是 OMGPOP 的路子,他们在绝境中放弃了完全创新,选择完美复制 Zynga 的《Words With Friends》回合制系统,做出了爆款《你画我猜》,在应用商店霸榜 60 天 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="23:00" data-who="Mark Pincus" data-en="Or do you want to do what OMGPOP did, which is very similar story but opposite path, which was OMGPOP created the hit game Draw Something and Zynga bought them." aria-label="回原文"></button>。概率上,后者的胜算大得多。

## 雄心勃勃的反面,是极度谦逊的起点

说完了为什么要走已验证的路,接下来是他对自己惨痛教训的复盘。Mark 发现了一个悖论:你越有雄心,就越应该谦逊地从极小的地方开始 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="28:14" data-who="Mark Pincus" data-en="Everyone has the same chance and in a lot of ways if you're in a more humble place, you have a better opportunity. So the paradox is the more ambitious you are that the more humble you should be in the smaller place you should be willing to start." aria-label="回原文"></button>。他在做 Tribe(一家早期的[[社交网络|社交网络]])时,看到了巨大的市场机会,试图什么都做,结果因为太雄心勃勃而失败 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="26:20" data-who="Mark Pincus" data-en="That's human instinct and we want to be more ambitious. And so when I got to Tribe and I saw social networking and LinkedIn was just starting and I saw how big the opportunity was, I tried to do everything with Tribe." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

被 Tribe 的失败狠狠折服后,41 岁的他再次创业做 Zynga,选择了一个小得让人尴尬的起点——做了一个扑克游戏 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="27:03" data-who="Mark Pincus" data-en="And then I was so humbled and I was in this abyss we can talk about for so long and I was so just desperate to get out of that abyss that by the time I got to Zynga, I did something that was embarrassingly small." aria-label="回原文"></button>。别人觉得他多次成功却做这么小的东西很没尊严,但他把雄心从十万英尺降到了 1000 英尺,这反而成了 Zynga 成功的关键 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="27:24" data-who="Mark Pincus" data-en="I mean people were like, &quot;Mark, there's so much you could do in the world.&quot; But I'd say my ambition came down to a 1000 ft altitude, not a 100,000 ft. And that was the key to that being successful." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

因为大公司(比如 Meta)有收入压力,只能做几十亿美金的生意,而初创公司的优势恰恰是可以去追求那些不成器的脆弱线索 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="28:35" data-who="Lenny" data-en="One of your insights along these lines is that this is your advantage as a startup is to be less ambitious because a Zuck needs to go really big because the revenue is already so high." aria-label="回原文"></button>。他甚至用 ChatGPT 举例,说 Facebook 最初也只是个用来查看哈佛男女生的小应用 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="25:14" data-who="Mark Pincus" data-en="Started with such humble non-ambitious places and The Facebook was just an app to check out girls and guys at Harvard. And the irony for me is I've found that my career has gone in these waves and maybe some of your other founders have had this experience too, that I've started in a very humble place of, &quot;I just want to get to anything, any product market fit&quot;, had bigger success than I imagined I would have in my first two companies, Freeloader and Support.com." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

这带出了他的另一句狠话:在希望扼杀你之前,扼杀希望 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="33:17" data-who="Lenny" data-en="But I think that's to me the real founder mode is can you have the courage to tell your team and your investors that this isn't it? That's exactly where I wanted to go, which is this other piece of advice you give, which is, &quot;Kill hope before hope kills you.&quot;" aria-label="回原文"></button>。希望是没有根据的信心,而最好的产品制造者是在收集奖金,而不是在赌博 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="33:51" data-who="Mark Pincus" data-en="There's a difference between belief and hope. Hope is confidence without basis. Hope is just, it's a prayer, but it's not founded in anything that your lived experience." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

如果你在问你的产品是不是 A 级,那它就不是 A 级,因为你充满了希望 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="58:06" data-who="Mark Pincus" data-en="And Osho said, &quot;If you're torn between two paths, they're both wrong. You have feet in two canoes. If you're asking whether or not your product is an A, it's not an A and you're full of hope." aria-label="回原文"></button>。真正的 A 级产品,你会在各方面都感受到像「瓶中闪电」一样强烈的信号 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="58:26" data-who="Mark Pincus" data-en="And then, well, how do you know how are you getting validation that it's not? You don't have the validation that it is. When you have lightning in a bottle, when you have true signal, everything works." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

更反直觉的是,他认为今天 AI 成了一种危险的工具,因为它让我们能在三个月而不是三年内做出一个产品 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="35:44" data-who="Mark Pincus" data-en="It's so powerful that it's dangerous. It's so powerful that it means we can get to a viable product in much less time and money than it took maybe three months instead of three years." aria-label="回原文"></button>。我们本该用 AI 做一台测试机器、一台失败机器,一天测 100 个想法,但大多数人却用 AI 去精心打磨一个错误的产品 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="36:17" data-who="Mark Pincus" data-en="How are you testing a 100 ideas a day instead of one in three months? I think AI is being used more to build one idea in three months than a hundred ideas in a day." aria-label="回原文"></button>。他建议:在确定它是对的产品之前,先以错误的方式构建它 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="36:27" data-who="Mark Pincus" data-en="And what would it look like? I like to say we should build it completely wrong before we know it's the right product. So build it wrong before you know it's right." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

## 社交产品的死穴:失去了肾上腺素

工具变了,人怎么办?这正是下一个话题:在 AI 时代,社交产品该往哪里走?

Mark 认为现在社交领域最大的机会,在于重拾「肾上腺素」<button class="pd-ts" data-t="52:24" data-who="Mark Pincus" data-en="I think a lot about adrenaline. Is there a heat in it? ## 管理就是把每个员工变成 CEO

格局聊完了,这一集也少不了非常接地气的管理和带人哲学。Mark 自称不喜欢管理,「你管理人员的每一天都是工作的一天」<button class="pd-ts" data-t="76:02" data-who="Mark Pincus" data-en="I don't like managing people. 他得出的终极原则是:所有的管理,就是如何让人们在你不在场的时候做正确的事 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="76:37" data-who="Mark Pincus" data-en="The bigger principle to me that I started to get to was that, &quot;Oh.&quot; I'm like, &quot;All of management is just how do we get people to do the right thing when we are not in the room?&quot;" aria-label="回原文"></button>。方法就是——让每个人成为真正的 CEO <button class="pd-ts" data-t="76:48" data-who="Mark Pincus" data-en="So I was like, &quot;Okay, if I give them a hill to take, and if I make them a CEO.&quot; Make them a real CEO. 还有一条看似反常识的建议:尽可能久地进行[[微管理|微管理]] <button class="pd-ts" data-t="81:37" data-who="Mark Pincus" data-en="Yeah, Elon famous for this too. ## 怎么在 AI 时代当好父母

工作之外,Mark 最后把话题落在了育儿上。作为五个孩子的父亲(其中还有特殊需求的孩子),他最大的感受是:我们正处于这 100 年大规模生产教育周期的尾声 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="89:40" data-who="Mark Pincus" data-en="And then I'd say in the... ## 本集带走

最后收个尾,这一集值得带走的是三句话。第一,如果你真的想做爆款,先烧掉你的简历,放下创新的虚荣心。

把别人已经做对的「已验证」的东西完美地抄过来,在细节上做到让重度用户尖叫的「更好」,最后再投入地赌一个大概率会失败的「全新」点子。第二,把雄心降到 1000 英尺的谦逊高度,别去造大船,去开快艇。

在希望扼杀你之前扼杀希望,别拿 AI 去精心打磨一个错误的产品,把它当成一天测 100 个想法的失败机器。第三,别被行业里大谈的「平台更迭」骗了,我们现在依旧深陷在移动端流量的泥潭里。与其去卷空热量的社交网络,不如去挖掘那些藏在 AI 智能体里的新可能,去找到下一个能让我们再次感到肾上腺素飙升的鸡尾酒会。

(function(){
  function fmt(s){
    if(!isFinite(s)||s<0) s=0;

    }
