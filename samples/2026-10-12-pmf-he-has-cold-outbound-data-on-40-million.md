[[Daniel Saks|Daniel Saks]] 23 岁时创办 [[AppDirect|AppDirect]],把它做成 B2B 软件交易的隐形巨头，如今商品交易总额高达上百亿美元。

2023 年，他卸任联席 CEO,创办了 AI 销售公司 [[Landbase|Landbase]],刚完成 3000 万美元的 A 轮融资。

这次他和 The Product Market Fit Show 主持人 Pablo 聊了两件事：产品市场契合到底是什么感觉，以及外向销售(outbound)在今天还有没有用、该怎么做。

> 【背景】Pablo 指 The Product Market Fit Show 的主持人 Pablo Srugo。

## 产品市场契合不是有或没有，而是一种磁力

Saks 对产品市场契合有个特别的说法：它像一种磁性动量——**你做成了一个客户，同行业的其他人主动来找你** <button class="pd-ts" data-t="02:28" data-who="Daniel Saks" data-en="So what I like to describe product market fit as is the feeling of magnetic momentum. So with AppDirect, we worked at it very hard. And one of our first customers was a telecom in Canada, Bell Canada. And we launched an app marketplace to be able to sell SaaS services. This was back in 2009, 2010, in the early days of SaaS. And some of the initial apps on the App Store included Shopify, which was like a really early stage company at the time, and other Canadian apps in the ecosystem. And that was our first go. We launched it. And because of launching Bell, we had AT&amp;T and Comcast and Deutsche Telekom all of a sudden reaching out. Because we had this one beachhead, we were able to attract others in the space. And over time, we used that same approach to enter new verticals or geographies. And we would have this strategy of going after a big player. So for example, when we wanted to get into IoT and manufacturing, we had Honeywell. And we would get them, we would evangelize that. And then we found that all the others would come to us saying, hey, we want to do something similar. So what felt like product markets fit to me is this feel of pull, where because of something that you've done here, you've done that elsewhere. And I feel in order to scale, you want to replicate these micro instances of pull or magnetic momentum and expand from beachhead to beachhead. And my belief is if you want to build a really big business, you can't just do one thing and scale it. Maybe there's a few examples of companies that have done that. But in general, you always need to be innovating. You always need to be changing. And product market fit is not something that you don't have and then you have. It's something that you always have to work for within given micro segments. So our key lesson, which we still take today at App Director, is just applicable at Landbase, is you want to look at where is there a leader or an innovator or what I call a digital hero of someone who's going to adopt the technology and everyone else is going to want to follow them because you can see how transformational it was to the company that they're at, but more specifically to the career of the individual that wanted to use your technology and drive a transformation." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

AppDirect 的第一个客户是加拿大电信巨头 Bell。拿下 Bell 之后，AT&T、Comcast、Deutsche Telekom 纷纷主动上门。后来想进入制造业，先拿下 Honeywell,其他厂商跟着来。

他的核心观点是：产品市场契合不是你某天突然拥有然后就一劳永逸的东西，而是要在一个个细分市场里不断争取。

想做大公司，不能靠一件事吃天下，要从一个滩头阵地打向下一个滩头阵地。

## 那个 flop 的发布会：大客户≠成功

Bell 的故事其实开局惨败。2009、2010 年左右，AppDirect 团队花了一年才签下 Bell,又花了半年到一年实施。上线前 72 小时，团队拼命拉各种应用入驻。

Bell 的高管放话：会上广告牌、上机场广告，流量会把服务器挤爆，你们得扩容 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="00:00" data-who="Daniel Saks" data-en="Product market fit is not something that you don't have and then you have. It's something that you always have to work for within given micro segments. I remember the head of Bell saying, this is going to be on billboards. This is going to be in airports. There's going to be so much that it's going to break the site. Like you got to have the infrastructure to make this work. You know, crunching to get this thing launched. We launched on time. It was like crickets. No one came to the app store. And in fact, like Harley from Shopify came to me saying, WTF, the Bell business app store has less traffic than my intern's blog. What's happened in the last year is there was a point where there was so much AI slop and so much spam that we're all overloaded. And there was a shot that like, hey, this is never going to work just because it's just our inboxes are flooded and no one's going to ever tune into this. But I think what's happened in the last year is the spam filters have gotten way better at filtering out bad messages and putting in good. It's not just if the message is generated via AI or not. That's not how they distinguish it. It's actually, you know, the relevance and the, you know, credibility of the domain." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

结果按时上线，门可罗雀。几周后 Shopify 的 Harley 找来说：Bell 商店的应用市场，流量比我实习生的博客还少。

团队从中学到两件事。第一，**企业只从自己信任的品牌买东西**——电信公司卖安全、视频会议服务说得通，卖记账软件就没人信。

第二，企业客户的信任来自多年打交道的销售员。

与其指望客户看广告牌后自己上网站，不如培训电信公司现有的销售队伍，让他们去卖 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="08:29" data-who="Daniel Saks" data-en="Exactly. It's the merchandising strategy. And then the other component is the reason why someone trusts, call it AT&amp;T or Dutch telecom in a business capacity, is that they've worked with them for many years. So the other insight that we had was instead of just believing that you can get people from a billboard to go on a website, the better strategy was to actually enable the sales channels and the relationships that already exist. So once we started training the physical human sellers at these companies, they were able to articulate the value prop. And that's really what drove a lot of the success and the momentum." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

等 Deutsche Telekom 和 AT&T 上线时，这套打法成功了，如今每年带来上百亿美元的交易额。

## AI 垃圾邮件泛滥之后，精准反而更值钱

Landbase 做的是外向销售工具，类似把 ZoomInfo 的数据、[[Clay|Clay]] 的编排能力、AI 写信发信功能合成一体。

And what we did is we actually, through partners, had access to a lot of transactional data from campaigns. And we trained our AI on what works and what doesn't. 另一种是极度精准——找对的公司、对的人、对的价值主张、对的时机。

第二步是给账户分层：最顶层的 A 类账户，CEO 本人出击，甚至约线下见面；中间层交给团队；

最低层用自动化邮件和 LinkedIn 触达，不浪费人力。核心逻辑很简单：**销售产能永远有限，时间要花在最值钱的名单上**。

## 数字长什么样：回复率、接通率与三步节奏

Saks 给出的基准很冷静。邮件方面，一般好名单的回复率约 2%;名单足够精准能到 8% 到 10%;像 Rippling、Ramp 这类有强大品牌的，可能到 20%,但极少见 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="40:01" data-who="Daniel Saks" data-en="so it's really tough out there on email I think there's a lot of people on LinkedIn who say oh I have like these high response rates but we see aggregate in millions of volume I think in general you're looking at a 2% response rate even on a good list for email because of span and deliverability but if you have a tight list and it's pretty strong you can get to 8% to 10% and then hypothesize that stronger companies with big brands like a rippling ramp might be in the 20% range but that's very rare and they've spent a lot of time on you know customized outreach I think for connect rates it's similar where industry connect rates you know people talk about 8% to 10% as being normal and great" aria-label="回原文"></button>。

电话接通率类似：很多公司卡在 2%,好名单能到 8% 到 10%。Landbase 服务的那批独立 [[SDR|SDR]](从大公司出来单干的销售)，接通率被提升了 2 到 4 倍。

触达节奏上，数据支持三步走：邮件、电话、LinkedIn 各来一次，三步能拿到 90% 的效果；做到五步可能多 10%,但被标记为垃圾的风险大增。

没回复的名单可以休眠一个季度再回来。他还分享了大学时在加拿大皇家银行打工的老办法：

先寄实体信再打电话，对方哪怕没看信，你看到我们寄的资料了吗也是更好的开场白——多渠道预热是在给电话铺路。

## 信任是冷触达的真正杠杆

Saks 生涯里最好的机会很多来自冷邮件——Michael Dell 曾直接用 michael@dell.com 给他发信，他当然回了。

反过来，一个不知名公司的普通销售发来的邮件，他永远不会读 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="43:00" data-who="Daniel Saks" data-en="all the data on this you could do different it's called a sequence so we enable omnichannel sequences Saks 说外向销售和产品市场契合一样，不是设置好就能永远运转的东西，要持续实验。

(function(){
  function fmt(s){
    if(!isFinite(s)||s<0) s=0;

        bar=box.querySelector('.bar'), fill=box.querySelector('.bar > i'),

    var total=0;

      var cur=a.currentTime||0;
