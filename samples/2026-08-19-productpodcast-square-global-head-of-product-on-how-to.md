这一集是 The Product Podcast 主持人 Carlos 采访 [[Square|Square]] 全球产品负责人 [[Willem Avé|Willem Avé]]。Willem 是 2014 年随着自己联合创办的预约公司 BookFresh 被 Square 收购进来的，从工程师一路做到产品负责人，见证了 Square 从一个小小的白色读卡器长成 [[Block|Block]] 旗下多个产品生态系统。这一集最有信息量的两个点：Square 两年前把整个公司从业务单元制改成完全[[职能制|职能制]]，理由是“旧的 org 模式在削弱每个职能的手艺”；以及他的判断——标准问答式[[聊天机器人|聊天机器人]]的时代已经结束了，接下来拼的是能不能造出“可靠的[[智能体|智能体]]”替小商家干真活。

## 为什么 Square 废除了业务单元模式

Willem 说，Square 多年来用的是通用管理(GM)模式，即按业务单元划分。大约两年前，整个公司被重组成完全职能化的结构：顶层是产品、设计、工程三大职能，内部再按品牌(Square、Cash App 等)划分关注领域。改的原因很明确：他们相信职能卓越(在自己领域里做到最好)非常重要，而之前的组织模式实际上削弱了每个职能的工艺水准 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="01:44" data-who="Willem Avé" data-en="Yeah, definitely. I've been at Square, slash Block, for many years. I've seen really every evolution of the company, from the little white reader all the way through to the sophisticated multiple ecosystems we have. It's unique to be at a company that has not only been able to innovate on one product, but then create multiple ecosystems of products throughout its journey. Maybe I'll start with how my journey here began. My company was acquired, I was CTO, and I grew up through the engineering org here. We had a general-management model for many years, so this was business-unit focused. And then about two years ago, we reordered the entire company to be more focused on our customers, functionalizing everything. One of the key reasons we did that was we believed functional excellence, i.e. excellence within your domain, engineering, product, design, and so on, was really important, and that some of the other org models we'd had previously reduced the craft and excellence we wanted to see within each of our functions. So the past two or so years, we've been in a fully functional world where there are top-line orgs: product, design, engineering. And within that we have brand-based focus areas, Square, Cash App, and so on. From an org-design perspective, there are many ways we could take the conversation, but one of the key things is you want to focus orgs around customer outcomes as much as possible. ## 硬件和软件不能分开想

Square 少见地同时做硬件和软件。Willem 说硬件的特别之处在于它活在商家的真实世界里——摆在柜台上、揣在兜里、轮班时一直用，所以硬件必须平易近人、好用、令人愉悦，而这不容易做到。真正的魔法是软硬件整合(他拿 iPhone 做标杆)，做新硬件时必须让软件形态和硬件形态匹配——**把它们当两件事分开做，正是那些用现成通用硬件的公司做不出好体验的原因** <button class="pd-ts" data-t="04:58" data-who="Willem Avé" data-en="Yeah. Hardware is something very special, and we have one of the best hardware teams on the planet. What I mean by special is that hardware shows up in businesses' real worlds. It's something you put on the counter, something you carry with you during your shift, something you use. So hardware must be approachable. It must be easy to use. It must be completely delightful. And doing so is not easy. The magic really happens when you integrate hardware and software together in a way that builds delightful experiences. Almost everyone's used an iPhone; they set the bar for a lot of that software-hardware integration. The key is making sure that when you're building new hardware products, you marry the software form factor with the hardware form factor. You can't think of them as completely separate, which is why it's difficult for companies to use off-the-shelf, commodity hardware and build truly excellent experiences." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

软硬件团队最大的差异是时间线：做硬件比做软件慢，要过的关卡也不一样。要出好结果，一是要有顶尖的人，二是流程里不能有让决策陷进官僚泥潭的繁文缛节——因为无论硬件还是软件，本质都是迭代：先有愿景，然后在摸清目标缺陷率、可靠性、电池寿命这些维度的过程中不断转向 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="06:20" data-who="Willem Avé" data-en="It's a good question. The biggest difference is the timelines are usually just different: building hardware takes longer than building software, and there are different steps you go through. To get really great outcomes, first you need incredible people. Two, you need to make sure the process you go through for building doesn't have a ton of burdensome red tape where decisions get mired in bureaucratic process or slow decision-making, because ultimately both building hardware and software is one of iteration. You have a vision of what you want to build, but the path there takes a bunch of different turns as you figure out what defect rates you're targeting, what reliability rates, battery life, and so on. There's a ton of dimensions to it. And then the best software is also built via an iterative learning loop. So if you combine both of those together, you can have both an org and a process that leads to incredible outcomes. The DRI Model: Killing the Silent Veto" aria-label="回原文"></button>。

## DRI 模型：杀死“沉默否决权”

Block 在组织设计上以激进著称，[[DRI|DRI]] 是他们力推的概念。Willem 解释说，好产品从构想到规模化是一条跨职能的弧线——不只把产品做出来，还包括怎么推向市场、怎么持续迭代。

而产品变慢往往卡在决策上：一种是“沉默否决”(一个团队就是不配合另一个团队想做的事)，一种是对方向缺乏共识。**DRI(直接负责人)就是让一个人被充分授权，把决策贯穿产品构建的整个生命周期**——这个人要能综合产品、技术和业务，从以设计工程为主导的构思阶段，一直管到推向市场和规模化，既塑造技术战略，又让团队对执行负责，替他们砍掉路上的障碍 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="07:37" data-who="Willem Avé" data-en="Yeah. It's an incredibly interesting concept, and one we've leaned into very hard. What's amazing is that for the best products, you have to have an arc from idea to scaling it, and that arc is a cross-functional effort. It's not just building an incredible product; it's how you go to market with it, and then how you keep iterating and building it. A lot of the time, products slow down because of decision-making. Either it's silent vetoes throughout the org, where one team doesn't want to do something that another team wants to do, or it's a lack of alignment on what we're doing or where we're going. So the DRI model is really meant to cut through decision-making and effectively empower an individual to see that decision-making through the entire life cycle of building that product or solution. It's a unique position, because you have to be able to synthesize product, technology, and business, and then work very well across the entire arc of building that software. Everything from ideation, and I think the best ideation happens in a design- and engineering-led fashion, where they can explore the entire space, all the way through to go-to-market and scale, where you need to be able to tell the world about what you're building and why. So the DRI really helps shape technical strategy, but also keeps the teams accountable to execution, and breaks down decision-making so they can cut through any roadblocks or challenges the teams may have. Reinventing the Org Around AI" aria-label="回原文"></button>。

主持人提到外界说法：这像是把公司重塑成一个迷你 AGI,每个人都向同一个人汇报的超级扁平组织。Willem 的回答很有分量：得先退一步理解组织为什么存在——历史上，组织存在的理由是**信息流**：需要机制让不同团队对齐到同一个结果。

而现代技术和 AI 放大了组织树末端节点之间的直接沟通能力。组织又极其擅长发明流程和繁文缛节(往往事出有因：出了问题就立个规矩防止重演)，但他认为 **AI 让知识可以编码在另一层——不用全编码在组织架构里，而是编码在智能体能访问的数据里，这真正让决策民主化**，让小而快、完全自主的团队成为可能。你不用再玩“传话游戏”去要一个答案，而是直接问智能体、做决定、往前走 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="09:33" data-who="Willem Avé" data-en="It's a good question. You have to step back and understand why orgs exist in the first place. At least historically, orgs existed because of information flow: there needed to be a way to communicate between different orgs and teams and align them on a singular outcome, whatever you're building. Modern technology and AI have actually amplified the communication ability between a lot of the leaf nodes of a tree, so to speak. Whether it's everyone reporting to one person, or there are evolutions of that, the key goal is to have small, autonomous teams that can take ownership of an outcome and build it as quickly as possible. Typically orgs are very good at inventing process and inventing red tape, because it meets the needs of whatever org they've created, or whatever goals that org has. A lot of the time those are for good reasons: you have a problem, and you create some process to stop that from happening again. But I think AI gives us the ability to encode that knowledge at another layer. It doesn't have to be encoded in the org entirely; it can be encoded in data that agents have access to, and that really democratizes decision-making. It allows small teams, and I truly believe the best teams are small, fast-moving, fully autonomous, and enabled, to really happen. You hear a lot right now about enterprise context graphs and things like that for AI." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

但他也划了边界：这不意味着不需要管理。人有职业成长和抱负，所以仍需要好的人员经理——好经理和好 DRI 必须两者都要 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="11:12" data-who="Willem Avé" data-en="I think it's really just another way of saying you have to encode knowledge in tools that individuals and teams can take advantage of. So you don't have to play telephone to get an answer; you can get an answer from an agent, make a decision, and move forward. So the DRI model, combined with smaller, flatter orgs, does allow you to move a lot faster. That doesn't diminish the need for management. People grow in their careers, people have aspirations, so you also need incredible people managers who can help people through their career growth and trajectories. You have to marry both of those together: you need great managers, and then you need great DRIs, people who can cut through decisions so you can deliver great products. Building for the Real Economy" aria-label="回原文"></button>。

## 为实体经济造 AI:聊天机器人时代结束了

Square 的用户不是整天挂在 X 上的科技公司，而是开面包店、花店、汉堡店的小商家。Willem 对小企业的态度带着敬意：他们是企业家最纯粹的形态——把生计押在自己的激情上；小企业是经济的动脉，Square 的口号是“让主街(Main Street)和华尔街一样高”。

他坚信把先进技术民主化、交给尽可能多的小企业，对经济和社会都是净收益。难点在于每家小企业都无穷地独特：面包店不同于花店，花店不同于汉堡店，工作流完全不同 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="12:41" data-who="Willem Avé" data-en="Yeah, the real world is definitely not on X every day, though some are. Small business is fascinating, because they're the most pure instantiation of an entrepreneur. They literally put their livelihood on the line for their passion, and that passion could be anything from candlestick making to bakeries to really anything. I find that endlessly fascinating, and I respect it a lot. Small business, in my opinion, really forms the artery, or the core, of the economy. The main streets of the world are unique. They're really important social areas for different economies and neighborhoods. What Square and Block have always stood for is economic empowerment, and what that means for Square is making sure neighborhoods can thrive. We say we want Main Street to stand as tall as Wall Street. That's really important, because to do so, they need technology. You can't compete today in the business landscape without automating parts of your business with software, and now increasingly with artificial intelligence. I firmly believe that if you take advanced technology and democratize it, give it to as many people, as many small businesses, as possible, that's net good for the economy and net good for society. So those are some of the things I spend a lot of time on. And it's not easy, because each of these businesses is endlessly unique. They need very specific workflows. A bakery is just different from a flower shop, and that's different from a burger joint. They have different people, different teams, different workflows. So it's all different, and that's one of the awesome things we've been able to do at Square. Bringing AI to People Who Aren't Tech Savvy" aria-label="回原文"></button>。

他观察到经营小生意的真实痛点：难不只难在手艺和雇人，更是**一种孤独**——你总不知道该往哪走、该做什么决定。汉堡该定价多少？

成本能算，但加价 10% 还是 20%？决策无穷无尽，小企业主常常从枕边人还没醒一直干到晚上。所以 AI 对他们的价值不是聊天机器人，而是一个**智能思维伙伴**：先帮做更好的决定，最终把完整任务委派出去 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="14:59" data-who="Willem Avé" data-en="Yeah, it's a good question. Running a business is difficult, and not for the most obvious reason. Yes, obviously you have to hire and do your craft, whether that's food or whatever. But it's also a kind of loneliness, where you don't always know where to go or what decisions to make. For example, what should you price a menu item at? What should you price your burger? Sure, you could calculate how much it costs you to make the burger, but should you mark it up by 10% or 20%? There's an endless set of decisions. Sometimes you hear small business owners start working the second there's another head still on the pillow in their bed, and they keep working all the way through the evening, because there's always some decision they have to make. The advantage, or the attraction, of AI for business is not just a chatbot; it's really to have this intelligent thought partner that lets businesses make better decisions, and then eventually offload and delegate full tasks to parts of their business. A couple of examples could be inventory intake, or spotting sales trends for your business and then suggesting a marketing campaign, or a number of other unique workflows. The interesting part is that the days of just a standard question-answer chatbot are kind of over, for a few reasons. First, we've basically already solved that problem, and what people are expecting now is to do real work with AI. ## 本集带走

- **组织围绕客户结果设计，离得越远结果越差**：Square 为此从业务单元制改成完全职能制(产品/设计/工程顶层分立、内部按品牌划分)，理由是保住每个职能的工艺和卓越。
- **用 DRI 对付“沉默否决权”**：产品变慢常是因为一个团队暗地里不配合。指定一个能综合产品、技术、业务的 DRI,把决策权贯穿从构思到规模化的全周期。
- **AI 改变了知识编码的位置**：过去知识编码在组织架构和流程里，现在可以编码进智能体可访问的数据——决策因此民主化，小而自主的团队成为可能；但人员管理仍然不可省。
- **别做聊天机器人，做“能产出工件的智能体”**：问答已经解决完了；用户要的是菜单工程、劳动力预测、库存工作流这种可直接使用的产出，难点从“会答”变成“可靠”。
- **信任先于委派**：让用户少干活(而不是解析一堆“文字呕吐”)才能建立信任，有信任才有批量操作和任务委派。
- **TAM 观**：对的团队 + 对的想法 + 对的学习循环，TAM 几乎无限——你可以创造市场；用“社区网络”这类强制函数帮大 TAM 市场里的团队做取舍。
