# Square 为什么废掉业务单元制

[[Square|Square]] 两年前做了一件很多公司反着来的事:把运行多年的业务单元(通管模式)整个拆掉,改成按职能——产品、设计、工程——来组织 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="01:44" data-who="Willem Avé" data-en="Yeah, definitely. I've been at Square, slash Block, for many years. I've seen really every evolution of the company, from the little white reader all the way through to the sophisticated multiple ecosystems we have. It's unique to be at a company that has not only been able to innovate on one product, but then create multiple ecosystems of products throughout its journey. Maybe I'll start with how my journey here began. My company was acquired, I was CTO, and I grew up through the engineering org here. We had a general-management model for many years, so this was business-unit focused. And then about two years ago, we reordered the entire company to be more focused on our customers, functionalizing everything. One of the key reasons we did that was we believed functional excellence, i.e. excellence within your domain, engineering, product, design, and so on, was really important, and that some of the other org models we'd had previously reduced the craft and excellence we wanted to see within each of our functions. So the past two or so years, we've been in a fully functional world where there are top-line orgs: product, design, engineering. And within that we have brand-based focus areas, Square, Cash App, and so on. From an org-design perspective, there are many ways we could take the conversation, but one of the key things is you want to focus orgs around customer outcomes as much as possible. The farther you go from that principle, the worse outcomes you get. Why Square Killed the Business-Unit Model" aria-label="回原文"></button>。原因是业务单元制让每个职能的「手艺」退化了,工程师和产品经理变成了通才,但专业深度不够。改成[[职能制|职能制]]之后,再在下面按客户群体切:服务业、零售、餐饮这些垂直领域各一个团队,加上核心平台、增长、资金(账单、工资、银行)几个横向团队,[[硬件|硬件]]则作为独立的姐妹组织并行 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="03:34" data-who="Willem Avé" data-en="Great. So it's the Square brand, and I lead product for the Square brand. The way we've decided to organize the Square org is leaning into a couple of beliefs I have for building products. One is you want teams as close to customer outcomes as possible, so they can really understand the needs of the customers and build products to serve those needs. That's led us to a couple of broad orgs. First is an audiences org: these are all of our verticals, think services, retail, food and beverage, and so on. Then a core platform org. But the core platform org importantly also has product services it's responsible for. I think it's very easy for platform teams to fall into a kind of &quot;one size fits none&quot; when they're building software, thinking about a very generic solution that doesn't solve any needs. So it's important to marry product and platform together in certain areas. Then we have a growth team, so the whole acquisition flywheel. And a money team, so money for us is everything from bill pay, payroll, banking, and so on. And then obviously we have our hardware teams, which is a sister org to us. Integrating Hardware and Software" aria-label="回原文"></button>。

硬件和软件整合是 Square 的老难题。硬件出现在商家的真实物理空间里——柜台上的、轮班时随身带的——它必须「平易近人、易用、令人愉悦」,但这不容易做到 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="04:58" data-who="Willem Avé" data-en="Yeah. Hardware is something very special, and we have one of the best hardware teams on the planet. What I mean by special is that hardware shows up in businesses' real worlds. It's something you put on the counter, something you carry with you during your shift, something you use. So hardware must be approachable. It must be easy to use. It must be completely delightful. And doing so is not easy. The magic really happens when you integrate hardware and software together in a way that builds delightful experiences. Almost everyone's used an iPhone; they set the bar for a lot of that software-hardware integration. The key is making sure that when you're building new hardware products, you marry the software form factor with the hardware form factor. You can't think of them as completely separate, which is why it's difficult for companies to use off-the-shelf, commodity hardware and build truly excellent experiences." aria-label="回原文"></button>。关键在于不能把硬件形态和软件形态分开想,这也是为什么拿现成通用硬件去拼,很难做出真正好的体验。

配合职能化重组,Square 推出了 [[DRI|DRI]](直接责任人)模型。背景是:好产品需要一条从构想到规模化的完整弧线,跨工程、产品、设计、市场,但产品经常在决策环节慢下来——要么各团队之间「沉默否决」,嘴上不说但就是不配合;要么对方向没对齐 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="07:37" data-who="Willem Avé" data-en="Yeah. It's an incredibly interesting concept, and one we've leaned into very hard. What's amazing is that for the best products, you have to have an arc from idea to scaling it, and that arc is a cross-functional effort. It's not just building an incredible product; it's how you go to market with it, and then how you keep iterating and building it. A lot of the time, products slow down because of decision-making. Either it's silent vetoes throughout the org, where one team doesn't want to do something that another team wants to do, or it's a lack of alignment on what we're doing or where we're going. So the DRI model is really meant to cut through decision-making and effectively empower an individual to see that decision-making through the entire life cycle of building that product or solution. It's a unique position, because you have to be able to synthesize product, technology, and business, and then work very well across the entire arc of building that software. Everything from ideation, and I think the best ideation happens in a design- and engineering-led fashion, where they can explore the entire space, all the way through to go-to-market and scale, where you need to be able to tell the world about what you're building and why. So the DRI really helps shape technical strategy, but also keeps the teams accountable to execution, and breaks down decision-making so they can cut through any roadblocks or challenges the teams may have. Reinventing the Org Around AI" aria-label="回原文"></button>。

关于组织未来的形态,有一种说法是「把公司重塑成迷你 AGI,每个人向同一个人汇报,超级扁平」。Willem 的看法是:组织存在的历史原因是为了信息流通和上下对齐,但现代技术和 AI 放大了组织末端节点之间的沟通能力 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="09:33" data-who="Willem Avé" data-en="It's a good question. You have to step back and understand why orgs exist in the first place. At least historically, orgs existed because of information flow: there needed to be a way to communicate between different orgs and teams and align them on a singular outcome, whatever you're building. Modern technology and AI have actually amplified the communication ability between a lot of the leaf nodes of a tree, so to speak. Whether it's everyone reporting to one person, or there are evolutions of that, the key goal is to have small, autonomous teams that can take ownership of an outcome and build it as quickly as possible. Typically orgs are very good at inventing process and inventing red tape, because it meets the needs of whatever org they've created, or whatever goals that org has. A lot of the time those are for good reasons: you have a problem, and you create some process to stop that from happening again. But I think AI gives us the ability to encode that knowledge at another layer. It doesn't have to be encoded in the org entirely; it can be encoded in data that agents have access to, and that really democratizes decision-making. It allows small teams, and I truly believe the best teams are small, fast-moving, fully autonomous, and enabled, to really happen. You hear a lot right now about enterprise context graphs and things like that for AI." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

Building for the Real Economy" aria-label="回原文"></button>。

## 本集带走
- **废业务单元、走职能制**:不是所有公司都该这么做,但如果你发现各职能的专业深度在退化,职能制是一个值得考虑的方向——按客户群体在职能下面再切,而不是按业务单元包揽一切。
- **DRI 贯穿全弧线**:产品慢往往不是能力问题,是决策问题。让一个人从构思到规模化全程负责、能综合产品技术商业做决策,比加流程加审批管用。
- **AI 编码组织知识,而不是编码在组织里**:原来靠层级传递的信息,可以编码到智能体能访问的数据层,让小团队自主决策、不用传话。
- **聊天机器人时代结束了,下一步是「工件」**:用户要的不是问答线程,是 AI 跨数据源综合后生成的具体产出物(菜单分析、库存工作流、销售排行榜)。建聊天机器人容易,建可靠的智能体难。
- **先给价值、再要信任、最后才委托操作**:别一上来就让 AI 改价格、动库存。先帮商家看到洞察、生成有价值的工件,信任建立了,他们才会把执行动作交出去。
- **TAM 可以被创造**:不要只盯着现有市场算天花板,用对的方法(Square 的方法是锁定「社区网络」这个北极星)可以拓展边界。
