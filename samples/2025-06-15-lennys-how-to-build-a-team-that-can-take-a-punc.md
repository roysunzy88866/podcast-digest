产品领导力是这样一种角色，如果你不控制脑子里的声音，它们会把你活活吞掉 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="00:00" data-who="嘉宾" data-en="Product leadership is the type of role where if you are not in control of the voices in your head, they will eat you alive. You spend a lot of time thinking about how to help your team learn to take a punch." aria-label="回原文"></button>。这话是 Hilary 的前老板 Kelvin 说的，Hilary 是 [[Whoop|Whoop]] 的核心产品负责人，之前在 Big Health 做数字疗法——就是经过临床验证、用来治疗失眠、抑郁、焦虑的移动应用。她把治疗抑郁症时学到的认知行为疗法核心技巧，搬进了日常管理。

## "反制叙事"：挨了一拳之后怎么办

"承受打击"不是硬扛，是有一套具体动作。核心就一句话：**别去纠正别人对你的印象，去做一件小事来证明你不是他以为的那种人** <button class="pd-ts" data-t="08:05" data-who="嘉宾" data-en="What are some of the things that you teach your team and help them develop a skill? So at its core, when I say take a punch, what I mean is you're going to run into situations where something has gone wrong." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

具体操作：当团队成员心烦意乱地来找你，说某人在会上批评了自己、或者听说别人对自己看法不好，不要跟着一起分析"那个人为什么那么想"——那是反刍，是焦虑思维模式的死循环 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="17:04" data-who="嘉宾" data-en="I feel like I've spent so much time talking to people in meetings, whatever it is, where it's just this ruminating on something that has already happened. It's very anxious thinking pattern, I think, and people can just get stuck in it." aria-label="回原文"></button>。先问三句话："你脑子里在想什么？

你害怕什么？你在担心什么？

" <button class="pd-ts" data-t="12:51" data-who="嘉宾" data-en="They come to me. They seem agitated about something and I say to them, &quot;It seems like this is really bothering you. What's going on in your head?" aria-label="回原文"></button>。通常会冒出来的是"我怕他觉得我不行""我怕他觉得我是白痴"。

然后挑战这个想法："有证据吗？就算有，你能不能做一件事，向他们展示那不是真的？" <button class="pd-ts" data-t="13:17" data-who="嘉宾" data-en="I think this is really important for managers too to challenge this negative thinking when you see it happen and not just validate it and allow them to go down these negative spirals." aria-label="回原文"></button>

Hilary 自己的例子：开会时 CTO 提议追踪氯胺酮使用情况，Hilary 笑了，CTO 非常严肃地说"这不好笑，这是很多人的严重问题" <button class="pd-ts" data-t="10:06" data-who="嘉宾" data-en="And our chief technology officer suggested ketamine tracking. And I thought she was making a joke and I laughed, and she looked at me very seriously and was like, &quot;This isn't funny, Hilary." aria-label="回原文"></button>。Hilary 觉得蒙羞——她其实非常认真对待成瘾问题，也自认是拥抱新想法的人。

本能反应是事后找 CTO 解释"我不是那个意思"。但她没这么做。

她花了五到十分钟查了一下新兴公共卫生问题，发现体育博彩在年轻人中正成为专家担忧的重点，于是给 CTO 发了一条短消息："想在你今天的想法上做补充，我看到了关于体育博彩的研究，我们可以追踪投注行为和压力水平之间的相关性。" <button class="pd-ts" data-t="12:00" data-who="嘉宾" data-en="And I did some research very, very quickly on what are some emerging public health concerns that people really aren't talking about that would be interesting to track?" aria-label="回原文"></button> 完事。没有解释，没有辩驳，用一个具体行动反制了"她不认真对待健康问题"这个她害怕的叙事 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="12:47" data-who="嘉宾" data-en="But I think it's a great example of showing this idea of counter-program that narrative, don't fight about the narrative. And so when I teach this to my team, I'm always doing the same thing." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

这套方法的源头是认知行为疗法里的"[[行为激活|行为激活]]"——抑郁的人觉得"我要等感觉好点了再行动"，但治疗师教的是反过来的：**先行动，然后感觉才会好起来** <button class="pd-ts" data-t="19:46" data-who="嘉宾" data-en="And so the misconception is I'll feel better and then I'll act. And the thing that therapists try to teach people, they're working with them in therapy is I will act and then I will feel better." aria-label="回原文"></button>。Hilary 手机里有个行为激活清单，列着她感觉情绪下行时能做的具体小事，比如从椅子上拿起一件衣服收好——足够小，但能把人从螺旋里拉出来 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="20:08" data-who="嘉宾" data-en="I mean, I have a list of myself. I've got a list on my phone of my behavioral activations, and it's things that I know I can do if I start feeling like the walls are closing around me, if I feel myself getting sucked into very low mood or negative thinking, or whatever it is." aria-label="回原文"></button>。"承受打击"就是把行为激活用到职场场景：你因为别人的看法而压力巨大，那就做一个最小的行动来反转那个叙事，而不是等自己"想通了"再行动 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="22:03" data-who="嘉宾" data-en="So behavioral activation just conceptually is, how are you taking action to reverse the downward feeling or the negative feeling that you're feeling? And then the take a punch concept is that applied in the context of I'm in a working environment." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

二阶效应：教了团队这套方法之后，他们反而不那么害怕挨打了。很多人不开口发言，不是因为不会说，是因为害怕说错 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="25:29" data-who="嘉宾" data-en="So much of that skill, it's like a communication skill at its core, it's just how to express yourself verbally, but so much of the blocker of that is I think fear." aria-label="回原文"></button>。当你有了"挨打之后怎么办"的具体工具，恐惧就降低了，这是 90% 的挑战所在 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="25:40" data-who="嘉宾" data-en="Fear of just the discomfort of everyone in a room turning and looking at you as you're trying to formulate a half-baked thought. And so if you can help people be less afraid of that, that's 90% of the challenge of actually improving some of these skills." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

## 建团队的心智模型：不是"老板想什么"，是"老板怎么想"

一封邮件要十个人签字，很多人说是"流程问题"——Hilary 说不是，是透明度和沟通问题 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="28:47" data-who="嘉宾" data-en="I think people will often say like, &quot;Oh, it's a process problem.&quot; It's not a process problem. It's not approval problem." aria-label="回原文"></button>。根本原因是这十个人对"CEO 是怎么思考的"各有各的模型，谁都不确定自己的理解对不对，于是都要过一遍 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="28:42" data-who="嘉宾" data-en="And I think the answer to that is because those 10 people all have different information, different context, and in many cases, completely different working models for how the CEO of the company and other strategic leaders in the company think." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

比"理解 CEO 想什么"更管用的是"理解 CEO 怎么想" <button class="pd-ts" data-t="29:44" data-who="嘉宾" data-en="And so if the way that you understand what's going on at the company is from reading a document that was written six months ago, you're going to be working from outdated information and you're not going to be able to think and respond to new things that happen." aria-label="回原文"></button>。Hilary 每周尽量给团队发一个 Slack 简报：这周最重要的对话是什么，那个人原话说了什么，她的解读是什么，她认为这个人为什么这么说，以及因此她会有什么不同的做法 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="30:54" data-who="嘉宾" data-en="And I try to make an effort every week. I don't always do it, but I try to send my team just a quick rundown in Slack of, &quot;Here are the most important conversations or the most interesting conversations I had this week." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

不长，有时候没时间就在团队会上过一遍本周笔记，边看边解说 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="31:22" data-who="嘉宾" data-en="And these aren't long. And sometimes if I don't have time, I'll just, in a team meeting, I'll literally just go through my notes from the week and sort of voiceover stuff and editorialize it as I go." aria-label="回原文"></button>。时间长了，团队对"每个人怎么思考"就有了感觉，不用事事来请示。

例子：Whoop 的 CEO Will 在设计评审里反复说"这感觉不像未来，我们构建的每样东西都需要感觉像未来" <button class="pd-ts" data-t="33:18" data-who="嘉宾" data-en="We often get feedback from him that's like, &quot;This doesn't feel like the future and everything that we're building needs to feel like the future.&quot; A lot of people hear that and they're kind of like, &quot;Oh gosh, we're never going to get this thing done on time." aria-label="回原文"></button>。很多人听到这话的解读是"完了，他又要把范围搞大" <button class="pd-ts" data-t="33:31" data-who="嘉宾" data-en="A lot of people hear that and they're kind of like, &quot;Oh gosh, we're never going to get this thing done on time. We can't make any sort of sacrifices to scope or anything like that.&quot;" aria-label="回原文"></button>。

Hilary 的解读不一样：Will 真正在意的是找到那些高影响但低成本的小触点——比如解释 VO2 Max（心血管健康指标）时，不是用静态文字，而是把用户自己的数据拉进解释过程里，用 AI 教练做对话式呈现，让用户感觉"这个产品比我的医生和教练都更了解我" <button class="pd-ts" data-t="34:02" data-who="嘉宾" data-en="It's like how you pull in. If you're explaining a concept like VO2 Max, which is a measure of your cardiovascular health, you can explain that to people with static content or you can explain that to people by bringing their data into the method of explanation that you're using." aria-label="回原文"></button>。不是把范围翻一百倍，而是在成本和影响的矩阵上找到那些"小而神奇"的点 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="35:32" data-who="嘉宾" data-en="And I don't want you all to think that this just means we have to just throw AI at everything and we have to just throw maximum scope at everything. I think the key is understanding on the matrix of cost and effort for impact, what are those high impact but low cost ways that we can just find and sprinkle through the experience and really try to make that magical?&quot;" aria-label="回原文"></button>。她把这个解读带回团队，团队就知道该怎么动作了，而不是在"老板又要加范围"的恐惧里瘫痪 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="35:49" data-who="嘉宾" data-en="I think the key is understanding on the matrix of cost and effort for impact, what are those high impact but low cost ways that we can just find and sprinkle through the experience and really try to make that magical?&quot;" aria-label="回原文"></button>。

## "神奇问题"：用陈述句掏出别人的心智模型

想了解一个人怎么思考，别问开放式问题——"你觉得我们应该怎么做？"——很多人自己都不清楚自己怎么想的 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="45:03" data-who="嘉宾" data-en="It makes me think about a lot of people, it could come across as it's a weird manipulative way of asking someone stuff, but it turns out that we're not good at really knowing what we think or know a lot of times." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

Hilary 的方法是"神奇问题"：**把你的判断写成陈述句，然后问"你同意吗？"或"那是对的吗？

"** <button class="pd-ts" data-t="41:56" data-who="嘉宾" data-en="I can't help but ask more about that. I talk about what I like to call the magic questions, but the thing about magic questions is they're not actually questions, they're statements and they end with, &quot;Do you agree?&quot;" aria-label="回原文"></button>。把事实摆到对方面前，看他对你说的什么点头、对什么摇头，你就在引出他的[[心智模型|心智模型]]，而不是让他从头解释 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="42:06" data-who="嘉宾" data-en="Or &quot;Is that right?&quot; And so I have found this, the most helpful way for kind of trying to understand a person's mental model is to just put facts in front of them and see what they say no to and what they say yes to." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

她最早是在和法务、合规团队合作时学会这招的。受监管领域很多规则是"看情况" <button class="pd-ts" data-t="43:15" data-who="嘉宾" data-en="And they'd be like, &quot;Oh, well, it depends. It depends, it depends.&quot; And so I learned that if I kind of flip that and approached that, &quot;Well, what if we did X, Y, and Z?" aria-label="回原文"></button>，问"规则是什么"得不到直截了当的答案。

但她换成"如果我们这么做，文案这么写，行不行？"——对方说行或不行，再追问为什么，心智模型就出来了 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="43:16" data-who="嘉宾" data-en="It depends, it depends.&quot; And so I learned that if I kind of flip that and approached that, &quot;Well, what if we did X, Y, and Z? What if this is what it looked like?" aria-label="回原文"></button>。

她也要求团队对她用这招。有人来问"你觉得我该怎么办"，她让对方改成"告诉我你觉得你该怎么办，然后问我同不同意" <button class="pd-ts" data-t="43:43" data-who="嘉宾" data-en="Or &quot;What could I have done differently?&quot; I'll say, &quot;Rephrase that as 'Tell me what you think you could have done differently.' And then ask me if I agree.&quot;" aria-label="回原文"></button>。两个好处：一是逼对方先形成自己的判断，然后校准跟她的差距，比直接要答案学得快得多 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="43:55" data-who="嘉宾" data-en="And when I do this, I think it has a few benefits. One, it helps them kind of calibrate their judgment over time. So they're actually forcing themselves to make this assertion." aria-label="回原文"></button>；二是不会变成什么都来问她的依赖关系 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="44:11" data-who="嘉宾" data-en="And then they're kind of calibrating how close that was to how I would think about it, which will get you much faster, much further than just asking open-ended questions and getting the answer." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

前提是带着好奇心和谦逊去用，不是逼别人说"对"——如果目标是得到"同意"，那就变味了 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="45:36" data-who="嘉宾" data-en="If your goal is to get to yes, that's not what I'm talking about. If your goal is to understand and you are coming from a, &quot;Help me understand how I'm wrong, help me understand what I'm getting wrong here&quot; and approach it with that sort of curiosity and humility and make sure that you're caring yourself and presenting yourself to this person in a way that shows that, that you're not coming in hostile or forceful or something." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

## 你不是主角——这反而是好事

Hilary 在 Big Health 开始直接向 CEO 汇报时，一位前 Coinbase 首席产品官给她当了教练，说了一句话彻底改变了她的认知：**很多人以为 CPO 的工作是把脑子里的东西拿出来、影响 CEO 让它发生——如果你带着这个意图进角色，你会失败。你的工作是理解 CEO 的愿景和他怎么思考，然后想办法把它以最好的产品形态运作出来** <button class="pd-ts" data-t="50:13" data-who="嘉宾" data-en="At my last company when I started reporting to the CEO, they found various coaches for me to work with, and one of them was the former chief product officer at Coinbase who's gone to found Bridge, which is just that apart by Stripe for a ton of money." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

这跟读小说是一个道理：你从小觉得自己是主角，你可以在自己的人生里当主角，但在公司的故事里，你大概率不是主角 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="51:07" data-who="嘉宾" data-en="Again, to go back to sort of the fiction example, you kind of come up thinking you're the protagonist. And you can be the protagonist in your life. ## 用行为心理学推习惯，别用"教育模式"推

## 本集带走

- **挨了 punch，别解释，反制叙事**：问自己"我害怕他怎么看我"，然后做一件最小的具体行动来证明相反的那一面。五分钟就够了。
- **行为激活先于感觉好转**："等我感觉好点了再行动"是抑郁思维陷阱；正确顺序是先行动，然后感觉才会变。给自己列一个情绪下行时能做的最小行动清单。
- **给团队建心智模型，不是给答案**：每周分享"这个人说了什么→我怎么解读→他为什么这么想→我因此怎么做"。团队有了模型就不用事事请示。
- **用陈述句掏别人的思考方式**：别问"你觉得呢"，把你判断写成陈述句问"你同意吗"。对方对什么点头、对什么摇头，就是他的心智模型。
- **你不是公司故事的主角，但这不是摆烂**：先做"如果我错了"练习，理解对方视角的逻辑；然后在该执行的地方全力执行，在微观决策的数百万个缝隙里发挥你独有的影响力。
- **推习惯用行为心理学，别用教育模式**：一致性（每天做）+ 低摩擦（从跟工作无关的简单场景开始）+ 奖励循环（强大、即时、有情绪冲击力）。刻意把表扬给到你希望变成习惯的行为，而不是"加班赶工"这种反模式。
- **用 AI 压缩学习循环**：关键不是 AI 能替你干活，是它能让你获得"练习次数"的速度和数量都根本性地提升。把工作中偶尔才碰到一次的判断场景，变成可以无限次练的工具。
