一款交友应用，[[北极星指标|北极星指标]]竟然是「让你离开」——用户越快找到成功、卸载应用，就越成功。说这话的人是 [[Ben Celebicic|Ben Celebicic]]，交友应用 [[Hinge|Hinge]] 的首席产品与技术官（CPTO），他在 Hinge 干了 11 年、从 2015 年重新上线就在，目前管着约 300 人的团队。Hinge 明年要冲 10 亿美元收入，而他对 AI 的态度，可能是你听过的最保守的 C 级高管观点之一。

## 一个人身上的 CPO-CTO 张力

两年半前 Hinge 的 CPO 离开时，是 COO 提议让时任 CTO 的 Ben 兼任产品，创始人兼 CEO Justin 拍板：「你跟我这么多年了，一直在这些会议室里，试试看。」Ben 坦言一开始非常难，但他说 CPO 与 CTO 之间的张力本身是健康的：CPO 想专注产品创新、尽快构建；CTO 想保证质量、偿还技术债、为未来打基础设施。

现在他要在自己脑子里打这场仗，靠的是一支有观点、会跟他辩论的团队——**当围绕资源分配和优先级没有任何辩论时，我脑子里就亮起一盏灯，说明有什么地方不对劲**。组织结构上，他每年定策略时都会重新审视：组织结构是愿景和策略的反映，反方向行不通 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="04:40" data-who="Ben Celebicic" data-en="Yeah, 100%. For me, org structure heavily depends on the problem you're trying to solve and the strategy you have in place. Org structure is always a reflection of those things, and it doesn't really work in the opposite direction. What you want is a very clear vision of what you're trying to achieve, a clear mission for the company, and a strategy that helps you move toward that vision and mission, and then you put a team in place to help you achieve it. So every year, as we build our strategy for the following year, we really look at the org, how it's organized, and what makes sense, and adjust from there. From this tension perspective, having leaders on engineering, AI, and data, and also leaders on product, design, and research in the room when we have these discussions, is tremendously helpful. When there's no debate around resourcing, around prioritization, whether we spend time on more product work or more tech work, that lights a bulb in my head that something is wrong. So I really prompt the team to think deeper about it. You have to have a team underneath you that has perspective and pushes you, and makes sure nothing falls through the cracks, especially now in the age of AI, where we're all still trying to figure out what technology is going to enable in the long run. Having infrastructure built and ready, once we've decided what makes sense to build, is tremendously important, so having people pushing to spend time building infrastructure and preparing for the future is very necessary. On the other hand, we also have to do good by our users and build features and improvements that get them to achieve success on our platform. So, that tension. ## 「为被删除而设计」与神圣的免费体验

行业早期解决的是「接触」问题——让你认识现实中遇不到的人。但这带来了新问题：选择太多，选择悖论让人难以推进。

所以 Hinge 特意只专注一件事：人们离开 Hinge 之后实际发生了什么。北极星指标是「优质约会」，他们竭尽所能把用户推离平台、回到现实生活。Ben 说正是这种违反直觉的优化逼出了创新——以前没人这么做过 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="07:03" data-who="Ben Celebicic" data-en="Yeah, that's a very interesting question. In the very beginning, the industry tried to solve for access. It was born to get you in contact and connect you with people you otherwise wouldn't meet in real life. And that problem was solved. But the problem created by solving that problem is the problem of access: all of a sudden you have access to too many people. You have this paradox of choice, and it's really hard to make progress when you always think there's something else out there you could look through. So Hinge purposely decided to concentrate on one thing, which is what actually happens when people leave Hinge. We measure our success, and our North Star is great dates. We don't really try to keep you engaged with the platform. We do everything we can to push people off the platform and get them to spend time in real life. When you optimize for something like that, that's where innovation is born, because it makes you think hard. It's counterintuitive that we're trying to get rid of people. Hinge is known as the app that's designed to be deleted. We really want people to come to us, find success, and leave. And when you optimize like that, which hasn't been done before, you really have to think outside the box and innovate quite a bit. The Free Experience Is Sacred" aria-label="回原文"></button>。

变现上只有两条原则：**免费体验是神圣的**，要让每个人靠免费功能就能获得成功；**只对无法免费送出去的东西收费**——要么会搞乱生态系统，要么靠稀缺性才有价值。Boost 和 Rose（帮你在人群中脱颖而出）如果人人免费，就变得毫无意义。他把 Hinge 比作一场很棒的派对：人越多派对越好，所以入场（接触所有人）绝不收费；订阅只是打破限制、让你更快成功 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="08:43" data-who="Ben Celebicic" data-en="Yeah, it's a great question. Most people on Hinge don't actually pay. I think probably less than 15% of users on Hinge pay for something. Our core principle from a monetization perspective is that the free experience is sacred. You want the free experience built so that everyone can achieve success, and then you charge for things that either break constraints or help you achieve success a little bit faster. The second principle we have is that we only charge for things we cannot give away for free, because if we gave them away for free, they would either mess up the ecosystem, and I'll explain what that means, or they would mean nothing, in cases where scarcity is actually a value you provide. So if you think about things like Boost on Hinge, or Rose, which helps you stand out in the crowd, those things would mean nothing if we gave them away for free to everyone. On the other hand, for dating apps to work well, think about it like a great party. You're at this great party, everyone is excited to be there, everyone wants to speak with other people, and the more people you have in the room, the better the party is. So providing access to this party comes down to not charging for it. And then if you want to break some constraints, if you want two people to go and talk with each other separately, or you want a leg up in how quickly you achieve success, those constraints can be broken through a subscription, or maybe by paying for some other things you can stand out with. So our monetization model is very simple: we only charge for things we otherwise cannot give away for free, and our free product is sacred. We want this great party to continue going. Personalizing for Every Kind of Dater" aria-label="回原文"></button>。

Hinge 上付费用户不到 15%。所有新功能都要过一道筛子：**如果做的东西不能提高两个人离开 Hinge 在现实中见面的几率，就不发布**。这也是产品保持简洁的原因 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="10:42" data-who="Ben Celebicic" data-en="Yeah, you can look at it from that perspective. You can also look at it from a perspective of, &quot;I'm brand new to dating and I need some help figuring things out,&quot; or &quot;I'm fresh out of a divorce and maybe I need a slightly different type of dating experience.&quot; The way Hinge operates is we always look at the dating culture. We have a team we call culture and consumer insights, who are constantly out there talking with daters, understanding their problems, understanding where the needs are, whether those are brand-new daters coming onto the scene now, or people who've been using dating apps for a while and not finding success, and deeply understanding what problems we need to solve for them. Once we align on what problems we need to solve, we form hypotheses that we use to provide solutions. The key thing to mention here is that, as I said, Hinge is really measured through getting people off the app. So if anything we build does not increase the chance of two people leaving Hinge and meeting in real life, we don't ship it. Everything goes through that lens. We prioritize all the work and all the problems we hear from people through the lens of, &quot;Is this increasing the chance of the vast majority of people meeting in real life or not?&quot; And that's also what helps us keep the product very simple and very clean." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

## AI 带来的个性化，与占三分之一的信任与安全

Ben 说真正的[[个性化|个性化]]是最近才可能的：两三年前，针对个人需求（约会新手 vs 刚离婚的人）做一对一体验几乎不可能。现在 AI 能根据行为模式识别每个用户的「下一步最佳行动」，比如新手在填资料时推他想得更深一点 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="12:27" data-who="Ben Celebicic" data-en="That's a great question. That is what AI is enabling us to do recently, that wasn't really possible before. If you look two or three years ago, it wasn't really possible to provide a personalized experience to everyone. You might have been able to provide an experience that fit one or two different needs, but an experience truly personalized for individual needs, and those can be many, was really impossible to do without AI. So if you think about what AI can do, it can really quickly recognize, based on behavioral patterns and how people behave on the product, what the next best step for them is, and help them achieve that. Think about how, as you create your profile, you might be really quick about selecting your photos or choosing prompts to answer about yourself, and you might be really good at it. Or, if you're new to dating, Hinge might push you to think a little bit deeper, or go a little deeper as you answer things about yourself. And we recognize that through how you answer questions on the profile. As I said, that was really not possible before generative AI. The Dark Side of AI, and Trust and Safety" aria-label="回原文"></button>。

AI 的黑暗面——假图片、伪造对话骗约会——则由[[信任与安全|信任与安全]]团队对付。这个团队归 Ben 管，**占整个 Hinge 组织的三分之一**。

但他强调：这些工作长期以来一直靠 AI 完成，用的不是生成式 AI，而是简单的传统机器学习模型。而且因为 Hinge 的目标是让用户线下见面，伪装毫无意义——你展现的样子和见面时不一样，就不可能成功，所以平台上反而能看到人们呈现真实的自己 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="13:51" data-who="Ben Celebicic" data-en="Yeah, that's a really great question. First of all, trust and safety lives under me, and it's a third of the entire Hinge organization. We deeply care about trust and safety of users, and we spend a lot of effort and resources finding fake users or bad actors on the platform. All of that has been done through AI for ages. It's not generative AI. The recent developments in AI have been game-changing in some spaces, but what's been game-changing here is AI generally, and simple, traditional machine-learning models that we've used forever. Now, if you think about people coming onto our platform, and the purpose of Hinge being to get them off the platform, you really want to make sure you're as authentic as you can be as a user, so that when you meet the other person in real life, you show up as they expect you to. You're not going to achieve success if you show up as something completely different. So what we're really seeing is people presenting themselves in their true and authentic light. Otherwise, success is almost impossible to achieve. The AI Narrative in the Public Markets" aria-label="回原文"></button>。

关于 [[Match Group|Match Group]] 年初至今涨约 25%、跑赢大多数下跌的 SaaS 公司，Ben 谢绝替母公司发言，只说 Hinge：过去十年保持同比两位数增长，秘诀简单——执着地跟用户对话、理解核心需求、盯着下一代约会者，业务就会拥有越转越好的飞轮 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="15:24" data-who="Ben Celebicic" data-en="Yeah, it's probably hard for me to speak generally about Match Group and what they're doing. I can speak specifically about Hinge. Hinge is on the mission to achieve $1 billion in revenue in 2027, next year, and we're well on the way to doing so. We've more or less grown double digits year over year over the last ten years or so. I think it's very much tied to our obsession with really talking with users, understanding their core needs and core problems, looking at the next generation of daters all the time, and building features and products that solve their core needs. That's the very simple formula for success: if you're obsessive about constantly thinking about your users and solving their problems, your business is going to have this flywheel of getting better and better. That's from a Hinge perspective. I can't really speak more broadly for Match Group, because it includes Tinder and many other apps under the same umbrella." aria-label="回原文"></button>。定位上，Match Group 伞下约 45 个品牌各有泳道（还有面向特定人群的 BLK 等），Hinge 的赛道是「有明确意图的联结」；而且用户本来就同时用多个交友应用（像同时用 Netflix 和 Hulu），这反而对他们有利 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="16:56" data-who="Ben Celebicic" data-en="Yeah, if you look at most of those 45 dating products, some of them are very specialized. You have products specialized for maybe an older generation of users, or for people looking for Black love, like BLK, or other specialized offerings. So there are multiple specialized products, and then among the big ones you have Tinder and Hinge. I would describe Hinge as a product that is more intentional. People coming to us are looking for really intentional connections, and getting off the app as quickly as possible. So our positioning is different from any of these other dating apps, and therefore we have our lane to play in. Other apps have their positioning. Of course, it's impossible to achieve perfectly clear swim lanes; everyone is going to play in each other's a little. But it's interesting to understand that this is a multi-use product category. ## AI 实际改变了什么：反馈循环，不是神话

Ben 眼中 AI 在 Hinge 的最大价值是**[[反馈循环|反馈循环]]**：他不需要任何工程师支持，就能把一个想法做成接近最终产品的原型——真的能调 API、连后端——放到用户手里，很快获得「值不值得做」的信念。其次是 PM 可以对想做的功能 vibe-code（用自然语言让 AI 生成代码）出可视化原型，代替 20 页的[[产品规格文档|产品规格文档]]。

用户研究也更容易触达更多人。还有移动端：iOS 上写完功能，直接让工具再生成 Android 版，一个人干了两个人的活 <button class="pd-ts" data-t="19:14" data-who="Ben Celebicic" data-en="For real, for real. So when I think about AI, whether I'm the CPO or CTO, I talk with individuals in New York, and we're all really asking the same question: how is this actually helping us achieve success? Everyone is still questioning the value. What I can tell you at Hinge specifically is that the biggest value I see is the feedback loop: how quickly I can learn whether something is valuable to invest in or not. What does that actually mean on the ground? I can, without any engineering support, prototype an idea that's as close as possible to the final product. It really works with the APIs, it connects to our backend, and I can put it in the hands of users and really gain conviction that this is valuable to build and put in the product, or that this is not worth pursuing and leave it behind. So this feedback loop of how quickly I learn whether something is worth building is real, and I learn much, much quicker. That's one. The second thing is that product managers can now vibe-code a quick visualization of something they're trying to create. So instead of having a 20-page product spec describing what a feature is supposed to do, me as a product manager can say, &quot;Okay, please use our design system and build something I can actually put in front of people,&quot; and show them what I'm thinking about without them having to read the whole spec to understand the idea. That kind of thing is much, much easier. Doing research, user research, has also become a little bit easier. Through some AI tools out there, you can reach more people, and you can do more user research, do more interviews with users. And then the last piece: we're starting to slowly move toward what I'd describe as mobile engineering. Hinge is a native product, so you have iOS and Android built separately, natively. Some people on the ground are picking up these tools and already starting to say, &quot;Look, I'm building this feature on iOS. Why don't I just tell the tool to build it for me on Android?&quot; And all of a sudden, I've done the work of two people at once. So we're already seeing those kinds of things happening on the ground, in multiple pockets. We see a lot of improvements in how these AI tools are making our lives easier. But if I really look holistically, it does not translate to what the narrative in the media out there probably is. We are moving faster, we're learning faster, we have more tools at our disposal, but I believe we still haven't achieved the full potential. So there are probably a couple of years left before we really start to see the true benefits of how quickly we move, how much better we build, how high-quality the products we build are, and things like that. Where Ben Draws the Line on Vibe Coding" aria-label="回原文"></button>。

但他泼了冷水：**从整体上看，实际效果并没有媒体叙事那么夸张**。他们确实更快、学得更快，但离 AI 的全部潜力还有几年。

## 他划的线：构建环节只归工程师

面对「人人都是构建者、PM 直接上生产」的行业浪潮，Ben 选择了保守路线——不是为了保守而保守，而是他要看到证据，证明 PM 或设计师能构建长期可扩展、可维护的技术。**线划在构建环节**：工程师仍然负责构建产品、设计架构、定质量标准；PM 和设计师可以用 AI 做原型、验证想法，但不为代码库做贡献。主持人说他是第一个公开这么讲的 CPTO <button class="pd-ts" data-t="22:41" data-who="Ben Celebicic" data-en="I'm taking a little bit more conservative approach to this personally, and not because I come from an engineering background, but because I really want to see the proof that product managers or designers can actually build technology that's going to be scalable and maintainable in the long run. In order for me to maintain that, I draw the line at the build. So for us, engineers are still building the product, and there are still those who design the architecture around it and set up standards for the quality of our product. I haven't really made that leap of having product managers or designers actually contribute to the codebase. They do use these tools, as I said before, to create prototypes, to learn quicker whether these things are worth pursuing, to convince others by visualizing their ideas. So we're learning much, much quicker, but we're not in a place where non-engineers are building products." aria-label="回原文"></button>。

(function(){
  function fmt(s){
    if(!isFinite(s)||s<0) s=0;
    var m=Math.floor(s/60), x=Math.floor(s%60);

        bar=box.querySelector('.bar'), fill=box.querySelector('.bar > i'),

      var cur=a.currentTime||0;
